# Advent of Hogwarts: Story Planning Document

## General Task Description

Create 24 self-contained short stories for an advent calendar spanning December 1-24, 2025. Each story should be 5-10 minutes reading time (approximately 1,200-2,400 words). The stories follow a consistent group of original student characters through their fourth year at Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry, exploring the magical everyday life of being a student.

The stories should be heartwarming, uplifting, and appropriate for young adult readers. They must be self-contained narratives while building a cohesive seasonal arc across December, showcasing friendship, personal growth, and the wonder of magic during the festive season.

## Main Characters

### Mira Patel (she/her)
- **House:** Ravenclaw
- **Year:** Fourth Year
- **Background:** British-Indian witch from London. Muggle-born, though her grandmother always claimed there was "something special" in their family history.
- **Personality:** Deeply curious, methodical, and introspective. Loves researching magical theory and history. Sometimes overthinks things but has a warm heart. Struggles with perfectionism.
- **Interests:** Ancient Runes, Arithmancy, magical creature rights
- **Visual:** Dark brown eyes, long black hair usually in a braid, often wearing reading glasses (even though magic could fix her vision—she likes them)

### Finn MacLeod (he/him)
- **House:** Hufflepuff
- **Year:** Fourth Year
- **Background:** Half-blood wizard from the Scottish Highlands. His dad is a wizard who runs a small wandmaking business; his mum is a Muggle herbalist. Gay, though he's still figuring out how to talk about it openly.
- **Personality:** Loyal, dependable, gentle sense of humor. The friend everyone turns to in a crisis. Patient and kind, but can be stubborn when defending his friends. Excellent listener. Sometimes worries about being accepted for who he is.
- **Interests:** Herbology, Care of Magical Creatures, cooking (sneaks into the kitchens regularly)
- **Visual:** Freckled, reddish-brown hair, stocky build, almost always has dirt under his fingernails from greenhouse work

### Zara Okonkwo (she/her)
- **House:** Gryffindor
- **Year:** Fourth Year
- **Background:** British-Nigerian witch from Manchester. Pure-blood family with strong ties to both British and West African magical traditions.
- **Personality:** Bold, passionate, natural leader. Speaks her mind and stands up for what's right. Can be impulsive but has strong intuition. Fiercely protective of her friends.
- **Interests:** Quidditch (Chaser), Defense Against the Dark Arts, magical history of different cultures
- **Visual:** Athletic build, box braids often tied back, confident posture, usually wearing her house scarf even indoors

### Elliot Novak (they/them)
- **House:** Slytherin
- **Year:** Fourth Year
- **Background:** Half-blood wizard from Prague, moved to Britain for Hogwarts. Their mother is a renowned potion master; their father is a Muggle architect.
- **Personality:** Clever, ambitious, dry wit. More sensitive than they let on. Strategic thinker who plans ahead. Struggles with others' expectations vs. their own desires. Loyal once you earn their trust.
- **Interests:** Potions, Transfiguration, wizard chess, sketching
- **Visual:** Tall and lanky, light brown hair in an undercut, grey eyes, always wears at least one interesting piece of jewelry (usually silver rings)

### Supporting Characters

**Professor Willow Thorne** - New Charms professor (replacing Flitwick who retired), enthusiastic, in her 30s, encourages creative magic

**Biscuit** - A persistent Niffler who lives in the grounds and frequently "borrows" shiny objects

**Madame Oakwood** - Stern but caring librarian who succeeds Madame Pince

**The Grey Lady (Helena Ravenclaw)** - Occasionally offers cryptic advice to Mira

**Peeves** - Still causing mischief, but the four friends have learned to redirect his chaos

## Thematic Guidance

### Core Themes
- **Friendship across houses:** Breaking down traditional house rivalries, found family
- **Identity and self-discovery:** Understanding who you are vs. who others expect you to be
- **Diversity and inclusion:** Natural representation of different backgrounds, cultures, and identities (including LGBTQ+ themes where appropriate)
- **Wonder of everyday magic:** Finding joy in the ordinary magical moments
- **Growth and learning:** Making mistakes, learning from them, supporting each other
- **Tradition and change:** Honoring the past while embracing the future

### Tone Guidelines
- Warm, hopeful, and gentle
- Humor should be character-based and kind (no mean-spirited jokes)
- Conflicts should be age-appropriate: social challenges, academic struggles, minor magical mishaps
- NO violence, threats, or sexual content
- Magic should feel wondrous but also like a natural part of daily life
- Seasonal/winter atmosphere throughout December

### Cultural Sensitivity
- Represent characters' cultural backgrounds authentically but don't make every story about their heritage
- LGBTQ+ themes should be normalized, not sensationalized
- Different magical traditions should be treated with equal respect
- Characters' identities are part of who they are, not their entire personality

## Story Synopses

### Story 1 (2025-12-01): "First Frost"
Mira wakes on December 1st to find intricate frost patterns on her dormitory window that shimmer with an unusual magical quality. As a student of Ancient Runes, she recognizes the patterns as runic sequences but can't decipher their meaning. She brings her friends to investigate, and together they discover this is a rare magical phenomenon that occurs only on the first day of December when conditions are perfect. Through research in the library and consultation with Professor Babbling, they learn the frost carries a message left by a Ravenclaw student from the 1970s—a reminder to "seek wonder in small things" during the busy term. The discovery sets a magical tone for the month ahead and reminds Mira to balance her academic intensity with appreciation for unexpected beauty.

### Story 2 (2025-12-02): "The Niffler's Hoard"
While working in the greenhouses, Finn hears distressed squeaking and discovers Biscuit, a resident Niffler, stuck in a drain pipe near the castle's foundations. After freeing him with help from Zara and Elliot, Biscuit seems determined to lead them somewhere. The friends follow the creature through hidden passages to a forgotten storage room where Biscuit has been collecting "treasures"—lost items from throughout the castle's history. Among the collection of buttons, coins, and trinkets, they find a silver locket that Madame Oakwood lost when she was a student decades ago. When they return it, she's moved to tears and shares stories of her own Hogwarts days. The experience teaches the group that what seems like hoarding to one creature is actually a form of connection—preserving pieces of people's stories.

### Story 3 (2025-12-03): "Quidditch in the Snow"
Zara has been preparing for weeks for her first match as Gryffindor's Quidditch captain—against Slytherin, naturally. An unexpected snowstorm rolls in just as the match begins, reducing visibility and making the Quaffle nearly impossible to see. Zara's carefully planned strategies fall apart, and she feels the weight of leadership crushing her. When she spots Elliot in the Slytherin stands cheering for their own team but also giving her an encouraging nod, she realizes leadership isn't about executing a perfect plan—it's about adapting and trusting her teammates to do the same. She calls an audible mid-game, and the team responds beautifully. Gryffindor wins, but more importantly, Zara learns that showing vulnerability and trusting others is its own kind of strength.

### Story 4 (2025-12-04): "The Potion of Reflection"
In Advanced Potions, Professor Slughorn assigns the Veritaserum Cordis—a potion that doesn't make you tell the truth to others, but forces you to be honest with yourself. It's notoriously difficult because it fails if the brewer is in denial about something significant. Elliot, who excels at Potions, is frustrated when their potion turns murky instead of clear. After multiple failed attempts, Mira gently suggests that maybe the potion is working exactly as intended. Elliot finally admits what they've been avoiding: they're terrified of disappointing their accomplished mother, and they're not sure they want to be a potioneer like her. They don't know what they want at all. Once they acknowledge this truth, the potion clears to perfect transparency. The story explores the pressure of parental expectations and the courage it takes to admit uncertainty.

### Story 5 (2025-12-05): "House Elves and Hot Chocolate"
On a particularly cold evening, Finn brings his friends to the Hogwarts kitchens—a place he visits regularly and where the house elves have come to know him. They arrive during preparations for a special hot chocolate service for the whole school. The friends are welcomed warmly and help with the preparations, learning about the complex magic and care that goes into every meal. Tibby, a young house elf who's Dobby's cousin, shares stories about Dobby's work for elf rights and mentions that some elves now receive small wages if they wish. This sparks a thoughtful conversation among the friends about ethical magic, service, respect, and how change happens slowly but importantly. The story avoids being preachy while showing the friends thinking critically about the magical world's traditions and their own roles in it. The evening ends with the satisfaction of good work and warm friendship.

### Story 6 (2025-12-06): "The Dueling Club"
Professor Thorne announces the revival of the Dueling Club, emphasizing that it's about skill, control, and respect rather than aggression. Zara immediately signs up and convinces her reluctant friends to join. During practice, Elliot discovers they have a natural talent for defensive spells and tactical thinking, quickly becoming one of the best in their year. However, when they're paired against Finn in a friendly match, Elliot's competitive Slytherin nature flares up and they win decisively—then immediately feels guilty seeing Finn's discouraged face. The story explores the line between healthy competition and taking things too seriously, the different ways people process winning and losing, and how true friends can compete and still support each other. It ends with Elliot and Finn talking it through, and Elliot offering to help Finn improve his defensive casting.

### Story 7 (2025-12-07): "Star Charts and Secrets"
Mira stays late after Astronomy class, determined to perfect her star chart for the upcoming assignment. Professor Sinistra notices her in the tower long past midnight, carefully re-plotting coordinates she's already plotted three times. Instead of sending her to bed, the professor sits with her and shares tea from a conjured thermos. They talk about the difference between excellence and perfection, and Professor Sinistra reveals that she nearly burned out in her sixth year trying to be the best at everything. She tells Mira that the stars don't judge—they simply are, and there's wisdom in that acceptance. The conversation helps Mira realize that her perfectionism isn't just about achievement; it's about fear of not being enough. By the time she leaves the tower, her star chart is "good enough"—and for the first time in a while, that feels perfectly fine.

### Story 8 (2025-12-08): "The Broken Remembrall"
Finn is showing his friends his family's heirloom Remembrall—given to his great-grandmother when she started at Hogwarts—when he accidentally drops it. The glass cracks, and the smoke inside dissipates. He's devastated, knowing how much his dad treasures the family's magical history. His friends rally to help, researching repair charms in the library and consulting with Professor Flitwick. They learn that while the Remembrall can be physically repaired, the original enchantment is tied to the glass's integrity and may never work quite the same. Finn must write home to tell his parents. His dad's response is gentle and understanding, explaining that the Remembrall has been dropped before, that its value isn't in its perfection but in being passed down and used. The crack will now be part of its story—including Finn's chapter. The story subtly parallels Finn's fear about coming out to his family eventually.

### Story 9 (2025-12-09): "Care of Magical Creatures: Thestral Edition"
In Care of Magical Creatures, Hagrid introduces the class to the Thestrals that pull the school carriages. Many students can't see them, including Finn and Mira, but Elliot and Zara both can—Elliot after their grandmother's death two years ago, Zara after losing her uncle when she was younger. Some students are unnerved by the skeletal horses, but Elliot gently approaches one, stroking its neck and explaining that they're not frightening once you understand them. Hagrid has the students who can see Thestrals guide those who can't in feeding and caring for them, creating a trust exercise. Afterward, Elliot quietly shares memories of their grandmother with the group, describing how seeing the Thestrals for the first time made death feel more real but also less frightening—proof that loss doesn't make you broken, just different. The story handles grief with tenderness and age-appropriate depth.

### Story 10 (2025-12-10): "The Library After Midnight"
While researching for an Ancient Runes essay well past curfew, Mira discovers a hidden alcove in the library's oldest section. The nook contains a worn armchair and a small collection of books that don't appear in the card catalog. Opening them, she finds handwritten notes in the margins—not vandalism, but a conversation spanning decades. Students from different eras have left comments, questions, and book recommendations for whoever finds them next. Some notes are funny, some are profound, and one from 1983 helped someone through a difficult time. Mira is enchanted by this secret tradition and carefully adds her own note to one book: a recommendation and a small piece of encouragement. She shares the discovery with her friends, and they agree to keep the secret but continue the tradition. The story celebrates the continuity of Hogwarts across generations and the quiet ways students support each other across time.

### Story 11 (2025-12-11): "Transfiguration Troubles"
Professor McGonagall assigns a particularly complex transfiguration: turning a teacup into a turtle, which requires maintaining the transformation while the turtle moves. Each of the four friends struggles in different ways—Mira overthinks the theory, Finn can't visualize the intermediate steps, Zara rushes and loses focus, and Elliot gets frustrated when their usual methodical approach doesn't work. They form a study group and discover that explaining their different approaches to each other actually helps everyone. Mira's theoretical framework gives structure, Finn's intuitive feel for living things helps with the turtle aspect, Zara's boldness encourages risk-taking, and Elliot's precision refines the technique. By teaching each other, they all improve. The story celebrates different learning styles and shows how collaborative learning can be more powerful than struggling alone. It also reinforces the cross-house friendship and mutual respect among the group.

### Story 12 (2025-12-12): "The Yule Ball Announcement"
Professor McGonagall announces that Hogwarts will host a small Yule Ball for fourth years and above, reviving an old tradition. The Great Hall erupts in excited chatter, but the announcement triggers different reactions: Zara is thrilled and immediately starts planning, Mira feels socially anxious about the formality, and Finn feels a knot of worry about expectations around dates. That evening, the four friends gather in an empty classroom to talk. The conversation meanders through their feelings—excitement mixed with pressure about who to ask, what to wear, whether going with a date even matters. In this safe space, Elliot takes a breath and tells the group something they've been holding close: they're non-binary. The friends respond with love and questions asked with care. Elliot explains their pronouns and what it means to them. The conversation ends with the group affirming that they'll face the Yule Ball together, dates or not, and that being themselves is the most important thing.

### Story 13 (2025-12-13): "Hogsmeade in December"
The friends take the carriage to Hogsmeade on a snowy Saturday, the village transformed into a winter wonderland. They agree to split up for individual shopping and meet at the Three Broomsticks later. Zara bumps into Hagrid at the magical creature shop and helps him pick out a gift for his new Kneazle. Finn nervously browses Gladrags Wizardwear, trying to find something thoughtful for Oliver, a Hufflepuff boy he's had feelings for—settling on warm dragon-hide gloves after much deliberation. Mira loses herself in Tomes and Scrolls, finding a beautiful edition of constellation myths. Elliot discovers a tiny antique shop down a side street and finds a simple silver pendant that feels right for expressing their identity. The story is told in vignettes, each friend having their own small adventure before reuniting over butterbeer, arms full of packages and hearts full of the warmth of friendship and anticipation for the season ahead.

### Story 14 (2025-12-14): "The Singing Suit of Armor"
Peeves has enchanted a suit of armor on the fourth floor to sing personalized embarrassing songs about every student who passes by. Most students are mortified or furious, which of course delights Peeves even more. When the friends pass and get their own embarrassing verses, Zara initially wants to hex the armor, but Mira has a different idea. Instead of fighting Peeves, what if they redirect him? They challenge Peeves: if he's so good at songs, can he do festive ones instead? Peeves, intrigued by the challenge and the attention, agrees—but only if students will actually listen. The friends start singing along to the armor's new carol versions, and soon other students join in. What started as harassment becomes an impromptu schoolwide sing-along in the corridor. Even Peeves seems pleased. The story shows creative problem-solving and how changing the frame of a situation can transform it entirely.

### Story 15 (2025-12-15): "Greenhouse Seven"
Finn has been helping Professor Sprout with Greenhouse Seven, which contains the rarest and most dangerous plants at Hogwarts. He gets permission to give his friends a tour after hours. They're awed by the exotic specimens—singing orchids, a baby Whomping Willow in training, self-pruning roses. But when Finn reaches up to check on a Venomous Tentacula's watering system, he accidentally triggers its defensive response. The plant breaks free of its bindings and its vines start lashing dangerously. The friends must work together: Zara's quick reflexes and shielding charms protect them, Mira remembers a botanical fact about the plant's weakness to cold, Elliot's precise cutting hex severs the right vines, and Finn's knowledge lets him soothe the plant once contained. Professor Sprout arrives at the commotion but, seeing they've handled it, is impressed rather than angry. The story showcases each character's strengths working in concert and Finn's expertise in his passion.

### Story 16 (2025-12-16): "Charms and Chances"
Finn has been carrying his feelings for Oliver alone, but the Yule Ball is approaching and he's running out of time. He finds Zara after Quidditch practice and, voice shaking, tells her he's gay and that he likes Oliver but doesn't know what to do. Zara's response is immediate and fierce: she hugs him, tells him she's honored he trusted her, and that Oliver would be lucky to go with him. She helps him think through how to ask, offering to be nearby for moral support. Meanwhile, in Charms class, Professor Thorne pairs Mira with someone unexpected—a quiet Slytherin named Priya who Mira has barely spoken to. Working together on a complex charm, they discover shared interests in magical theory and similar anxieties about perfectionism. The story parallels two kinds of connection: the deep trust of confiding in a friend, and the pleasant surprise of finding kinship with someone new.

### Story 17 (2025-12-17): "The Grey Lady's Gift"
Late one evening in Ravenclaw Tower, Mira sits alone reviewing notes, feeling overwhelmed by the approaching exams and the social demands of the Yule Ball. Helena Ravenclaw, the Grey Lady, drifts down from the upper floors—an unusual occurrence, as she rarely speaks to students at length. She sits (or rather, hovers) near Mira and begins to tell a story. Helena speaks of her own time at Hogwarts, when she was so consumed by learning and proving her brilliance that she neglected friendships and experiences. She sought the Diadem hoping wisdom would fill the emptiness, but it was already too late. Her tone isn't bitter but wistful and wise. She tells Mira that knowledge is valuable, but the people who share your life are irreplaceable. Before fading away, she encourages Mira to go to the ball, to be present with her friends, to live rather than just study. Mira is left with tears in her eyes and a new perspective on balance.

### Story 18 (2025-12-18): "Elliot's Sketch"
Elliot has been carrying a leather sketchbook everywhere, but always tucks it away when others approach. They've been drawing scenes from around Hogwarts and portraits of students—especially their friends—capturing quiet moments and genuine expressions. When Elliot leaves their sketchbook on a library table and Mira finds it, she can't help looking. She's stunned by the skill and care in every drawing. She shows Finn and Zara, and they're all moved by how Elliot sees them—not just their appearances but something essential about who they are. When Elliot returns and realizes they've seen, they freeze with vulnerability. But instead of teasing or dismissing, their friends are full of genuine admiration and appreciation. Zara asks if she can keep her portrait. The moment becomes about artistic vulnerability and being truly seen—parallel to Elliot's journey with gender identity. Elliot realizes that sharing their art isn't weakness; it's another way of being honest about who they are.

### Story 19 (2025-12-19): "Winter Solstice Traditions"
On the winter solstice, the friends gather in an unused classroom and decide to share their family traditions with each other. Mira talks about Diwali from earlier in the autumn but also her family's secular solstice celebration with candles representing the returning light. Zara shares her family's blend of British Christmas traditions and West African customs, including special foods and ancestral remembrance. Finn describes the Scottish Hogmanay preparations and his family's tradition of first-footing. Elliot shares Czech traditions of St. Nicholas Day and carp for Christmas dinner, plus their family's custom of making wishes on winter stars. Rather than just sharing, they decide to create their own tradition—a blend of all four backgrounds plus their Hogwarts experience. They light candles, share food they've sneaked from the kitchens, and make a promise to gather like this every year. The story celebrates cultural exchange, chosen family, and creating new traditions while honoring old ones.

### Story 20 (2025-12-20): "The Yule Ball Preparation"
The day before the Yule Ball arrives with nervous excitement. The morning starts with Finn finding Oliver in the library. With Zara pretending to study nearby for moral support, Finn asks Oliver to the ball—voice shaking but sincere. Oliver lights up and says yes, he was hoping Finn would ask. Later, the friends gather in the Ravenclaw common room to help each other prepare. There's gentle discussion about what to wear, how to present themselves, and being authentic. Elliot shares their uncertainty about formal gendered clothing, and Mira helps them find an outfit that feels right—elegant but not traditionally masculine or feminine. Zara, who learned braiding from her older sister, offers to do everyone's hair and creates beautiful styles for Mira and Elliot. Finn is a bundle of nerves and excitement. The story is warm and intimate, celebrating the care they take with each other and the courage it takes to show up as yourself.

### Story 21 (2025-12-21): "The Yule Ball"
The Great Hall is transformed into a winter wonderland—ice sculptures, floating snowflakes, silver and blue decorations, and an enchanted ceiling showing the aurora borealis. The friends arrive together before meeting their respective dates or plans. Finn and Oliver are adorable together, a bit awkward but genuinely happy, and Finn experiences the profound relief of being himself openly. Zara came without a date by choice and spends the night dancing with everyone, her joy and confidence infectious. Mira, who almost didn't come, finds herself asked to dance by Priya from Charms class, and they have a surprisingly wonderful time talking between dances. Elliot, wearing their carefully chosen outfit, receives compliments that acknowledge their style without gendering it, and feels genuinely seen. The four friends reconvene on a balcony overlooking the grounds, watching snow fall, and share a moment of quiet happiness. The story captures the magic, nervousness, joy, and deep friendship of the evening.

### Story 22 (2025-12-22): "The Twelve Magical Days"
Professor Thorne announces a special holiday tradition: a magical scavenger hunt throughout the castle with clues hidden in twelve locations representing the "twelve days of Christmas." Students can form teams of up to four, and the winning team receives a prize—and bragging rights. The four friends immediately team up. The clues require different magical skills and knowledge: deciphering runes (Mira's strength), identifying magical plants (Finn's expertise), defensive spell recognition (Zara's area), and potion ingredient identification (Elliot's specialty). They race through the castle alongside other teams, solving riddles, navigating magical obstacles, and using everything they've learned this term. It's competitive but fun, showcasing how much they've grown individually and as a team. They don't win first place—a group of sixth years does—but they come in second and have an absolute blast. The story is lighthearted, fast-paced, and celebrates learning through play.

### Story 23 (2025-12-23): "The Gift of Magic"
On December 23rd, the friends gather in their now-familiar unused classroom for their private gift exchange before the chaos of Christmas. The gifts are modest but deeply thoughtful: Mira gives each person a book with a personal inscription explaining why it made her think of them. Finn presents hand-pressed flower bookmarks using rare magical plants, each chosen specifically for what it represents. Zara has commissioned a local wandmaker to create personalized wand holders for each friend, carved with symbols meaningful to them. Elliot, hands shaking slightly, gives each person a portrait from their sketchbook—not the polished ones, but sketches that capture spontaneous moments of joy, laughter, or concentration. As they exchange gifts, they talk about how much has changed since September, how much they've shared this month alone. The story is quiet, emotional, and reflective—celebrating the depth of their friendship and how they've helped each other grow and be brave.

### Story 24 (2025-12-24): "Christmas Eve at Hogwarts"
Christmas Eve at Hogwarts is quiet as many students have gone home, but the four friends remain, along with a handful of others. The castle feels peaceful rather than empty—intimate and magical. They spend the day wandering the snow-covered grounds, having a gentle snowball fight, visiting Hagrid for tea and rock cakes. As evening falls, they attend the special Christmas Eve feast in the Great Hall, which seats perhaps thirty people at a single long table rather than the usual house tables—professors and students together. The food is spectacular, the decorations breathtaking, and there's caroling afterward. The friends eventually make their way to the Astronomy Tower and look out over the winter landscape, the castle glowing warm below them. They don't need to say much; the contentment is palpable. They're exactly where they belong—together, accepted, home. The story ends with a sense of peace, magic, and deep gratitude for found family and this extraordinary place.

## Story Format Specification

### File Format
- **Filename:** `{date}.md` where date is in `YYYY-MM-DD` format
- **Location:** `src/data/stories/` directory
- **Language:** US English spelling and grammar

### YAML Frontmatter
Each story file must begin with YAML frontmatter containing:
```yaml
---
title: "Story Title Here"
date: "YYYY-MM-DD"
---
```

Both `title` and `date` must be strings (use quotes).

### Story Structure
- **Length:** 1,200-2,400 words (5-10 minutes reading time)
- **Opening:** Hook the reader immediately with sensory details or intriguing situation
- **Middle:** Develop the conflict/challenge, show character interactions
- **Ending:** Satisfying resolution that's uplifting but not saccharine
- **Pacing:** Each story should feel complete but leave readers wanting the next one

### Writing Style
- **Perspective:** Third person limited (can shift between the four main characters, but stay with one per scene)
- **Tone:** Warm, accessible, immersive
- **Magic:** Describe magical elements with wonder but as a normal part of the world
- **Dialogue:** Natural, age-appropriate, reveals character
- **Sensory details:** Especially winter/seasonal atmosphere
- **Show, don't tell:** Especially for emotions and character growth

### Content Requirements
- Each story must be self-contained with beginning, middle, and end
- Include at least two of the four main characters per story
- Reference Hogwarts locations and elements authentically
- Maintain continuity with previous stories (characters remember events)
- Balance quieter character moments with magical plot elements
- Ensure appropriate representation appears naturally, not forced

### What to Avoid
- Excessive exposition or explaining Harry Potter lore
- Violence, threats, or dark magic
- Sexual content or inappropriate romance
- Stereotyping or tokenization of diverse characters
- Preachy or heavy-handed messages
- Endings that feel rushed or unearned
- Making every story about serious issues (balance with lighthearted adventures)
