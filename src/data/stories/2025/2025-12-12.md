Professor McGonagall's voice cut through the din of breakfast with the precision of a well-aimed spell.

"Your attention, please."

The Great Hall settled into expectant silence. It was the kind of silence that came when Professor McGonagall used her announcement voice—the one that promised something either very exciting or very serious, and sometimes both.

From her seat at the Ravenclaw table, Mira glanced over at her friends. Finn was at the Hufflepuff table, piece of toast halfway to his mouth. Zara sat at Gryffindor, already leaning forward with interest. And Elliot, at Slytherin, had that carefully neutral expression they wore when they were curious but didn't want to show it.

"As some of you may know," Professor McGonagall continued, standing at the staff table in her emerald robes, "Hogwarts once hosted a Yule Ball as part of our December festivities. This tradition has been dormant for several years, but—" she paused, and Mira could have sworn she saw the hint of a smile "—we have decided to revive it."

The Hall erupted. Not with chaos, exactly—Professor McGonagall's presence prevented that—but with excited chatter that rippled through the tables like a wave.

"The Yule Ball will take place on December 21st, the winter solstice. It will be open to fourth years and above. Dress robes are required. And yes—" she raised her voice over the increasing noise "—you may invite a guest from another house or year, so long as they are also fourth year or above. Further details will be posted in your common rooms. That is all."

She sat down, and the Hall exploded into full conversation. At the Ravenclaw table, Mira's neighbors immediately started talking over each other about what to wear, who to ask, whether there would be dancing. Mira felt a small knot form in her stomach.

A ball. With dancing. And dress robes. And dates.

She pushed her scrambled eggs around her plate and tried to look enthusiastic.

---

They met at lunch, claiming their usual spot at the end of one of the middle tables where the house boundaries got blurry.

"This is brilliant," Zara said immediately, her eyes bright with excitement. "An actual Yule Ball! Like in the stories my sister told me about the Triwizard Tournament. There'll be dancing and decorations and—oh, we need to figure out what to wear!"

"It could be fun," Finn said, but his voice was careful. Too careful. Mira recognized that tone—the one he used when he was trying to convince himself of something.

"I suppose it's traditional," Elliot said, pushing food around their plate much like Mira had been doing. "Very Hogwarts. Very... formal."

"You don't sound thrilled," Zara observed.

Elliot shrugged. "It's just a dance. People get so worked up about these things."

"But it's not just a dance," Zara protested. "It's a whole event! And we're finally old enough to go. Fourth years barely ever get included in the good stuff."

"True," Mira admitted. She tried to sound positive. "It'll be nice to dress up. And the decorations will probably be beautiful."

"See? Mira gets it." Zara grinned, then her expression turned speculative. "Now we just have to figure out the important stuff. Like who everyone's going with."

The knot in Mira's stomach tightened. Across from her, she saw Finn's shoulders tense slightly. Elliot became very interested in their pumpkin juice.

"I mean, we don't have to go with dates," Mira said quickly. "We could just... go as a group?"

"We could," Zara said. "But also, what if there's someone you want to ask? Or someone who might ask you?" She waggled her eyebrows. "This is a perfect opportunity for... you know. Opportunities."

"Very eloquent," Elliot said dryly.

"You know what I mean!"

They did. That was the problem.

The conversation moved on to speculation about what the ball would actually be like, but Mira noticed that Finn stayed quiet and Elliot's responses were more clipped than usual. Even Zara's enthusiasm seemed to dim slightly as lunch wore on.

Something was off. And that evening, as Mira found a note in her bag—_Empty classroom, third floor, 7pm? —F_—she suspected she wasn't the only one who'd noticed.

---

The classroom was one they'd used before, tucked away where most students didn't bother going. Finn had already conjured some cushions and lit a few floating candles by the time Mira arrived. He was sitting on the floor, back against a desk, looking pensive.

"Hey," she said softly.

"Hey." He smiled, but it didn't quite reach his eyes. "Zara and Elliot should be here soon. I just... I thought we should talk. Away from everyone else."

"About the ball?"

"About the ball. About—yeah. Just about how we're all feeling."

The door opened and Zara slipped in, still in her Quidditch practice gear. "Sorry, got held up. What's—oh." She took in the setup, the serious expression on Finn's face. "This is a feelings talk, isn't it?"

"If that's okay," Finn said.

"Always okay." Zara dropped onto a cushion. "Where's Elliot?"

"Right here." Elliot appeared in the doorway, closing it carefully behind them. They surveyed the scene—the cushions, the candlelight, the three friends waiting—and something in their posture softened. "This looks serious."

"Or just... safe," Finn said. "Space to talk without everyone else listening."

Elliot nodded slowly and sat down, completing their circle. For a moment, no one spoke. Outside, the December evening was dark, and the castle felt distant. It was just the four of them in the warm candlelight.

"I'm excited about the ball," Zara said finally. "I really am. But also—" she hesitated, which was unusual for her "—also I feel weird about the pressure? Like, everyone's immediately asking about dates and what to wear and who likes who. And it's fun but also exhausting?"

"Yeah," Mira said quietly. "I feel that too. Like, the ball itself sounds lovely. But all the expectations around it make me anxious. What if I'm bad at dancing? What if I don't have the right dress robes? What if someone asks me and I don't know what to say, or no one asks me and that's somehow worse?"

"It's a lot," Finn agreed. His hands were fidgeting with a loose thread on one of the cushions. "And there's all this assumed... stuff. Like, everyone's talking about asking someone or being asked. And there's this whole thing about it being romantic or whatever. And that's fine for people who want that, but what if—" He stopped, swallowed. "What if you're not sure what you want? Or you want something but don't know if you're allowed to want it?"

The raw honesty in his voice made Mira's chest ache. She reached over and squeezed his hand.

"You're allowed to want whatever you want," she said firmly.

"Are we though?" Elliot's voice was soft but clear. "Are we allowed to want things that don't fit what everyone expects?"

They all looked at Elliot. In the candlelight, their grey eyes reflected the flames, and their expression was more open than usual—vulnerable in a way that Elliot rarely let show.

"What do you mean?" Zara asked gently.

Elliot drew in a breath. Let it out. Drew in another.

"I need to tell you something," they said. "And I'm—I'm nervous about it, which is stupid because you're my best friends and I know you'll be okay with it, but I'm still nervous."

"You can tell us anything," Mira said. "You know that, right?"

"I do. I just—" Elliot ran a hand through their undercut, a gesture of agitation. "Okay. So. The Yule Ball. Everyone's talking about suits and dresses. Boys asking girls or girls asking boys or whatever. And every time someone talks about it, I feel like I'm... watching from outside. Like none of the language quite fits."

They paused. Their friends waited, patient and present.

"I've been thinking about this for a while," Elliot continued. "Actually, more than thinking. Knowing, maybe? But not having words for it. And then a few weeks ago I found some books in the library—Muggle books, mostly, about gender and identity. And I found a word that fits."

Another pause. Elliot's hands fidgeted with their silver rings.

"I'm non-binary," they said. The words came out quiet but clear. "I'm not a boy. I'm not a girl. I'm just... me. Neither. Both. Something else entirely. And my pronouns are they and them, which you've all actually been using anyway, but now you know why that matters to me."

The silence that followed wasn't empty. It was full—full of processing, of understanding clicking into place, of love adjusting its shape to fit this new-but-not-new information.

Zara spoke first. "Thank you for telling us."

"That's it?" Elliot's voice cracked slightly. "Just thank you?"

"What else would there be?" Zara leaned forward, her expression fierce with affection. "You told us something important about who you are. So thank you for trusting us with that."

"I have questions," Mira said carefully. "But I don't want to make you explain everything or defend yourself. Can I ask, or would you rather not?"

Elliot let out a breath that might have been relief. "You can ask. That's—actually that's why I wanted to tell you. So I could talk about it. So you'd know."

"Okay." Mira adjusted her glasses, thinking. "So, they/them pronouns. We've been using them already—was that because you asked people to, or did it just happen?"

"Bit of both. I started using them for myself at the start of term. Introduced myself that way to new people. Most people just went with it without questioning. Some people still use he or him because that's what they assume, and I haven't corrected them yet."

"Do you want us to correct them?" Finn asked. "If we hear someone using the wrong pronouns?"

"Maybe? If it feels natural? I don't want to make it a big dramatic thing, but also—yeah. It matters." Elliot's shoulders had relaxed slightly. "It really matters."

"Consider it done," Zara said firmly. "Anyone messes up and I'll set them straight. So to speak."

That got a small laugh from everyone, breaking some of the tension.

"Can I ask," Mira said, "when did you know? Or realize, I guess?"

Elliot considered this. "I think part of me always knew something didn't fit right. When people talked about 'boys' or 'girls' I never quite felt like either category worked. But I thought everyone felt that way, you know? That gender was just this weird thing people performed and we were all just going along with it. It wasn't until I read about non-binary identity that I realized—oh. This is actually a thing. This is who I am. It has a name."

"And non-binary means...?" Finn trailed off, clearly trying to understand.

"It means my gender isn't binary. Not male or female. Some non-binary people are somewhere in between. Some are both. Some are neither. Some are something completely outside that whole spectrum. For me, it's—" Elliot gestured vaguely "—it's like that question doesn't make sense. I'm not a point on the male-female line. I'm off the line entirely. I'm just Elliot."

"Just Elliot," Finn repeated, smiling. "I like that."

"Me too," Mira said. "And for what it's worth, this doesn't change anything about how we see you. You're still brilliant and sarcastic and absurdly talented at Potions. You're still our friend. You're still you."

"But it also changes everything," Zara added, "because now we know you better. We understand you more. And that matters."

Elliot's eyes were shiny. They blinked rapidly. "Okay, you need to stop being so perfect about this or I'm going to cry."

"You're allowed to cry," Finn said gently.

"Absolutely not. I have a reputation."

"Of what, being emotionally constipated?" Zara teased, but her voice was warm.

"Exactly. Very important for my Slytherin image."

They all laughed, and the tension dissolved further. But the sincerity remained, settling around them like a protective spell.

"So," Mira said after a moment, "back to the Yule Ball. Does this help explain why you felt weird about all the talk around it?"

"Yeah." Elliot pulled their knees up, wrapping their arms around them. "Everyone's talking about suits or dresses, and I'm here thinking—what do I wear? What fits? There's so much assumption about how you're supposed to present, and none of it feels right. Plus all the romantic stuff—everyone assuming you'll ask a boy or a girl, when maybe that's not even... I don't know. It's complicated."

"It doesn't have to be," Finn said quietly. "What if we just—all of us—decide that the ball is however we want it to be? We wear what makes us comfortable. We go with who we want or with each other or alone. We dance if we feel like it. We leave if it's overwhelming. We make it ours."

"I love that," Mira said. "The ball is ours to define."

"Together," Zara added. "We face it together. Dates or no dates, however we show up, we're there for each other."

Elliot was quiet for a moment, then nodded. "Together. Yeah. I can do that."

"And Elliot," Zara said seriously, "when we figure out what to wear, we're going to find you something that makes you feel incredible. Not boy clothes or girl clothes. Just Elliot clothes."

"Eloquent again, Zara," Mira teased.

"You all know what I mean!"

They did. And sitting there in their circle, surrounded by candlelight and cushions and the deep trust they'd built over years of friendship, Elliot felt something ease in their chest. Something that had been tight and anxious for longer than they'd realized.

"Thank you," they said quietly. "For listening. For not making it weird. For just—being exactly who you are."

"Right back at you," Finn said. "For being exactly who you are."

"Which is non-binary and brilliant," Mira added.

"And our friend," Zara finished. "Most importantly, our friend."

Elliot smiled—a real smile, soft and genuine. "Yeah. Friends."

---

They stayed in that classroom for another hour, the conversation drifting from serious to silly and back again. Finn admitted he was anxious about the ball for his own reasons—something about expectations and not being sure how to navigate them. Zara confessed that part of her excitement was performative, that underneath she was also nervous about fitting in. Mira shared her anxiety about social situations in general, about feeling like she was constantly performing adequacy.

It turned out they were all carrying different weights, different worries. But sharing them made the load lighter.

When they finally stood to leave, stretching out cramped legs and extinguishing the floating candles, there was a sense of rightness among them. They'd entered this room as four friends with secrets. They left as four friends who knew each other better, who'd chosen vulnerability and received acceptance.

"Same time next week?" Finn suggested. "Check in about ball prep and just... whatever?"

"Absolutely," Mira said.

"I'll bring snacks," Zara offered.

"I'll bring my usual sparkling personality," Elliot said dryly, but they were smiling.

They split up at the corridor junction, heading toward their different common rooms. But before they separated completely, Elliot called out.

"Hey. All of you."

They turned back.

"I love you," Elliot said simply. "You're my family. You know that, right?"

"We know," Mira said, her voice thick with emotion. "We love you too."

"So much," Zara added.

"Always," Finn finished.

And then they really did go their separate ways, each carrying the warmth of that moment like a candle flame in their chest. Elliot walked down to the Slytherin common room feeling lighter than they had in weeks. Mira climbed to Ravenclaw Tower with tears in her eyes—happy ones. Zara bounded toward Gryffindor with fierce joy. And Finn made his way to Hufflepuff thinking about friendship and courage and the many different ways people could be brave.

---

Later, in their respective dormitories, each of them thought about the Yule Ball with different feelings than they'd had that morning.

It was still slightly scary. Still came with pressures and expectations. But now it also came with solidarity. With the knowledge that whatever happened, they'd face it together. That being themselves—their actual, complicated, authentic selves—was not just allowed but celebrated.

Mira wrote in her December journal: _Today Elliot told us they're non-binary. It was one of the most important conversations we've ever had. Not because it changed who Elliot is, but because it let us see them more clearly. And in that clarity, I saw how much courage it takes to say "this is who I am" when the world wants to put you in a box. I saw how love adjusts and expands and never diminishes. And I understood that the best gift we can give each other is the space to be fully, authentically ourselves._

Elliot lay in bed staring at the ceiling of their dormitory, replaying the evening. They'd been so scared. But their friends had been perfect—asking questions but not prying, offering support but not treating them like they were fragile. Just accepting and loving and moving forward together.

Tomorrow, everyone would still be talking about the Yule Ball. There would still be pressure and expectations and awkwardness. But Elliot would face it knowing that their people—their chosen family—saw them for who they really were.

And that made all the difference.

Outside, snow continued to fall over Hogwarts. The castle settled into its nighttime rhythms. And in four different common rooms, four friends went to sleep feeling grateful for each other and the safe space they'd created—a space where truth was welcomed, vulnerability was honored, and love expanded to hold whoever you were becoming.

It was, Mira thought as she drifted off, exactly what December magic was supposed to feel like.
