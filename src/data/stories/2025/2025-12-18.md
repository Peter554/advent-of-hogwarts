The library on Thursday afternoon was unusually quiet, even by library standards. Most students had either gone down to Hogsmeade for last-minute Yule Ball preparations or were holed up in their common rooms with friends, and the reference section felt almost abandoned.

Mira found the silence soothing. After her conversation with Helena and her resolution to find better balance, she'd been more intentional about her study time—focused but not frantic, thorough but not obsessive. Right now, she was cross-referencing a passage in her Ancient Runes textbook with a much older translation, purely out of curiosity rather than assignment pressure.

She was so absorbed that she almost didn't notice the leather sketchbook sitting on the table beside her reference stack.

It wasn't hers. She'd been alone at this table for over an hour, and the book hadn't been there when she arrived. Someone must have set it down while passing by, or perhaps abandoned it by accident.

Mira glanced around. Still no one nearby. She should probably take it to Madam Oakwood's desk, let her put it in the lost and found.

But then she noticed the silver ring caught in the book's leather strap, holding it closed. A simple band with a Celtic knot pattern.

Elliot's ring. Elliot's sketchbook.

Mira picked it up, intending to set it aside safely until she saw Elliot later. But as she moved it, the strap loosened and the book fell open.

She looked down before she could stop herself.

And froze.

The page showed Finn in the greenhouse, bent over a potted plant with late afternoon sunlight streaming through the glass behind him. The drawing was done in pencil, incredibly detailed—every freckle, every strand of reddish-brown hair, the dirt under his fingernails, the gentle concentration in his expression. But it wasn't just technically skilled. It was... loving. The way the artist had captured Finn's entire essence—his patience, his gentleness, his complete absorption in caring for a living thing—in a single sketch.

Mira's hand moved to turn the page before she could think better of it.

Zara mid-laugh, head thrown back, box braids cascading over her shoulder. The line work somehow conveyed motion and joy, the confidence in her posture, the light in her eyes.

Another page. The four of them in their usual classroom hideaway, viewed from above as if the artist had been standing in the doorway. Finn was repotting a plant, Zara was practicing wand movements, and Mira herself was curled in a chair reading. The composition made them look like a constellation, separate but connected.

Another. Elliot had drawn Professor Sprout examining a Venomous Tentacula with such fierce focus that you could see her dedication radiating off the page.

And another. Mira again, this one from the frost morning at the beginning of the month. She was reaching toward the window, her face illuminated by the magical glow, and in Elliot's rendering she looked... wonder-struck. Beautiful. Like someone discovering magic for the first time.

Mira sat down heavily, the sketchbook in her hands.

She should close it. This was private. These were Elliot's personal observations, their art, their way of seeing the world. She had no right to—

But she couldn't stop looking. Page after page of careful sketches. Some were quick gesture drawings—students in the Great Hall, a cat sleeping on a windowsill, the suit of armor that Peeves had enchanted. Others were more detailed studies. Elliot had drawn Hagrid with one of the Thestrals, somehow managing to suggest the invisible creature through Hagrid's pose and expression alone.

There was a portrait of Priya from Charms class, caught in a moment of concentration as she cast a spell, magic sparking at her fingertip.

And more of the three of them—Mira's friends, her found family. Zara after a Quidditch match, sweaty and triumphant. Finn in the kitchens with the house elves, laughing at something Tibby had said. Mira in the library, glasses slipping down her nose, utterly absorbed in a book.

Each drawing was annotated with small notes in Elliot's neat handwriting. Beside the sketch of Finn: *Patience incarnate. The way he holds growing things like they're precious.* Beside Zara's portrait: *She doesn't know how bright she shines.*

And beside the drawing of Mira reaching toward the frost: *The moment she chose wonder over worry. The moment everything started to change.*

Mira felt tears prick her eyes. This was how Elliot saw them. How Elliot saw her. Not as grades or achievements or the anxiety that so often clouded her own self-perception, but as someone capable of wonder. Someone worth capturing in careful, loving detail.

"Mira?"

She jumped, nearly dropping the sketchbook. Finn stood a few feet away, carrying his own stack of books. He looked worried.

"Are you okay? You look—" He noticed what she was holding. "Is that Elliot's?"

"I found it," Mira said quickly. "I wasn't snooping, it was just here and it fell open and—" She stopped, took a breath. "Finn, you have to see these."

"I don't know if we should—"

"Please. Just look."

Finn set down his books and moved to look over her shoulder. Mira turned to the drawing of him in the greenhouse.

She heard his sharp intake of breath.

"That's..." He reached out, then stopped, hand hovering above the page. "That's really how they see me?"

"Yes," Mira said softly. "Keep looking."

She turned the pages slowly, watching Finn's face as he took in drawing after drawing. His eyes grew wet when he saw the annotations, especially the one beside his own portrait.

"Where's Elliot?" he asked. "We should—"

"Find me?"

They both turned. Zara stood at the end of the row of shelves, and behind her, looking absolutely stricken, was Elliot.

Elliot's face had gone pale. They were staring at the sketchbook in Mira's hands like it was a live snake about to strike. Their hands, usually so still and controlled, were trembling.

"I didn't mean—" Elliot's voice came out rough. "I left it. I was just. I needed a different book and I must have—" They stopped, seemed to fold in on themselves. "You weren't supposed to see that."

"Elliot," Mira started, but Zara was already moving forward.

"Can I look?" Zara asked, her voice gentle but firm. "Please?"

Elliot looked like they wanted to bolt. Like they wanted to grab the sketchbook and run and never speak of this again. But something in Zara's expression made them pause.

"I..." Elliot swallowed hard. "It's just sketches. It's not. They're not good, I was just practicing, I—"

"Elliot." Finn's voice was thick with emotion. "They're beautiful. You're incredibly talented."

"It's not about talent," Elliot said desperately. "It's—they're private. They're how I—" They stopped, looking away. "You'll think I'm weird. Drawing you without asking. Watching you all the time like some kind of—"

"Like an artist who cares about their subjects," Mira finished quietly. She stood, holding out the sketchbook. "Like someone who sees beauty in everyday moments. Who sees us—really sees us—and wants to capture it."

Zara had reached them now. She took the sketchbook from Mira's hands with reverence and began turning pages slowly. When she reached her own portrait—the one mid-laugh—she made a sound that was half-sob, half-laugh.

"This is how you see me?" she asked, looking up at Elliot with shining eyes.

Elliot's jaw was tight, defensive. "Yes. So what? You're photogenic, everyone knows that, it's not—"

"No." Zara pointed to the sketch. "This isn't just about being photogenic. You drew me like I'm... joyful. Powerful. Like I'm exactly who I want to be." She looked back at Elliot, and her voice cracked slightly. "No one's ever drawn me like this before."

"That's because that's who you are," Elliot said, the words seeming to escape against their will. "That's what I see when I look at you. All of you." They gestured helplessly at the sketchbook. "Finn with his gentleness. Mira with her curiosity. Zara with her light. I just... I wanted to remember. To hold onto moments before they disappeared."

The four of them stood there in the quiet library, Elliot looking like they might shatter, the others holding this unexpected gift of being truly seen.

"Can I keep mine?" Zara asked suddenly. "The one where I'm laughing. Can I... would you let me have it?"

Elliot stared at her. "You want it?"

"Yes. I want to remember that this is how someone I care about sees me. That this is real, not just how I try to present myself." Zara's voice was fierce now. "Your art isn't just good, Elliot. It's true. And that makes it precious."

Finn nodded eagerly. "Could I have mine too? The greenhouse one?"

"And I'd like the frost morning," Mira added softly. "If you're willing to share. But even if you're not, I want you to know—seeing these, seeing how you see us—it's one of the most beautiful gifts anyone's ever given me. Even accidentally."

Elliot's hands were still shaking. They reached up to touch the undercut at the side of their head—a nervous gesture Mira had seen a hundred times. "I don't... you're not weirded out? By me drawing you without permission?"

"Are you joking?" Finn asked. "Elliot, these are amazing. You've been hiding this incredible talent and we had no idea."

"It's not about talent," Elliot said again, but quieter now. "It's about... paying attention. About noticing things. About—" They stopped, struggling for words. "About being honest about what I see. What I feel."

"Like being honest about who you are," Mira said gently, making the connection. "About not hiding the parts of yourself you're afraid people won't understand."

Elliot's eyes met hers, and something shifted in their expression. Understanding. Recognition.

"Yeah," they whispered. "Like that."

Zara carefully tore the page with her portrait from the sketchbook—wincing slightly at the sound, but Elliot didn't stop her—and held it against her chest. "Thank you for seeing me," she said. "For drawing me. For being brave enough to show us how you see the world, even though you didn't mean to."

"I should thank you," Elliot said, voice rough. "For not... for not making this weird. For actually wanting the drawings."

"Of course we want them," Finn said, carefully removing his own portrait. "These are pieces of you. Your perspective. Your heart on paper. Why wouldn't we want that?"

Elliot's composure cracked then. They pressed their hands to their face, shoulders shaking with something between laughter and tears. "I've been so scared," they admitted. "Of sharing this. Of letting people see. Not just the art but—but all of it. Who I am. How I am. What I care about."

Mira moved forward and, slowly, carefully, pulled Elliot into a hug. After a moment, Finn joined from one side and Zara from the other, until the four of them were tangled together among the library stacks.

"You don't have to be scared with us," Mira said quietly. "Not about your art. Not about your identity. Not about any of it. We see you, Elliot. The whole you. And we're not going anywhere."

"You're stuck with us," Finn added. "Even when we accidentally discover your secret sketchbook and get emotional about it in the library."

That startled a real laugh out of Elliot. They pulled back, wiping their eyes with the back of their hand, their silver rings glinting. "You're all ridiculous."

"Says the person who's been secretly drawing us like we're subjects in a Renaissance painting," Zara pointed out.

"They're not that good—"

"They absolutely are," Mira interrupted. "Elliot, you have real talent. Have you thought about doing something with this? Showing your work, or studying art formally?"

Elliot shook their head. "My mother wants me to be a potioneer like her. Art isn't... it's not practical. It's just something I do for myself."

"But it makes you happy," Finn observed. "We can tell. Your whole face changes when you talk about it."

"That doesn't mean it's a career," Elliot said, but they sounded less certain than usual.

"Maybe not," Mira allowed. "But it means it's important. And things that are important deserve time and space and respect, even if they're not practical." She smiled. "Someone wise recently reminded me that life isn't just about achievement. It's about the things that make us whole. For you, that's clearly art."

Elliot looked down at their sketchbook, now missing three pages. "I've never shown anyone before. Not since my grandmother died. She was the only one who understood."

"Well now you have us," Zara said firmly. "And we understand. Or we're trying to, anyway."

"Can we see more?" Finn asked. "Only if you want to share. But I'd love to see the other sketches."

Elliot hesitated, then nodded slowly. They sat down at the library table, and the others gathered around as they began turning pages, narrating some of the drawings, explaining what they'd been trying to capture or what had caught their attention.

There were sketches from Hogsmeade, from Quidditch matches, from classes and common rooms and corridors. Some were silly—Peeves in mid-chaos, Nearly Headless Nick looking affronted. Others were tender—two first years helping each other with homework, Professor McGonagall smiling at something a student said, Hagrid with Fang's head in his lap.

"You see so much," Mira said wonderingly. "So many small moments that everyone else just walks past."

"That's what artists do," Elliot said, and for the first time they didn't sound defensive about it. Just matter-of-fact. "We notice. We remember. We try to hold onto things that would otherwise disappear."

"Like photographs," Finn said, "but better. Because they have your interpretation in them. Your feelings about what you're seeing."

"Exactly." Elliot's expression had softened. "A photograph shows what was there. A drawing shows what it meant."

They reached a sketch near the end of the book—all four of them on the balcony at the Yule Ball, which hadn't happened yet. It was rougher than the others, more gesture than detail, but the composition was striking. Four figures against a starry sky, close together, belonging.

"When did you draw this?" Zara asked.

"Last week. It's not real—obviously, since the ball hasn't happened yet. It's just... how I imagine it. How I hope it will be." Elliot's voice went quiet. "All of us together. Happy. Exactly ourselves."

"It will be," Mira promised. "It'll be exactly like this."

They sat there for a while longer, going through the sketchbook, asking questions, marveling at Elliot's ability to capture personality in a few pencil strokes. Eventually Madam Oakwood came by with a pointed look at the time, and they gathered their things reluctantly.

As they left the library, Elliot pulled Mira aside. "Thank you," they said quietly. "For not making me feel like a creep for drawing everyone. For understanding."

"Thank you for showing us how you see the world," Mira replied. "For trusting us with something so personal."

"I didn't exactly have a choice," Elliot pointed out, but they were smiling.

"Maybe that's better sometimes. Maybe the things we'd never choose to share are the ones that matter most."

"Very Ravenclaw of you."

"I'll take that as a compliment."

They walked together toward the Great Hall for dinner, catching up with Finn and Zara ahead. Zara had rolled up her portrait carefully and was holding it like treasure. Finn had tucked his into his bag with extreme care.

"So," Zara said as they all walked together, "three days until the Yule Ball. Everyone clear on the plan?"

"Meet in the Entrance Hall at seven," Finn recited. "You're going to do everyone's hair beforehand."

"In the Ravenclaw common room at five," Mira added. "Because it has the best light."

"And we're taking at least one photo all together," Elliot finished. "Before we get too caught up in the actual ball."

"Perfect." Zara grinned. "It's going to be amazing."

"It already is," Finn said, and they all knew he wasn't just talking about the ball. He was talking about this—the four of them, their secrets shared and accepted, their art and vulnerabilities and true selves seen and celebrated.

That evening, Mira carefully pressed Elliot's drawing of her reaching toward the frost between the pages of a heavy book to keep it flat. Later, she'd find a proper frame for it. But for now, she kept taking it out to look at, trying to see herself as Elliot had drawn her.

Wonder-struck. Open. Brave enough to reach toward magic even when she couldn't fully understand it.

Was that really her? Or was it who she was becoming?

Maybe, Mira thought as she tucked the drawing safely away, it was both. Maybe Elliot's gift wasn't just in capturing what was, but in seeing what could be. In drawing out the potential they recognized in their friends and reflecting it back until it became real.

She added a new entry to her December journal:

*December 18: Today I learned that being seen—truly seen—is both terrifying and necessary. That art isn't just about technical skill but about honesty, vulnerability, and the courage to say "this is how I experience the world." I learned that Elliot has been drawing us, holding our moments and our essences on paper, seeing us not as we fear we are but as we actually are at our best.*

*I learned that sometimes the most important gifts are the ones we never meant to give. That sharing the hidden parts of ourselves—whether that's art or identity or any other truth we've kept close—is how we let people love us fully.*

*Three days until the Yule Ball. But the real magic has already happened. In a quiet library, in a sketchbook left behind by accident, in the moment we chose to see and be seen without flinching.*

*That's intimacy. That's friendship. That's the kind of magic no spell can create.*

Outside her window, snow fell gently over the castle. And in three other dormitories, three other students looked at pencil sketches that showed them how they were loved, and felt the particular warmth of being known.

Not perfect. Not curated. Not performing.

Just themselves. Seen and celebrated and enough.

Exactly as they were.
