The December evening had settled into that particular kind of cold that made even the warmest common room fires feel inadequate. Finn MacLeod pulled his scarf tighter as he waited outside the entrance to the Ravenclaw common room, bouncing slightly on his heels to stay warm. Through the narrow windows of the corridor, he could see snow falling steadily—the third snowfall in five days.

"This better be good, MacLeod," Zara's voice came from behind him. She appeared around the corner with Elliot, both looking windswept from their journey up from the dungeons and Gryffindor Tower respectively. "Some of us were perfectly comfortable by a fire."

"It's good," Finn promised. "Trust me."

"We always trust you," Elliot said, adjusting the silver scarf they'd wrapped around their neck—a handmade gift from their grandmother, they'd mentioned once. "That's why we're here freezing our arses off instead of somewhere warm."

The door to Ravenclaw Tower swung open, and Mira emerged, already bundled in her winter robes with a thick knitted hat. "Sorry! Had to finish an essay sentence. Where are we going exactly? Your note just said to dress warm and bring an appetite."

"The kitchens," Finn said, grinning at their reactions.

Zara's eyes widened. "You're finally taking us to the kitchens? After months of us asking?"

"Special occasion," Finn said mysteriously. "Come on, or we'll miss it."

They made their way down through the castle, past the decorated corridors—the suits of armor were still periodically breaking into Christmas carols, courtesy of Peeves' continuing enchantment—and down several more flights of stairs. The lower levels of Hogwarts were warmer, heated by the kitchens and the general industry of castle maintenance.

"How did you even find the kitchens?" Mira asked as they walked. "They're not on the Marauder's Map, and I've read three books about Hogwarts' architecture."

"Hufflepuff tradition," Finn said. "Gets passed down. We're right near our common room, so there's a sort of... cultural knowledge, I guess? Plus the house elves like Hufflepuffs. Something about Helga Hufflepuff being particularly kind to them."

"They *like* you?" Elliot raised an eyebrow. "As in, it's mutual?"

"You'll see," Finn said.

He stopped in front of a painting of a massive bowl of fruit and reached out to tickle the pear. It squirmed, giggling, and transformed into a large green door handle. Finn pulled the door open and gestured them through. "After you."

---

The Hogwarts kitchens were enormous.

That was Mira's first thought as she stepped through the doorway—the sheer *scale* of the place. It was the same size and shape as the Great Hall above it, with high ceilings and long wooden tables. But where the Great Hall was grand and formal, the kitchens were warm and busy and alive with activity.

House elves were everywhere. Dozens of them, maybe more, working at various stations throughout the kitchen. Some stirred massive copper pots at the enormous stove that took up most of one wall. Others chopped vegetables with precise, quick movements. A team worked at what looked like a pastry station, rolling dough and filling tarts. The air smelled incredible—bread and chocolate and cinnamon and something else, something warm and comforting that Mira couldn't quite name.

"Master Finn!" A high, delighted voice called out, and a small house elf wearing a tea towel patterned with dancing snitches came hurrying over. "Master Finn is here! And Master Finn brought friends!"

"Hey, Dibby," Finn said warmly, crouching down to be more at eye level. "I hope it's alright that I brought them? I wanted them to meet everyone, and I thought—well, I heard you were doing something special tonight?"

"Yes, yes! Tonight is the special hot chocolate service!" Dibby's huge eyes were bright with excitement. "For all the students, because it is being so cold. We is making five different kinds! With the marshmallows and the cream and the special spices! And—oh! You is wanting to help? Master Finn always helps!"

"If that's okay?" Finn glanced back at his friends. "We'd love to help, if you need extra hands."

"We would be most happy for help!" Dibby clapped their small hands together. "Come, come! Tibby is being in charge of the chocolate station. She will be showing you!"

They followed Dibby through the kitchen, weaving between work stations. The house elves they passed looked up and waved or called greetings to Finn, who returned them all by name. Mira was impressed—there had to be at least forty elves in here, maybe more.

"You come here a lot," Zara observed quietly.

Finn shrugged, looking slightly embarrassed. "I like it here. It's peaceful. And they're—well. You'll see."

Dibby led them to a large station where several enormous pots were stationed over carefully controlled flames. A house elf in a pristine white tea towel stood on a stool, directing operations. This elf was slightly taller than the others, and wore what looked like a small knitted scarf in Gryffindor colors.

"Tibby!" Dibby called. "Master Finn is here to help, and brought friends!"

The elf turned, and Mira saw her eyes brighten. "Finn! Good, good. We is needing all the help we can get. Five hundred servings of hot chocolate is not making itself!"

"Everyone, this is Tibby," Finn said. "She's basically the dessert genius of Hogwarts. Tibby, these are my friends—Mira, Zara, and Elliot."

"Very pleased to meet you," Tibby said, with a little bow. "Any friends of Finn is welcome in our kitchens. Now—is you wanting to help, or is you just wanting to watch?"

"Help," Zara said immediately. "What do you need?"

---

What followed was one of the most unexpectedly enjoyable evenings Elliot could remember.

Tibby organized them with the efficiency of a general commanding troops. Mira was stationed at the chocolate melting station, carefully monitoring temperature with a precision that reminded Elliot of Potions class. Zara was put in charge of the enormous whisk, stirring pots of milk that were being heated to exact specifications. Finn moved between stations, fetching ingredients and checking temperatures with the easy familiarity of long practice.

Elliot found themselves at a station with a young house elf named Winky, carefully measuring out spices for the different varieties of hot chocolate. There was the classic, of course, but also peppermint, cinnamon-orange, salted caramel, and something Winky called "Tibby's Special Winter Blend" that involved cardamom and a hint of chili.

"You is being very precise with the measuring," Winky observed approvingly. "Most students is just dumping things in."

"My mother's a potioneer," Elliot said, weighing out another quarter-teaspoon of cardamom. "She always says precision is the difference between success and disaster."

"Your mother is being very wise." Winky nodded seriously. "Is you wanting to be a potioneer too?"

The question caught Elliot off-guard. They thought about their cleared potion from the other day, about the truth they'd finally admitted. "I don't know," they said honestly. "Maybe. Or maybe something else. I'm still figuring it out."

"That is being perfectly fine," Winky said. "Winky was not always knowing she wanted to work in kitchens. When Winky was younger, she was thinking she might want to help in greenhouses. But then Winky discovered baking, and—oh! That was being what Winky loved. Sometimes we is finding our path by trying different things."

"Yeah," Elliot said softly. "Yeah, I guess we are."

Across the kitchen, they could hear Zara laughing at something Tibby had said, and Mira was deep in conversation with Dibby about the magical properties of different types of chocolate. Finn moved between the stations like he belonged here, and the house elves clearly adored him—not in the way they were magically compelled to serve wizards, but with genuine warmth.

It was strange, Elliot thought, stirring the spice mixture. They'd learned about house elves in school, of course. Magical creatures bound to serve wizarding families. The lessons had been matter-of-fact, clinical. But this—this was different. These were people, clearly. People with personalities and preferences and pride in their work.

"Can I ask you something?" Elliot said to Winky. "And please tell me if it's rude—I don't want to offend you."

Winky looked up, curious. "You can be asking."

"Do you... like working here? In the kitchens?"

Winky tilted her head, considering. "Is a complicated question. Winky is bound to Hogwarts, yes? House elves is always bound to someplace or someone. But Hogwarts is being a good place. Professor McGonagall is fair. We is having good working conditions. And—" she lowered her voice, "—some things is changing, slowly."

"Changing how?"

"Come, come!" Tibby's voice called out. "Hot chocolate is ready for testing! Everyone must try before we is sending it up!"

---

They gathered around one of the long wooden tables, where Tibby had set out five mugs for each of them. The house elves clustered around, watching anxiously for reactions.

Mira picked up the classic hot chocolate first. It was perfect—rich and smooth, with real melted chocolate, not just cocoa powder. The peppermint was refreshing, cool mint playing against the warm chocolate. The cinnamon-orange was bright and festive. The salted caramel was Zara's immediate favorite, declaring it "absolutely brilliant" while Tibby beamed with pride.

But the Winter Blend—that was something special. The cardamom and chili gave it complexity, warmth that built gradually, with a depth of flavor that surprised Elliot.

"This is incredible," they said, and meant it. "Seriously, Tibby, this is restaurant-quality."

Tibby's ears went pink with pleasure. "Tibby is working very hard on the recipe. And—oh! This is being special for another reason." She glanced around, then seemed to make a decision. "We is not supposed to talk about it much, but—Tibby is being paid for special recipes now."

There was a moment of silence.

"Paid?" Mira asked carefully. "As in, wages?"

"Small wages, yes." Tibby pulled at her scarf almost nervously. "Is still new. Is still being figured out. But Professor McGonagall, she is saying that house elves can be receiving payment for special work, if they is wanting it. Not all elves do! Some is thinking it's not proper. But some of us—well. Is nice to be having options."

"Was this because of Dobby?" Finn asked quietly.

Several of the house elves exchanged glances. Finally, Dibby spoke up. "Dobby is being Tibby's cousin. He is working here for two years, after—after he died."

"He *worked* here?" Zara looked confused. "But—"

"No, no. Before. Before the battle. Before he died." Tibby's voice was soft but steady. "Dobby was being very strange elf. He wanted to be paid. He wanted to be *free*. Most elves thought he was being crazy. But Dobby, he was saying that house elves should be having choices. That serving is being fine if you is choosing it, but not when you is having no choice at all."

The kitchen had gone very quiet. Even the pots seemed to have stopped bubbling.

"After Dobby died—saving Harry Potter and his friends—people is starting to listen more. Harry Potter, he is talking about what Dobby did. And slowly, very slowly, some things is changing." Tibby straightened up, her voice stronger. "Is not being perfect yet. Most house elves is still bound without choosing. But Hogwarts is starting to offer some options. If we is wanting to be paid, we can be. If we is wanting time off, we can ask. If we is wanting to leave—well. That is still being complicated. But is better than before."

Mira had taken off her reading glasses and was wiping them, though Elliot suspected it was more about having something to do with her hands. "That's... that's really important. What Dobby did. What you're doing now."

"Change is being slow," Dibby said. "And sometimes change is being scary, even when is good change. Not all elves is wanting wages. Some is thinking it's not right. But having choice—that is being the important thing."

Zara leaned forward, elbows on the table. "Can I ask—and please tell me to shut up if this is too much—but what would you want? If you could change anything about how things work for house elves?"

There was a long pause. Then Winky spoke up, her voice small but clear. "Winky is wanting young elves to learn different things. Not just how to serve, but how to think for themselves. How to know what they is wanting."

"I is wanting respect," another elf added. "Not being treated like furniture. Being treated like people."

"I is wanting," Tibby said slowly, "for house elves to be able to choose who they is serving. Or if they is serving at all. To have that be a choice we is making, not something that is just happening to us."

Elliot found themselves nodding. They thought about their own struggles with expectations, with paths laid out that they hadn't chosen. It wasn't the same—not at all the same, they were free in ways house elves weren't—but something about the desire to choose your own path, that resonated deeply.

"For what it's worth," Mira said quietly, "I think what you're doing—talking about this, working toward change—I think Dobby would be proud."

Tibby's eyes grew shiny. "Tibby is hoping so. Now!" She clapped her hands, clearly needing to move past the emotion. "Enough being serious! We is having hot chocolate to serve, and students who is being cold and grumpy upstairs! Everyone back to work!"

---

The next hour was a flurry of activity as they prepared the service. The hot chocolate was poured into enormous enchanted pitchers that would keep it perfectly hot. Platters of marshmallows—both regular and enchanted to toast themselves—were arranged. Whipped cream was conjured in enormous bowls. Tibby even added special cookies that another elf had baked, shaped like snowflakes and stars.

"How does it get upstairs?" Zara asked, watching the organization with a professional interest born of planning Quidditch strategies.

"Magic!" several elves chorused, and with a unified snap, the entire service disappeared.

"Where did it—" Elliot started.

"Is appearing in the Great Hall," Dibby explained. "Students will be finding hot chocolate stations set up at each house table. They is helping themselves. Is easier than us serving individually—less work, and students is having fun choosing their flavors."

Finn checked a clock on the wall. "We should probably head up there. If we're not in the Hall, people will wonder where we are."

"Thank you," Mira said, looking around at the house elves. "For letting us help. And for—for talking with us. About everything."

"You is being welcome anytime," Tibby said warmly. "Any friends of Finn's is friends of ours. And—thank you for listening. Not many students is really listening when we talks about these things."

As they made their way to the door, Finn hung back for a moment. Elliot saw him talking quietly with Tibby, saw him press something into her hand—coins, probably, though Tibby tried to protest. Finn said something else, too quiet to hear, and Tibby nodded, holding the coins carefully.

Then Finn jogged to catch up with his friends, his cheeks slightly pink.

"You pay them," Zara said. It wasn't a question.

"When I can," Finn said. "When they'll take it. Most don't want to, but Tibby and a few others—they're trying to normalize it. Make it less strange. So sometimes I buy special stuff from them. Cookies, or hot chocolate mix to send home. It's not much, but—"

"It's something," Mira finished. "It's treating them like people who provide a service, not just... automatons."

"Yeah."

They walked in silence for a moment, climbing back up toward the main levels of the castle. Elliot was thinking about Winky's comment about trying different things, about finding your path. About Tibby's wages and Dobby's choices and the slow, difficult work of change.

"I didn't expect tonight to be so..." Zara trailed off.

"Heavy?" Elliot supplied.

"Important," Zara corrected. "I thought we were just going to make hot chocolate and eat cookies. But that was—we talked about things that actually matter."

"Things can be both," Mira said thoughtfully. "Light and heavy. Fun and important. That's kind of the point, isn't it? They were making hot chocolate for the whole school, doing something kind and warm. And also thinking about ethics and rights and change. It's all connected."

They emerged into the entrance hall just as students began streaming toward the Great Hall, drawn by word spreading about the hot chocolate service. Through the open doors, Elliot could see the stations that had appeared—each house table had a setup with the five different varieties, complete with toppings and festive decorations.

"Come on," Finn said. "Let's get some before it's mobbed."

They entered the Great Hall together, the warmth and noise and excitement washing over them. Students were already crowding around the hot chocolate stations, exclaiming over the flavors, piling on marshmallows and whipped cream. The enchanted ceiling showed the snowy evening outside, flakes drifting but never reaching the floor.

Elliot ended up with the Winter Blend again, finding a spot at one of the long tables between Mira and Finn. Zara had gotten the salted caramel and was already going back for seconds. The Hall was filled with contentment—that particular kind of coziness that comes from being somewhere warm while it's cold outside, with something sweet to drink and friends nearby.

"Can I ask something?" Mira said, cupping her mug in both hands. "And this is just me thinking out loud, so tell me if I'm being ridiculous."

"You're never ridiculous," Finn said loyally.

"What do we do with what we learned tonight?" Mira looked around at them. "About the house elves. About what's changing and what still needs to change. Do we just... know it? Or should we do something?"

"Like what?" Zara asked.

"I don't know. Talk about it? Write about it? There was that group Hermione Granger started, right? S.P.E.W., Society for the Promotion of Elfish Welfare? Maybe we could—"

"Carefully," Elliot interrupted. "We'd have to be careful. The house elves said themselves that change is complicated. Some of them don't want wages or different treatment. We can't just decide what's best for them."

"No," Mira agreed. "But we could listen to what they're saying they want. Support the ones who are trying to create change. Pay them when we can, like Finn does. Treat them like people, not servants. Talk about it when others don't want to hear."

"Slow change," Finn said. "Like Tibby said. But real change."

Zara nodded slowly. "I can work with that. Small things that build up over time."

They sat with that for a moment, sipping their hot chocolate and watching the Hall fill with warmth and light. Around them, students laughed and talked and enjoyed the unexpected treat. In the kitchens below, house elves were probably cleaning up, proud of a job well done. And somewhere in the complex web of it all, change was happening—slowly, incrementally, but genuinely.

"You know what this reminds me of?" Finn said suddenly. "The frost. From December first. Mira's frost message."

"Seeking wonder in small things?" Mira asked.

"Yeah, but also—it was about paying attention. Not just rushing past stuff. Actually seeing what's there." Finn gestured with his mug. "Tonight we paid attention. We saw the house elves as people. We listened. That's the small thing. But it matters."

"Everything matters," Elliot said quietly. "Every choice we make about how to treat people—or how to treat anyone. It all adds up."

Zara raised her mug. "To small things that matter."

"To paying attention," Mira added.

"To house elves who make incredible hot chocolate," Finn said, grinning.

"And to friends who drag you down to kitchens on cold evenings," Elliot finished.

They clinked their mugs together, the ceramic meeting with soft taps that were lost in the noise of the Hall. The hot chocolate was still perfectly warm, the Winter Blend complex and comforting. Outside, snow continued to fall. Below, in the kitchens, house elves worked and rested and talked about the future they were slowly building.

And four friends sat together, warmed by more than just hot chocolate, thinking about ethics and change and the small actions that, over time, could shift the whole world.

It had been, Elliot thought, an unexpectedly perfect evening. Good work, good conversation, good friends, and really excellent hot chocolate. Sometimes the most important moments came disguised as simple ones. You just had to pay attention.

Tibby would approve of that thought.

---

Later, as they prepared to head to their separate common rooms, Finn pulled something from his bag—a small package wrapped in brown paper.

"Almost forgot," he said, handing it to Mira. "From Tibby. She said to give it to you specifically."

Mira unwrapped it carefully. Inside was a small tin of what looked like hot chocolate mix, with a note in careful, spidery writing:

*For Mira who listens. Is Tibby's Special Winter Blend. Is also being recipe on bottom of tin, if you is wanting to make at home. Thank you for caring about house elves. Thank you for seeing us. —Tibby*

Mira's eyes were suspiciously shiny as she read it. "Oh. That's—" She stopped, wiped her glasses again. "That's really lovely."

"She liked you," Finn said simply. "You listened like you actually cared."

"I do care," Mira said firmly. "We all do."

"I know," Finn said. "That's why I brought you."

They said their goodnights and headed off in different directions—Zara to Gryffindor Tower, still raving about the salted caramel; Elliot to the Slytherin dungeons, thinking about choices and paths and respect; Finn to the Hufflepuff common room, satisfied with sharing something important with his friends; and Mira to Ravenclaw Tower, carefully holding her tin of hot chocolate and thinking about house elves and change and the power of actually listening.

The castle settled into its evening rhythms, warm and peaceful, full of students warmed by hot chocolate and house elves finishing their cleaning. And in the complex, ongoing story of how wizarding society treated magical creatures, a few more small steps had been taken toward something better.

Slowly. Carefully. With respect and care and attention.

Just like Tibby's Winter Blend—small ingredients coming together to create something unexpectedly wonderful.
