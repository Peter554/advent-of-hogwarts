The carriages to Hogsmeade rattled along the snow-covered road, their wheels leaving dark tracks through the white. Inside, students chattered excitedly about their plans for the day—the shops, the butterbeer, the freedom of a Saturday afternoon away from the castle.

Mira pressed her face close to the window, watching the grounds transform as they descended the hill. Hogwarts looked like something from a storybook behind them, all snow-dusted towers and glowing windows. Ahead, the village of Hogsmeade emerged from the winter landscape like a gift waiting to be unwrapped.

"I can't believe we're already halfway through December," Zara said from across the carriage. She was wedged between Finn and a third-year Gryffindor who couldn't stop staring at her prefect badge. "Feels like the month just started."

"That's because it's been eventful," Elliot observed. They were sketching something in their notebook—probably the view from the window. "Frost messages, Nifflers, Quidditch, Dueling Club, the Yule Ball announcement..."

"Don't forget the Great Pudding Incident of last Tuesday," Finn added.

"We agreed never to speak of that again," Mira said firmly, though she was smiling.

The carriage slowed as they entered the village proper. Snow had transformed Hogsmeade into something magical even by wizarding standards. Every shop front was decorated for the season—wreaths on doors, enchanted snowflakes dancing in windows, garlands of holly and ivy strung between lampposts. The Three Broomsticks glowed warmly at the village center, smoke rising cheerfully from its chimney.

"Right," Zara said as they disembarked, stamping snow from her boots. "What's the plan?"

"I was thinking we could split up," Mira suggested. "Do our own shopping, then meet up later? Say, four o'clock at the Three Broomsticks?"

"Giving us room to buy each other surprise gifts?" Elliot asked knowingly.

"Maybe," Mira admitted. Christmas was coming, after all, and she had specific ideas about what she wanted to find for each of her friends.

"Works for me," Finn said, adjusting his yellow and black scarf against the cold. "I've got some errands to run anyway."

"Three Broomsticks at four, then," Zara confirmed. "And if anyone finds anything cool, we can show off when we meet up."

They separated at the main street, each heading in different directions. Mira watched her friends disappear into the crowd—Zara striding toward the upper end of the village, Finn hovering uncertainly near Gladrags, Elliot already turning down a side street she hadn't noticed before. Then she turned her own steps toward Tomes and Scrolls, anticipation warm in her chest.

---

## Zara

Zara had a mission: find the perfect gift for her younger sister. Amara was ten, would be getting her Hogwarts letter next year, and was absolutely obsessed with magical creatures. Which meant Zara needed something special, something that said *I'm proud of you* and *you're going to love this place* all at once.

She headed for Dervish and Banges first, thinking maybe a student care package—replacement quills, parchment, useful things. But nothing felt right. Too practical, not enough wonder.

She was passing the Magical Menagerie, considering whether they'd let her mail-order a Pygmy Puff, when she literally bumped into someone large, hairy, and smelling distinctly of dog.

"Oh! Sorry, I wasn't—Hagrid!"

The half-giant gamekeeper turned around, his beetle-black eyes crinkling with recognition. "Zara! Didn't see yeh there. How're yeh doin'?"

"Good! Christmas shopping." She gestured vaguely at the village. "You too?"

"Aye. Got a new Kneazle kitten at me hut—real beauty, orange and ginger she is—and I'm tryin' ter find her somethin' special fer Christmas. Reckon she deserves a treat, considerin' she keeps the Nifflers from eatin' me furniture."

Zara grinned. "I'm trying to find something for my sister. She's starting next year, and she's creature-obsessed. You have any ideas?"

"Creature-obsessed, eh?" Hagrid's face lit up. "Well, yeh've come ter the right place. C'mon, let me show yeh somethin'."

He led her into the Magical Menagerie, ducking to fit through the doorway. Inside, the shop was warm and noisy with chirps, squeaks, and mysterious rustling sounds. The witch behind the counter nodded at Hagrid familiarly.

"Right then," Hagrid said, navigating between cages with surprising grace. "If yer sister's comin' ter Hogwarts, she'll want somethin' useful *and* interesting. Now, fer useful, yeh can't beat a good creature care kit—brushes fer owls, treats fer cats, that sort of thing."

He picked up a leather case, opening it to reveal an array of small tools and bottles. "But fer interesting..." He moved to a display near the back. "These here are creature identification cards. Like Famous Witches and Wizards cards, but fer magical creatures. Got information on 'em, habitats, care instructions, the works. Been collectin' 'em meself."

Zara examined the display. The cards were beautiful—illustrated with moving creatures, each one shimmering with enchantment. She could see a Hippogriff bowing, a Phoenix mid-flight, even a tiny Hungarian Horntail breathing fire.

"These are perfect," she breathed.

"Tell yeh what," Hagrid said conspiratorially. "Get her the starter set, and I'll send her some o' me doubles when term starts. Got a whole collection o' rare ones."

"Hagrid, you don't have to—"

"Course I don't have to. Want to. Any friend o' yours, and all that. Plus, always nice ter encourage young folks interested in creatures. Never enough of us who really care about 'em properly."

Zara felt a swell of affection for the gentle giant. "Thank you. Really. She's going to love these."

"Just tell her ter come say hello when she arrives next year. I'll introduce her ter some o' the gentler creatures we've got on the grounds."

Twenty minutes later, Zara left the Menagerie with a wrapped package of creature cards and a small toy Niffler for Hagrid's Kneazle ("She'll love chasin' it," he'd assured her). She also left with a reminder of why she loved Hogwarts so much—not just the magic, but the people. The unexpected kindness, the way community just happened when you let it.

She tucked the package carefully into her bag and turned her steps toward Honeydukes. Amara deserved some chocolate to go with those cards.

## Finn

Finn stood outside Gladrags Wizardwear for a full five minutes before finally gathering the courage to go inside.

This was ridiculous. It was just a shop. He was just buying a gift. There was absolutely nothing strange or significant about wanting to buy something nice for a friend.

Except Oliver wasn't just a friend. Oliver was... Oliver. The Hufflepuff fifth year who helped in the greenhouses, who had a smile that made Finn forget how to form words, who'd said *yes* when Finn had finally, nervously asked him to the Yule Ball.

And now Finn wanted to get him something thoughtful. Something that said *I've been paying attention* without saying *I'm hopelessly in love with you*, because that felt like too much too soon.

The shop was warm when he finally entered, smelling of wool and cedar and something faintly spicy. A wizard in emerald robes looked up from arranging a display of dress robes.

"Help you find something?"

"Just browsing," Finn said automatically. Then, gathering courage: "Actually, I'm looking for a gift. For someone. Gloves, maybe?"

"Winter gloves? Right over here."

The selection was overwhelming. Leather gloves, wool gloves, gloves with warming charms, gloves that changed color with your mood. Finn picked through them carefully, trying to think about what Oliver would actually want.

Oliver worked with plants. He needed something practical but nice. Something warm but durable. Something—

His hand landed on a pair of dragon-hide gloves, dyed a deep forest green. They were beautiful—clearly quality craftsmanship—but understated. The kind of thing you could wear in the greenhouse without worrying about ruining them, but nice enough to show you'd put thought into it.

"Dragon-hide's very warm," the shopkeeper said. "Practically indestructible, too. Popular with Quidditch players, but I've sold plenty to Herbology students. The hide breathes well, see, so your hands don't get too sweaty when working."

Practical *and* thoughtful. Perfect.

"How much?"

The price was more than Finn had planned to spend, but he had savings from helping Professor Sprout over the summer. And it was worth it to see Oliver's face when he opened them.

"I'll take them."

As the shopkeeper wrapped the gloves in silver paper, Finn felt a mix of emotions he couldn't quite name. Nervousness, excitement, fear, hope—all tangled together. He'd told Zara he was gay. He'd asked Oliver to the ball. He was buying a gift with intention and care.

He was being honest about who he was and what he wanted. And that was terrifying and exhilarating all at once.

He tucked the wrapped package into his coat pocket, where it rested against his heart like a secret promise. Outside, snow was starting to fall again, dusting his shoulders as he walked. He still had time before meeting the others, so he headed toward Honeydukes, thinking about peppermint and possibilities.

## Mira

Tomes and Scrolls was everything Mira loved about bookshops: cramped, cluttered, smelling of old paper and leather bindings. She could spend hours here and had, on previous visits.

Today, though, she had a purpose. She was looking for something specific—books for her friends that would mean something personal. But she also couldn't resist browsing.

The shop's owner, an elderly witch named Mrs. Catchpole, recognized her immediately. "Miss Patel! Back again? Let me guess—Ancient Runes section?"

"Eventually," Mira said, smiling. "But I'm actually shopping for gifts today."

"Ah! Then you'll want the winter collection. I've just got some beautiful editions in—classic texts with new illustrations. Very gift-appropriate."

Mrs. Catchpole showed her to a table near the back, where books were displayed like treasures. Mira's fingers traced over spines, reading titles, opening covers to check the quality of pages and printing.

For Zara: a book on the history of magical sports across different cultures. It had chapters on African flying traditions and Arabian carpet racing alongside the more familiar Quidditch history. Perfect.

For Finn: a beautiful illustrated guide to magical and mundane medicinal plants, with pressed samples and handwritten notes from various herbologists. He'd love it.

For Elliot: this one was harder. Elliot read widely but was particular about books. She browsed the shelves, thinking about what Elliot had said about finding themselves in books, about discovering words for experiences they'd always had.

And then she found it: *The Alchemy of Identity: Transformation and Truth in Magic and Life*. It was part philosophy, part magical theory, with essays about change, self-discovery, and becoming. The kind of book that would give Elliot words for things they were still figuring out.

With her three gifts selected and wrapped, Mira finally allowed herself to browse for pleasure. She drifted to the constellation mythology section and found a gorgeous edition with silver-edged pages and illustrations that seemed to shimmer in the light. It was expensive, but...

She'd worked at her dad's surgery over the summer, helping file patient records. She had spending money. And this book would sit on her bedside table and remind her of star charts and conversations with Professor Sinistra, of learning to be good enough instead of perfect.

"I'll take this one too," she told Mrs. Catchpole.

"Treating yourself? Good. We should all give ourselves gifts occasionally. Reminds us that we matter."

Mira left the bookshop with a heavy bag and a light heart. The street outside was busier now, students and villagers moving between shops. Snow continued to fall, and the afternoon light was beginning to soften toward evening. She had just enough time to stop at Honeydukes before meeting the others.

## Elliot

Elliot had no particular destination in mind, which was unusual for them. Usually they planned their Hogsmeade visits with precision—which shops, in what order, with time allotted for each. But today they just... wandered.

They'd already stopped at the Hogsmeade branch of Flourish and Blotts, picking up new sketching supplies and a book on magical portraiture. They'd browsed the apothecary, thinking about their Potions homework. They'd even stopped at Honeydukes, buying a bag of crystallized ginger that reminded them of home.

But nothing felt quite right. They were looking for something, they just didn't know what.

Which is how they found themselves turning down a narrow side street they'd never noticed before, following some instinct they couldn't quite name.

The shop at the end of the street was tiny—barely more than a doorway wedged between two larger buildings. A small sign read *Peculiar Antiquities* in faded gold lettering. The window display showed an eclectic mix of objects: a tarnished compass, a music box, several pieces of jewelry, a small painting of a ship.

Elliot tried the door. It opened with a soft chime.

Inside, the shop was bigger than it appeared—magically extended, clearly. Every surface was covered with objects of varying age and origin. An elderly wizard sat behind the counter, smoking a long pipe, not looking up as Elliot entered.

"Help yourself to looking," he said, voice rough but not unfriendly. "Everything's got a story. Some'll tell you theirs if you listen right."

Elliot moved through the shop slowly, touching objects gently. A silver pocket watch. A set of crystal vials. A worn leather journal with blank pages that shimmered when opened.

And then, in a small case near the back, they saw it.

A pendant. Simple, elegant—a silver circle with a horizontal line through it, hanging on a delicate chain. It was subtle enough to wear every day but distinct enough to mean something. To say something.

Elliot knew the symbol. They'd seen it in the library books they'd found, the ones about gender identity and expression. It was one of the symbols for non-binary identity—clean, clear, present without being loud.

They picked it up carefully, feeling the weight of it in their palm. This was exactly what they'd been looking for without knowing they were looking.

"That's a good choice," the shopkeeper said. He'd appeared beside them without sound. "Old piece, that. Silver from Wales, charm-worked to resist tarnish. Been waiting for the right person."

"How much?"

The price was reasonable. Elliot paid without haggling, watching as the wizard wrapped it in soft cloth and placed it in a small velvet bag.

"Wear it well," he said. "Symbols have power. Not just magical power—the power of recognition. Of saying 'this is who I am' to yourself and to the world."

Elliot nodded, unable to form words around the sudden tightness in their throat.

Outside, they found a quiet spot in a small park, brushing snow from a bench to sit. They pulled out the pendant, studying it in the winter light. Then, carefully, they fastened the chain around their neck, tucking the symbol under their shirt where it rested cool against their skin.

It felt right. Like putting on a piece of clothing that actually fit instead of something borrowed or too tight. Like wearing their own name.

They sat there for a while, watching snow fall, feeling the pendant's weight. Feeling seen, even though they were alone. Feeling like themselves in a way that was new and yet entirely familiar.

Eventually, they checked their watch. Nearly four. They stood, brushing snow from their robes, and headed toward the Three Broomsticks with their bags of purchases and one very important piece of jewelry hidden beneath their collar.

---

The Three Broomsticks was packed, warm and loud and smelling of butterbeer and roasting meat. But their friends had claimed a corner table, and Finn was already waving them over.

"There you are!" Zara said as Elliot slid into the remaining seat. "We were about to send a search party."

"Got distracted," Elliot said. "Found a shop I'd never noticed before."

"Same," Mira said. "I lost an hour in Tomes and Scrolls without realizing."

"I ran into Hagrid at the Magical Menagerie," Zara announced. "He helped me find the perfect gift for Amara."

"The creature cards?" Finn asked. "Those are brilliant."

"You saw them?"

"I was in there earlier looking at owl treats. Saw you and Hagrid conferring very seriously over the display."

They laughed, and Madam Rosmerta appeared with four butterbeers, the foam topped with cinnamon. Elliot wrapped their cold hands around the warm mug, feeling the day settle around them like a comfortable blanket.

"Good day?" Mira asked, looking around at each of them.

"Really good," Finn said, and there was something in his voice—a quiet confidence—that hadn't been there this morning.

"Same," Zara agreed. "Productive and festive."

"I found exactly what I needed," Elliot said, fingers brushing unconsciously against the pendant beneath their shirt. "Didn't know I was looking for it, but I found it anyway."

"That's the best kind of shopping," Mira said. She raised her mug. "To December in Hogsmeade."

"To surprise discoveries," Finn added.

"To friendship," Zara said.

"To being exactly who we are," Elliot finished quietly.

They clinked their mugs together, and butterbeer sloshed slightly, and someone at the next table started singing an off-key carol. Outside, snow continued to fall on the village, blanketing everything in soft white. Inside, four friends sat together, bags full of carefully chosen gifts and hearts full of the particular warmth that comes from a day well spent.

Mira pulled out her notebook and started sketching the scene—not to preserve it perfectly, but to remember it. The way the lamplight caught Zara's box braids. The slight smile on Finn's face as he stared into his butterbeer, thinking about something that made his ears go red. The way Elliot sat, relaxed in a way they rarely were, one hand absently touching their throat.

Moments like this, she thought, were what December magic was really about. Not the big gestures or dramatic revelations, but the quiet accumulation of good days. The way friendship felt like coming home. The courage it took to buy gloves for someone you liked, or to wear a symbol of who you were, or to simply be present with people who saw you.

"What are you writing?" Elliot asked, noticing her focus.

"Just remembering," Mira said. "Paying attention to small things."

"Frost message wisdom," Finn said, understanding immediately.

"Exactly."

They stayed at the Three Broomsticks until the sky outside grew dark and Madam Rosmerta started lighting more candles. They talked and laughed and showed off a few of their purchases (the non-gift ones), and when they finally bundled back up for the carriage ride to Hogwarts, Mira felt the particular satisfaction of a day spent exactly right.

The carriage was less crowded on the return journey, students dozing against windows or comparing purchases in tired voices. Mira watched Hogsmeade disappear behind them, the village lights twinkling through the snow like earthbound stars.

"Next Saturday's the last Hogsmeade visit before holiday," Zara said drowsily. "We should go again."

"We should make it a tradition," Finn agreed. "December Hogsmeade adventures."

"I'm in," Elliot said. Their hand had found its way to their throat again, touching something Mira couldn't quite see. But whatever it was made them smile—small and private and genuine.

"Then it's decided," Mira said. "A tradition."

And as Hogwarts came back into view, its windows glowing warm against the winter night, she thought about traditions—how they started as simple decisions and became something more. How the best ones weren't imposed but chosen. How friendship itself was a tradition, renewed every time you showed up for each other.

The carriage pulled up to the castle entrance, and they tumbled out into the cold, laden with packages and full of butterbeer and good stories. They separated at the entrance hall, heading to different common rooms but carrying the same warm feeling.

And that night, in four different dormitories, they each thought about the day in their own way. About gifts chosen with care. About symbols that meant something. About the courage it took to be honest about who you were and what you wanted.

About December magic, found not just in frost patterns or formal balls, but in ordinary Saturdays made extraordinary by intention and friendship.

Outside, snow continued to fall on Hogwarts. And inside, wrapped in separate packages in separate rooms, were gifts that said *I see you* and *I know you* and *I'm glad you're in my life*.

Which was, perhaps, the best kind of magic there was.
