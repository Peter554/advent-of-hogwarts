Finn had been trying to work up the courage all day.

He'd almost said something at breakfast, but Elliot had been there, and Mira too, and it felt like too many people. Then he'd considered pulling Zara aside after Herbology, but Professor Sprout had asked him to help reorganize the potting supplies, and by the time he'd finished, Zara had already left for her next class.

Now it was late afternoon, the December sun already setting behind the castle walls, and he was running out of day. Running out of time. Running out of excuses.

The Yule Ball was in five days.

Finn stood at the entrance to the Quidditch pitch, his hands shoved deep in his pockets, watching the Gryffindor team run drills. Even from this distance, Zara was easy to spot—she flew with a confidence and joy that made it look effortless, weaving between the other Chasers as they practiced passing patterns.

His stomach twisted with nerves that had nothing to do with flying and everything to do with what he needed to tell her.

Finally, after what felt like hours but was probably only twenty minutes, he heard the whistle blow and saw the team begin their descent. Finn waited, shifting his weight from foot to foot, as the players landed and started gathering their equipment.

Zara spotted him as she dismounted, her face breaking into a grin. "Finn! What are you doing out here? It's freezing."

"I know. I just..." He swallowed hard. "Can we talk? Just you and me?"

Something in his voice must have registered, because Zara's expression shifted immediately from playful to concerned. She turned to her teammates. "Go ahead without me. I'll catch up."

"But the debriefing—" one of the other Chasers started.

"I said I'll catch up," Zara repeated firmly, already walking toward Finn. She rested her broom against the stands and gave him her full attention. "What's going on? You look like you're about to be sick."

"I feel like I'm about to be sick," Finn admitted. His hands were trembling in his pockets. "There's something I need to tell you. Something I've needed to tell someone for a while now."

Zara's concern deepened, but she nodded. "Okay. Do you want to walk? Or sit?"

"Walk, maybe. I don't think I can sit still right now."

They set off across the grounds, away from the pitch and toward the lake. The frozen grass crunched under their feet, and the cold air made Finn's breath visible in small clouds. He tried to organize his thoughts, to find the right words, but everything he'd rehearsed in his head suddenly seemed inadequate.

"Take your time," Zara said quietly. "Whatever it is, you can tell me."

And maybe it was that—the gentleness in her voice, the certainty that she would listen—that finally made the words come.

"I'm gay," Finn said, the words tumbling out faster than he'd intended. "I like boys. And there's this person—Oliver, he's in Hufflepuff too, in our year—and I've had feelings for him for months now. And the Yule Ball is coming up, and I want to ask him, but I'm terrified, and I haven't told anyone any of this until right now, and I just—"

He ran out of breath and stopped walking, his heart hammering so hard he thought it might break through his ribs.

Zara had stopped too. For a moment that stretched into eternity, she just looked at him. And then she stepped forward and wrapped him in a fierce hug.

"Thank you," she said, her voice slightly muffled against his shoulder. "Thank you for trusting me with this."

Finn felt something crack open in his chest—relief so overwhelming it was almost painful. His eyes stung with tears he hadn't expected, and he hugged her back just as tightly.

"You're not... upset? Or weirded out?"

Zara pulled back just enough to look at him, her hands still on his shoulders. Her expression was almost fierce. "Upset? Finn, I'm honored. I'm honored that you felt safe enough to tell me. And I'm so, so happy that you're figuring out who you are."

"You really mean that?"

"Of course I mean that." She squeezed his shoulders gently. "You're my friend. One of my best friends. This doesn't change that. It just means I know you a little better now."

The relief was so intense that Finn actually laughed, a slightly watery sound. "I've been so scared to say anything. I kept thinking... what if people react badly? What if it changes how everyone sees me?"

"Then those people aren't worth your time," Zara said firmly. "But for what it's worth, I don't think our friends are going to react badly. Mira and Elliot? They'll be supportive. I'm sure of it."

Finn nodded, wiping at his eyes with the heel of his hand. "I want to tell them. I do. Just... not yet. One step at a time."

"Whenever you're ready," Zara assured him. "At your pace. No pressure." She paused, then her expression shifted to something more mischievous. "Now. Tell me about Oliver."

"Oh god." Finn felt his face heat despite the cold. "Where do I even start?"

"Start with how you met. And what he's like. And why you like him." Zara linked her arm through his and steered them back toward their walk. "I want all the details."

---

And so Finn told her. About how Oliver had been in his Herbology class since first year, but they hadn't really talked until this autumn when they'd been partnered for a project on Bouncing Bulbs. About how Oliver had made him laugh when one of the bulbs had escaped and bounced straight into Professor Sprout's hat. About the way Oliver's eyes crinkled when he smiled, and how he always had dirt under his fingernails from working in the greenhouses, just like Finn.

"He sounds lovely," Zara said warmly when Finn finally ran out of words. "And I think he'd be lucky to go to the ball with you."

"I don't know about that," Finn said, his earlier anxiety creeping back. "What if he says no? What if he doesn't—what if he's not interested in boys? Or what if he is, but he's not interested in me?"

"Then he says no, and it'll hurt, but you'll survive," Zara said practically. "But Finn, what if he says yes? What if he's been hoping you'd ask?"

"That seems too good to be true."

"Maybe. Or maybe you're exactly what he's hoping for too." Zara bumped her shoulder against his. "Look, I know it's scary. Asking someone to the ball is scary for everyone. But you can't let fear stop you from taking chances. Especially not when it matters this much."

Finn knew she was right. He'd been so focused on all the ways it could go wrong that he hadn't let himself imagine it going right.

"Will you..." He hesitated, feeling vulnerable all over again. "Will you be nearby? When I ask him? I don't need you to hold my hand or anything, but just... knowing you're there might help."

"Of course," Zara said immediately. "Just let me know when and where, and I'll be there. Moral support from a discrete distance."

Finn felt his throat tighten with gratitude. "Thank you. For listening, for not making it weird, for just... being you."

"Anytime," Zara said, and he could hear the smile in her voice. "That's what friends do. We show up for each other."

They walked in comfortable silence for a while, the lake stretching out beside them, its surface frozen and glittering in the fading light. Finn felt lighter than he had in months—like he'd been carrying a weight he hadn't fully acknowledged until he set it down.

He'd told someone. He'd said the words out loud. And the world hadn't ended. In fact, it felt like maybe, just maybe, it had opened up a little.

---

While Finn and Zara were walking by the lake, Mira was in Charms class, trying not to feel anxious about the partner assignment Professor Thorne had just announced.

"The Gemino Charm is particularly complex," Professor Thorne was saying, her enthusiasm for the subject evident in her animated gestures. "It requires not only the correct wand movement and incantation, but also a clear mental image of the object you're duplicating. That's why you'll be working in pairs—one person to cast, one person to observe and provide feedback."

Mira glanced around the classroom, automatically looking for Finn or Zara or Elliot. But Finn was in Herbology this period, Zara had Quidditch practice, and Elliot was in Potions. She'd have to work with someone else.

"I'll be assigning partners randomly," Professor Thorne continued, pulling out a piece of parchment. "Let's see... Mira Patel, you'll be with Priya Desai."

Mira blinked. Priya Desai? She knew the name—a Slytherin in their year—but she couldn't remember ever having a conversation with her. She looked around the classroom and spotted a girl with dark hair and brown skin sitting near the back, gathering her things to move.

Priya approached Mira's table quietly, her expression neutral but not unfriendly. "Hi," she said, settling into the seat beside Mira. "I guess we're partners."

"I guess so," Mira agreed, trying for a smile. "I'm Mira. But you probably knew that already."

"And I'm Priya." There was a hint of amusement in her dark eyes. "I've seen you in the library. You're usually in the Ancient Runes section."

"Oh! Yes, that's... yes." Mira felt herself relax slightly. "I'm taking Ancient Runes as an elective. Do you study it too?"

"I take Arithmancy instead," Priya said. "But I'm interested in the theoretical overlap between the two subjects. There's some fascinating research on how runic sequences can be translated into numerical patterns."

Mira felt a spark of interest. "Really? I've read a little about that, but I haven't found many detailed sources. Most of the books in the library are either purely theoretical or completely practical, with no middle ground."

"Exactly!" Priya leaned forward, her earlier reserve melting into enthusiasm. "It's frustrating. I've been trying to cross-reference *Ancient Runes Made Easy* with Bridget Wenlock's work on the magical properties of the number seven, but the methodologies are so different it's hard to draw direct comparisons."

Before Mira could respond, Professor Thorne clapped her hands for attention. "Alright, everyone! Let's begin with a simple object—a quill. One partner will attempt the Gemino Charm while the other observes. Remember: focus on exact duplication, not just creation. The charm should produce an identical copy, right down to the smallest detail."

Mira pulled out a spare quill and set it on the desk between them. "Do you want to try first, or should I?"

"You go ahead," Priya said. "I'll watch and see if I notice anything in your technique."

Mira lifted her wand, visualizing the quill in careful detail—the slightly bent tip, the small crack near the shaft, the way the feather curved. She focused on that mental image and spoke the incantation clearly.

"Gemino!"

For a moment, nothing happened. Then, with a small shimmer of magic, a second quill appeared beside the first. Mira picked it up, comparing it to the original. The copy was close—the same basic shape and color—but the details were wrong. The tip was straight instead of bent, and the feather was pristine.

"Interesting," Priya said thoughtfully. "You got the overall structure perfectly, but the specific imperfections didn't translate. I think maybe you were focusing too much on what the quill should look like ideally, rather than what it actually looks like."

Mira considered this. It was a perceptive observation, and uncomfortably accurate. She had been visualizing a "perfect" quill, she realized, not this particular quill with all its wear and flaws.

"That's... actually really helpful," she said. "Let me try again."

This time, she forced herself to look at the real quill—really look at it, taking in the bent tip and the small imperfections. She held that messy, real image in her mind as she cast.

"Gemino!"

The duplicate that appeared was much better. Not perfect—the crack was in slightly the wrong place—but recognizably a copy of this specific quill rather than just any quill.

"Much better!" Professor Thorne said, sweeping past their table. "Good observation work, Miss Desai. That's exactly the kind of feedback that helps refine the technique."

Priya looked pleased but modest. "It's easy to spot from the outside. Let's see if I can actually do it myself."

She took her turn, and Mira watched carefully. Priya's visualization seemed strong—her duplicate included the bent tip on the first try—but the proportions were slightly off, the feather a bit too long.

"I think maybe you're focusing on one detail at a time," Mira suggested tentatively. "Like, you're thinking 'bent tip' very clearly, but then the rest of the quill isn't as sharp in your mental image?"

"That makes sense," Priya said, nodding thoughtfully. "I do tend to think in sequential steps rather than holistic images. Let me try holding the whole image at once."

They worked together for the rest of the class period, taking turns casting and observing, offering each other increasingly nuanced feedback. Mira found herself genuinely enjoying the collaboration. Priya was thoughtful and precise in her observations, but not critical in a harsh way. And when Mira made suggestions, Priya actually listened and adjusted her approach.

By the end of class, they'd both successfully duplicated a quill, a button, and a small brass key—each time with increasing accuracy.

"Excellent work, you two," Professor Thorne said as students began packing up. "Your collaboration was exemplary. That's exactly the kind of productive partnership I was hoping to see."

As they gathered their belongings, Priya turned to Mira with a slightly hesitant expression.

"This might be forward, but... would you want to study together sometime? For the Charms exam, or really anything. I feel like we work well together."

Mira felt a warm surprise. She had her core friend group, of course, but she realized she'd been so focused on those relationships that she'd missed opportunities to connect with other students.

"I'd like that," she said, meaning it. "I'm usually in the library after dinner. We could work on that runic-arithmantic correlation too, if you're interested. I think between my Ancient Runes texts and your Arithmancy background, we might actually make some progress."

Priya's face lit up. "Really? That would be amazing. I've been wanting to explore that more but I didn't know anyone else who'd find it interesting."

"Are you kidding? It sounds fascinating."

They exchanged smiles—tentative but genuine—and Mira felt a small glow of pleasure. Making new friends was something she'd always found difficult, too anxious about saying the wrong thing or not being interesting enough. But this had felt easy, natural.

Maybe because they'd been focused on the work rather than trying to force a connection. Or maybe because they genuinely had shared interests that made conversation flow without effort.

Either way, as Mira left the classroom and headed toward the Great Hall for dinner, she found herself looking forward to their study session.

---

At dinner that evening, the four friends gathered at their usual spot—tonight at the Ravenclaw table, near the large windows overlooking the grounds.

Finn seemed different somehow. Lighter. Mira couldn't quite put her finger on what had changed, but there was something in the way he held himself, the way he smiled at Zara when she made a joke about the suspicious casserole on offer.

"You seem happy," Mira observed, passing him the bread basket.

"I am," Finn said simply. Then, with a glance at Zara, "I had a good talk with Zara this afternoon. Really good."

Zara smiled at him—warm and encouraging—and Mira saw some kind of understanding pass between them. Something significant had happened, then. Something Finn wasn't ready to share with everyone yet, but that Zara now knew.

Mira felt a small pang—not jealousy exactly, but a reminder that friendships weren't uniform. People shared different things with different friends, and that was okay. Healthy, even.

"I'm glad," she said sincerely. "You both seem... I don't know, like you're in on a secret."

"Maybe a little," Zara admitted. "But a good secret. The kind that makes things better, not worse."

"Fair enough." Mira turned her attention to her plate, but she was smiling.

Elliot, who had been quietly working through their stew, looked up with interest. "How was everyone's afternoon? I spent mine trying not to inhale Amortentia fumes in Potions. Slughorn was demonstrating the brewing process, and the classroom smelled like a perfume shop exploded."

"What did you smell?" Finn asked curiously.

Elliot's expression became carefully neutral. "Parchment, silver polish, and petrichor. Very boring."

"That's not boring at all," Mira said. "Those are all quite specific. Mine would probably be old books, cardamom tea, and... I don't know, maybe the smell of rain on stone?"

"See, that's not boring either," Elliot pointed out. "Finn?"

Finn's ears went slightly pink. "Fresh soil, wood shavings, and... um, maybe honey?"

Zara grinned. "Mine would definitely include broom polish and leather. Maybe cinnamon too, from my grandmother's kitchen. She makes these amazing chin chin cookies when I visit in the summers."

"I don't think I've ever had chin chin," Finn said.

"They're amazing. Fried dough with nutmeg and sugar. She always makes huge batches because she knows I'll eat half of them myself." Zara's expression turned slightly wistful. "I miss her cooking."

"Does she ever send you care packages?" Mira asked.

"Sometimes, but it's hard to keep fried food fresh. They're never quite the same when they arrive by owl post."

The conversation drifted to holiday foods and family traditions, each of them sharing small pieces of home. Mira talked about her mother's masala chai and the way her house always smelled like cumin and coriander when her parents cooked together. Finn described his father's attempts at traditional Scottish recipes, which were usually enthusiastic but occasionally disastrous. Elliot spoke fondly of kolače, sweet pastries their Czech grandmother made every Christmas.

It was comfortable, warm—the kind of conversation that felt like wrapping yourself in a favorite blanket.

Later, as they were finishing dessert, Mira remembered her own news.

"I made a new friend today," she said, slightly surprised by her own announcement. "Or, well, the beginning of a friend. In Charms class."

"Oh?" Elliot raised an eyebrow. "Do tell."

"Her name is Priya. She's in Slytherin, takes Arithmancy. We got paired for the Gemino Charm practice, and it turned out we work really well together. We're going to study together sometime."

"That's great!" Finn said warmly. "I don't think I know her. What's she like?"

"Smart. Thoughtful. She gives really good feedback without being mean about it. And she's interested in the theoretical overlap between Ancient Runes and Arithmancy, which is something I've been curious about but haven't had anyone to discuss it with."

"Look at you, expanding your social circle," Zara teased gently. "I'm proud of you."

"It's not that big a deal," Mira protested, but she was smiling. "I just... I realized I've been so focused on you three that I haven't really gotten to know other people in our year. And there are probably lots of interesting people I've been missing out on."

"There definitely are," Elliot agreed. "Though for the record, we're still your favorite."

"Obviously," Mira laughed. "You're irreplaceable. But it's nice to have more than one friend group. Makes Hogwarts feel bigger and smaller at the same time, if that makes sense."

"It does," Finn said quietly. He glanced at Zara again, and that look passed between them once more—something meaningful that the others weren't quite part of. "It's good to know you have people who understand different parts of you."

Mira nodded, even though she wasn't entirely sure what Finn meant. But she trusted that whatever he and Zara had talked about, it was important. And that when Finn was ready, he'd share it with the rest of them.

For now, it was enough to sit here together, the four of them plus the growing constellation of other connections—Priya and Oliver and all the other students they were slowly getting to know better. Hogwarts was full of potential friendships, potential understanding.

You just had to be willing to take chances.

---

Two days later, Mira was in the library at her usual table when Priya appeared with an armful of books.

"Is this seat taken?" she asked, gesturing to the chair across from Mira.

"All yours," Mira said, clearing some space on the table. "What've you got there?"

"Arithmancy texts, mostly. And one very dusty volume on proto-runic symbols that I found in the back of the Restricted Section." Priya set down her books with a soft thud. "I got special permission from Professor Babbling to check it out. Told her it was for independent research."

"Did you mention you were working with me?"

"I did, actually. She seemed pleased. Said you'd be a good research partner." Priya opened the oldest book carefully, its spine cracking softly. "So, where should we start?"

They worked for nearly two hours, comparing symbol sets and numerical associations, finding patterns and connections that neither of them had seen on their own. It was intellectually stimulating in a way that felt almost luxurious—pure curiosity-driven learning with no exam pressure or assignment deadline.

At one point, Madame Oakwood stopped by their table. "Making good progress, I see. It's nice to see students collaborating across houses on independent research."

After she'd moved on, Priya said quietly, "Does it ever bother you? The house divisions?"

Mira considered the question. "Sometimes. I mean, my closest friends are from three different houses, so I don't think house boundaries are absolute. But there's definitely this expectation that you'll mainly socialize with your own house. It can feel limiting."

"Agreed." Priya tapped her quill against her parchment thoughtfully. "I like Slytherin, and I like most of my housemates. But sometimes I feel like people make assumptions about me because of the house I'm in. Like they think I'm going to be ambitious to the point of ruthlessness, or that I don't care about things like academic integrity or fairness."

"That's unfair," Mira said. "Every house has all kinds of people. Slytherin doesn't have a monopoly on ambition any more than Ravenclaw has a monopoly on intelligence."

"Exactly!" Priya looked relieved to hear someone else articulate it. "One of your friends is in Slytherin, right? I've seen you eating lunch with them sometimes."

"Elliot. They're amazing—really strategic and clever, but also one of the most loyal people I know. They get the same thing sometimes, people making assumptions about them because of the green robes."

"Elliot Novak?" Priya's eyes lit up with recognition. "They're in my Potions class. They're incredible—probably the best in our year. And they're always really helpful when people are struggling with their brews."

"That sounds like Elliot," Mira said fondly.

They returned to their research, but the brief personal conversation had shifted something. The study session felt less like two acquaintances working in proximity and more like the beginning of an actual friendship.

When Priya finally gathered her books to leave, she hesitated.

"Thank you," she said. "For this. I've been feeling a bit... isolated, I guess. It's nice to connect with someone over shared interests."

"Thank you too," Mira replied sincerely. "I was just thinking earlier this week that I needed to branch out more. I'm really glad we got paired in Charms."

"Me too." Priya smiled—shy but genuine. "Same time next week?"

"Definitely."

As Mira watched Priya leave, she thought about the different kinds of connection that existed. The deep, intimate friendship she had with Finn, Zara, and Elliot. The new, tentative connection forming with Priya. The way Finn trusted Zara with something significant, something he wasn't ready to share with everyone.

All of it was valuable. All of it mattered.

And all of it required the same thing: being willing to take a chance. Being willing to be vulnerable, to reach out, to say "I'd like to get to know you better" and risk the possibility of rejection.

It was scary. But as Mira packed up her things and headed to dinner, she decided it was worth it.

The best things usually were.
