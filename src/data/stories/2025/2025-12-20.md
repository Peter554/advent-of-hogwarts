Finn woke on December 20th with a knot of anxiety sitting precisely where his breakfast should be. Today was the day. He'd promised Zara—promised himself—that he would do it. Ask Oliver to the Yule Ball.

The actual ball was tomorrow night. Which meant he was officially running out of time.

He lay in bed for a moment, staring at the ceiling of his dormitory, listening to his roommates beginning to stir. The morning light filtering through the round windows had that particular quality of midwinter—thin and pale, as if the sun itself was still waking up.

He could do this. He'd faced down a Venomous Tentacula just days ago. Asking a boy to a dance should be less terrifying than carnivorous plants.

Except it wasn't. Not even close.

---

After a breakfast he barely tasted, Finn found himself in the library, ostensibly studying for Herbology but actually rearranging the same three textbooks over and over while his heart tried to climb out of his throat.

Oliver was usually here on Saturday mornings. Oliver liked the library. Oliver was reliable and kind and had the sort of smile that made Finn forget how to form complete sentences.

Oliver was also, according to every signal Finn had tried desperately to interpret over the past few weeks, possibly interested. Maybe. Hopefully.

"You're going to wear a hole in that page," Zara's voice said from behind him.

Finn jumped, nearly knocking over his inkwell. Zara slid into the seat across from him, already dressed in her Quidditch practice gear but clearly in no hurry to get to the pitch.

"Moral support," she said before he could ask. "I told the team I'd be late. This is more important."

"It's not—"

"Don't." Zara's expression was fierce and gentle at the same time. "It is important. You're important. And you're going to do this."

"What if he says no?"

"Then he's an idiot and doesn't deserve you. But he's not going to say no." Zara pulled out a book—Quidditch Through the Ages, which she'd probably read a dozen times—and opened it to a random page. "I'm just a student studying over here. You do your thing. I'm here if you need me."

Finn felt a rush of gratitude so strong it made his eyes sting. "Thank you."

"Always."

They sat in companionable silence for maybe ten minutes—Zara actually reading, Finn pretending to read—before Oliver arrived.

He came in through the main entrance, his bag slung over one shoulder, his sandy hair still damp from what was probably a morning shower. He was in his Hufflepuff robes, yellow and black, and when he spotted Finn his face lit up with that smile.

Finn's brain immediately stopped working.

Oliver headed over, weaving between tables. "Morning, Finn. Zara."

"Morning," Zara said without looking up, the picture of casual disinterest.

"Hi," Finn managed. His voice came out higher than normal. He cleared his throat. "Morning. Hi."

"You okay?" Oliver asked, setting his bag down. "You look a bit..."

"Nervous. I mean—I'm fine. Good. Just... can I talk to you about something?"

Oliver's expression shifted to concern. "Of course. Is everything alright?"

"Yes. No. I mean, everything's fine, I just—" Finn took a breath. This was it. He could do this. "The Yule Ball. Tomorrow. Are you... do you have plans?"

Oliver blinked. "Plans?"

"Like, are you going with anyone?"

There was a pause that felt roughly seven years long.

Then Oliver smiled—soft and wondering and impossibly warm. "I was hoping you'd ask."

Finn's brain, which had been running on pure panic, ground to a complete halt. "You were?"

"I've been trying to work up the courage for weeks." Oliver laughed, running a hand through his damp hair. "I kept talking myself out of it. Figured you probably had someone else in mind."

"Me? No. Just—just you." Finn could feel heat creeping up his neck. "So, is that... would you want to go? With me?"

"Yes." Oliver was grinning now, properly grinning, and it was the most beautiful thing Finn had ever seen. "Absolutely yes."

"Okay," Finn breathed. "Good. Great. That's—yeah."

They stood there for a moment, both smiling like idiots, until Zara loudly cleared her throat.

"Congratulations, both of you. Very touching. Now if you'll excuse me, I have Quidditch practice to get to." She stood, gathering her things, but not before shooting Finn a wink that probably wasn't subtle at all.

After she left, Finn and Oliver sat down together, and somehow studying became talking became hours passing in what felt like minutes. They discussed everything—what they'd wear, whether they actually knew how to dance (neither did), whether the enchanted ceiling would show stars or snow or something else entirely.

And underneath all the practical questions was something else: the giddy, electric feeling of being seen. Of taking a chance and having it work out. Of not having to hide.

By the time they parted ways for lunch, Finn was floating.

---

That afternoon, Mira's note arrived via owl: _Ravenclaw common room, five o'clock. Preparation party. Bring whatever you want to wear tomorrow. —M_

Finn showed up at five on the dot, his dress robes draped carefully over his arm, his heart still doing little flips every time he remembered Oliver saying yes.

Elliot and Zara were already there, sprawled on the blue and bronze furniture. The Ravenclaw common room was one of Finn's favorite places in the castle—circular and airy, with windows overlooking the mountains and that strange, beautiful quality of light that seemed unique to towers.

"He said yes," Finn announced as he entered.

Zara whooped. "I know. I was there, remember?"

"Of course he said yes," Elliot added, looking up from their sketchbook. "We could have told you that weeks ago."

Mira emerged from her dormitory carrying what looked like an entire wardrobe draped over both arms. "I'm so happy for you, Finn. I knew Oliver was interested. He watches you in Herbology all the time."

"He does?"

"Constantly." Mira dumped her armload onto a sofa. "Now. Let's talk strategy."

What followed was two hours of organized chaos.

Mira had brought down not just her own ball outfit but also several alternatives she'd borrowed from roommates, determined to find the perfect option for everyone. Zara had arrived with a bag full of hair supplies that she set out with the precision of a general preparing for battle. And Elliot sat very quietly in an armchair, their dress robes draped across their lap, looking like they might bolt at any moment.

"Elliot," Mira said gently, noticing their tension. "What's wrong?"

"These are what my mother sent." Elliot gestured at the robes—they were beautiful, expensive-looking, but unquestionably masculine in cut. Formal wizarding dress robes in dark green with silver embroidery. "She just assumed..."

"That you'd want something traditionally male," Zara finished. "Because she doesn't know yet."

"Right." Elliot's hands twisted in their lap. "And they're nice. They're really nice. But they feel..."

"Wrong?" Mira asked softly.

"Not wrong exactly. Just not... me." Elliot looked up, and their grey eyes were vulnerable. "I don't want to wear a dress. That's not right either. But this feels too..."

"Gendered," Finn offered.

"Yes." Elliot let out a breath. "I want to look elegant. Put-together. But I want to look like myself. And I don't know what that means in formal wear."

Mira sat down beside them, her mind already working. "What if we modified them? The robes. They're beautiful quality—your mother has excellent taste—but what if we made them more... androgynous?"

"How?"

"Well, the cut is very structured. Very traditional masculine lines. But what if we took in the shoulders slightly, added some drape to the silhouette? Kept the elegance but softened the masculine coding?" Mira was warming to the idea now, that particular gleam in her eye that appeared when she was solving a problem. "And the embroidery is silver, which is perfect—silver is neutral, works for anyone. We could add a different closure at the neck, something that reads as elegant rather than specifically masculine..."

"I have a brooch," Zara interrupted, digging through her bag. "My mother gave it to me. Silver knotwork. Very striking. You could use that instead of traditional clasps."

Elliot was staring at both of them. "You'd help me alter them?"

"Of course," Mira said, as if this was the most obvious thing in the world. "You should feel comfortable tomorrow. More than comfortable—you should feel like yourself."

"I know some tailoring charms," Finn added. "My dad uses them in his wandmaking work. I could help with the modifications."

For a moment, Elliot didn't speak. Then they nodded, something loosening in their shoulders. "Okay. Yes. Let's try it."

They spent the next hour working on Elliot's robes—Finn and Mira carefully altering the cut with a combination of tailoring charms and actual needle and thread (Mira insisted some things were better done by hand). They softened the shoulder line, added subtle drape to the torso, removed the aggressively masculine collar and replaced it with something simpler that would showcase Zara's brooch.

When Elliot finally tried them on again, the difference was remarkable. Still elegant. Still formal. But unmistakably them.

"Oh," Elliot breathed, looking at themselves in the mirror Mira had conjured. "Oh, that's—"

"Perfect," Zara declared. "You look amazing."

And they did. The robes moved differently now, less rigid, more flowing. The silver embroidery caught the light. The brooch at their throat—a complicated Celtic knot that could have come from either masculine or feminine tradition—added a focal point that drew the eye without demanding categorization.

"I look like myself," Elliot said wonderingly. "I actually look like myself."

"That's the whole point," Mira said, smiling.

Once Elliot's robes were sorted, they turned to everyone else's outfits. Finn's dress robes were simple—he'd borrowed them from his father, traditional Scottish formal wear in Hufflepuff colors. They fit well enough, though Mira insisted on taking in the waist slightly.

Mira herself had chosen a deep blue set of dress robes that complemented her Ravenclaw colors, simple and elegant with silver embroidery at the hems. Nothing flashy, nothing that would make her self-conscious. Perfect.

Zara's outfit was the most dramatic—deep crimson that practically glowed against her dark skin, with gold accents that caught the light when she moved. She'd borrowed it from an older cousin who'd worn it to a wedding, and it fit her like it had been made for her specifically.

"You're going to break hearts," Finn told her.

"That's the plan," Zara said with a grin. "I'm going solo, but I'm going in style."

With the clothing sorted, Zara pulled out her hair supplies and pointed at the chair she'd positioned by the window for the best light. "Alright. Who's first?"

"What are you going to do?" Mira asked nervously.

"Magic," Zara said. "Trust me."

Mira went first, settling into the chair while Zara studied her long black hair with the same intensity she brought to Quidditch strategy. "Your hair is beautiful," Zara said, running her fingers through it. "And you always wear it in a braid. What if tomorrow we did something different?"

"Like what?"

"You'll see. Close your eyes."

Mira closed her eyes, and Zara went to work. Finn watched, fascinated, as Zara's hands moved with practiced confidence—sectioning, twisting, pinning. She'd mentioned learning this from her older sister, and it showed. This was expertise born of hours of practice, of sisterly bonding, of care.

Twenty minutes later, Zara stepped back. "Okay. Look."

Mira opened her eyes and turned to the mirror. Her hair was still down—or mostly down—but Zara had created an intricate pattern of small braids that wove through the length, pulling some of it back from her face while leaving the rest to flow freely. It was elegant without being fussy, and it made Mira look older, more sophisticated.

"Zara," Mira breathed. "This is gorgeous."

"You're gorgeous," Zara corrected. "I just highlighted what was already there."

Elliot went next. Their hair was shorter, but Zara worked her magic anyway, using small braids and strategic pinning to create something that enhanced rather than feminized, something that read as artistic and intentional rather than traditionally gendered.

By the time she was done, Elliot looked like they'd stepped out of a Renaissance painting—elegant and androgynous and absolutely themselves.

"I don't have enough hair for anything fancy," Finn protested when Zara turned to him.

"You have plenty," Zara said, already pushing him into the chair. "Besides, it's not about quantity. It's about intention."

She worked more quickly with Finn—his reddish-brown hair was shorter and less cooperative—but when she finished, she'd managed to tame the usual chaos into something that looked deliberately tousled rather than accidentally messy. She'd even convinced a few stubborn pieces to stay out of his eyes.

"There," she said with satisfaction. "You clean up nice, MacLeod."

Finn studied himself in the mirror. He looked... good. Older. More confident. Like someone who could ask a boy to a dance and have him say yes.

"Thank you," he said quietly.

"Anytime."

They spent the rest of the evening in the common room, trying on their complete outfits, practicing walking in their formal robes (which were longer and more elaborate than school robes and required some getting used to), and talking through the plan for tomorrow.

"We meet in the Entrance Hall at seven," Zara reiterated. "Before our dates or plans or anything else. The four of us together first."

"Pictures," Mira added. "Elliot, you'll have to show someone else how to work your camera since you'll be in them."

"Already thought of that," Elliot said. "I'll ask Colin Creevey. He's good with cameras and he owes me a favor."

"And then we just... have fun?" Finn asked. "That's the plan?"

"That's the plan," Zara confirmed. "Dance, eat too much fancy food, enjoy the fact that we're young and alive and at Hogwarts during a Yule Ball. Nothing more complicated than that."

"Revolutionary," Elliot said dryly.

As the evening wound down and they began gathering their things to return to their respective dormitories, Finn felt something settle in his chest. This was what the whole month had been building toward—not just the ball itself, but this moment. The four of them, helping each other prepare, celebrating each other's courage to show up as themselves.

Elliot caught his eye as they were packing up their modified robes. "Thank you. For helping with these. For understanding why it mattered."

"Of course," Finn said. "We all want you to feel comfortable."

"It's more than comfortable." Elliot's voice was soft. "It's being seen. Really seen. That's what you all do for me. For each other."

"That's what friends do," Mira said, joining them. "We see each other. All the parts, including the ones that are scary to show."

"And then we help braid each other's hair and alter each other's robes and make sure we all look absolutely fantastic," Zara added with a grin.

They left the common room together, separating in the corridor outside. Finn and Zara headed down together toward their respective dormitories, while Mira stayed in Ravenclaw Tower and Elliot descended toward the dungeons.

"Nervous about tomorrow?" Zara asked as they walked.

"Terrified," Finn admitted. "But in a good way? I think?"

"That's the right kind of nervous," Zara said. "That's the 'something wonderful is about to happen' nervous."

"What about you? No regrets about going solo?"

"None." Zara's voice was confident. "I thought about asking someone—there are a few people who would probably say yes. But honestly? I want to be able to dance with everyone. To not be tied to one person all night. To just... be free and happy and present."

"That sounds perfect for you."

"It is." She bumped his shoulder affectionately. "We're all going to have exactly the night we need. I can feel it."

In his dormitory, Finn carefully hung his altered dress robes and stared at them for a long moment. Tomorrow night, he'd wear them. He'd meet his friends in the Entrance Hall. He'd walk into the Great Hall with Oliver.

He'd show up as himself—nervous and excited and unmistakably gay, even though most people probably wouldn't notice or care. But he'd know. His friends would know. Oliver would know.

And that was enough. That was everything.

Across the castle, in three other dormitories, three other students were looking at their own carefully prepared outfits and feeling the same nervous anticipation. The same gratitude for friends who saw them clearly and helped them be brave.

Tomorrow, the Yule Ball. Tomorrow, a celebration of winter and magic and the courage it took to be yourself in a room full of people.

But tonight—tonight was its own kind of magic. The quiet preparation. The careful braiding and altering and planning. The intimacy of helping each other become who they wanted to be.

In her journal, Mira added a new entry:

_December 20th: Tomorrow is the Yule Ball. Tonight we prepared—not just our clothes and hair, but ourselves. We helped Elliot modify their robes so they could look elegant and androgynous. We helped Finn look confident for his date with Oliver. We helped each other practice being the people we want to be._

_Zara said something tonight that stuck with me: "We see each other." That's what this month has been about. The frost patterns, the sketchbook, the solstice traditions—all of it has been about seeing and being seen. About having the courage to show people who you really are, and having friends brave enough to look without flinching._

_Tomorrow we'll dance and celebrate. But tonight we were vulnerable and careful and gentle with each other. And that's its own kind of celebration._

_One more day until the ball. But I already feel like I'm exactly where I'm supposed to be._

Outside, the last light faded from the winter sky. Inside Hogwarts, students slept and dreamed of tomorrow's celebration. And somewhere in the space between sleep and waking, magic gathered—not the kind cast with wands, but the kind woven from friendship and acceptance and the profound gift of being known.

The Yule Ball would be beautiful. But the preparation—the braiding and altering and supporting—that was beautiful too.

Sometimes the anticipation was just as magical as the event itself.

Sometimes getting ready together was the whole point.
