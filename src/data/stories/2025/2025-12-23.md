The unused classroom on the third floor had become theirs over the course of December. Not officially—no professor had granted them permission, no house points were awarded for its discovery—but in the way that spaces sometimes choose their occupants. The room had welcomed them during their solstice celebration, had held their conversation after the Yule Ball announcement, and now, on December 23rd, it would witness their private gift exchange.

Mira arrived first, carrying a bag that clinked softly with each step. Afternoon light streamed through the tall windows, catching dust motes and turning them golden. Someone—probably Finn—had dragged in cushions from somewhere, and they were piled in the corner along with a few blankets. The room smelled faintly of the cinnamon candles Elliot had conjured last week, the scent now baked into the stone walls.

She set her bag down carefully and pulled out the four wrapped packages she'd been hiding in her trunk for weeks. The paper was simple brown parchment tied with string—nothing fancy, but somehow that felt right. These weren't gifts meant to impress. They were meant to matter.

"Starting without us?" Zara's voice came from the doorway. She entered carrying her own collection of packages, these wrapped in scarlet paper that matched her Gryffindor scarf. Her box braids were tied up today, and she wore the comfortable jumper she usually reserved for Hogsmeade weekends.

"Just setting up," Mira said. "Are we doing a proper circle, or—"

"Proper circle," Finn declared, squeezing past Zara with his arms full of gifts and what appeared to be a tin of biscuits. "I spent too much time in the kitchens this morning getting these to not do this properly."

Elliot appeared last, moving quietly as always, their grey eyes taking in the scene with that particular softness they only showed around the three of them. They carried a flat portfolio case under one arm and a smaller wrapped box in their hand.

"Is that—" Finn started.

"We agreed on modest gifts," Elliot said quickly, a faint flush coloring their cheeks. "These are modest."

"I commissioned a wandmaker," Zara admitted. "So I'm not sure I have room to judge."

"You what?" Mira blinked.

"We'll get to it!" Zara said, grinning. "Come on, circle. Let's do this before I explode from anticipation."

They arranged themselves on the cushions in their now-familiar formation: Mira with her back to the window, Finn to her left, Zara across from her, Elliot to her right. The gifts were piled in the center, a colorful jumble of wrapping and ribbon and anticipation.

For a moment, no one spoke. Outside, snow drifted past the window. Somewhere in the castle, students were packing for the holidays or playing wizard chess or writing last-minute letters home. But here, in this room, four friends sat together in the particular silence that comes before something important.

"I can't believe term's almost over," Finn said finally. "Feels like September was about five minutes ago."

"And five years ago," Mira added. "Is that possible? For time to feel both ways at once?"

"Definitely possible," Elliot said. "It's been a long December."

"A good one though," Zara said firmly. "A really good one."

They all knew what she meant. The frost message on December first, teaching them to seek wonder in small things. The Niffler and the library and the dueling club. Finn's broken Remembrall and the lesson about things not needing to be perfect to be valuable. Elliot coming out as non-binary, and the group's fierce acceptance. The Yule Ball, where they'd all shown up as themselves. The scavenger hunt just yesterday, proving how well they worked together.

Twenty-three days of friendship, growth, and the quiet magic of being truly known.

"Should we—" Finn gestured at the pile of gifts.

"Alphabetically?" Elliot suggested. "By last name?"

"Random draw," Zara countered. "More exciting."

"Or," Mira said slowly, "we could each give our gifts at the same time. So it's not just one person being watched while everyone stares."

The relief on Elliot's face was immediate. "Yes. That. Let's do that."

They rearranged the gifts, sorting out who'd brought what for whom. Mira's hands trembled slightly as she distributed her packages—one for each friend, the books she'd chosen with such care. She'd spent hours in Hogsmeade's Tomes and Scrolls, even longer in the Hogwarts library, finding exactly the right volumes.

"On three?" Finn suggested, holding his own offerings—small pressed flower bookmarks, each one carefully preserved between thin panes of charmed glass.

"On three," Zara agreed.

"One," Elliot said.

"Two," Mira added.

"Three," they said together, and began unwrapping.

Mira opened Finn's gift first because it was smallest: a bookmark of pressed flowers arranged in a delicate pattern. The card attached read: *Dittany for healing, moonflower for dreams, and Scottish thistle for home. Because you're healing from perfectionism, you dream bigger than anyone I know, and you make this place feel like home. —F*

Her eyes stung immediately.

Zara's gift was a wand holder—dark wood carved with intricate symbols. Mira recognized some from Ancient Runes: Ansuz for wisdom, Perthro for mystery, and others she'd have to research. The craftsmanship was extraordinary, each line precise and purposeful.

"Zara, this is—"

"I described you to the wandmaker," Zara said, already unwrapping her own gifts. "Told her about your love of learning and questions. She said those symbols chose themselves."

Elliot's gift made Mira's breath catch. It was a sketch—not formal or polished, but raw and real. Mira at the window on December first, reaching toward the frost patterns, her face lit with wonder and curiosity. Elliot had captured something essential: not just how she looked, but who she was in that moment.

"I don't know what to say," Mira whispered.

"You don't have to say anything," Elliot replied softly. They were looking at their own lap now, at Mira's gift to them: a slim volume titled *The Courage to Be: Essays on Identity and Self.*

"It's essays by wizards and witches who came out or transitioned or just figured out who they were," Mira explained quickly. "I found it in a bookshop in Diagon Alley last summer. One of the authors is non-binary, and they write about choosing your own name, and I thought—I hoped—" She stopped, suddenly uncertain.

Elliot looked up, their grey eyes bright. "It's perfect," they said, voice thick. "Genuinely perfect. Thank you."

Finn was making small sounds of delight as he examined his gifts: from Mira, a book on magical herbology across cultures; from Zara, a wand holder carved with plant symbols; from Elliot, a sketch of Finn in the greenhouse, dirt on his cheek, hands gentle around a rare seedling, his expression one of complete contentment.

"This is how you see me?" Finn asked, staring at the sketch.

"That's who you are," Elliot corrected. "I just drew it."

Zara had gone quiet, holding her four gifts with uncharacteristic stillness. Mira had given her a book about the history of magical sports across cultures—including chapters on West African flying traditions. Finn's bookmark for her featured fire orchid and rowan berries: *For courage and protection. Because you're the bravest person I know, and you protect all of us even when we don't realize we need it.* Elliot had drawn her mid-flight during the snowy Quidditch match, determination and joy mixed on her face, box braids streaming behind her.

And the wand holder Zara had commissioned for herself—because she couldn't resist seeing what the wandmaker would suggest—featured symbols of strength, leadership, and family.

"I didn't know I could feel this much," Zara said finally, wiping at her eyes. "This is ridiculous. They're just gifts."

"They're not just gifts," Mira said. "They're us. Seeing each other."

Elliot was turning their book over in their hands, running fingers along the spine. "I've never—" they started, then stopped. Started again. "I've spent a lot of my life feeling like I had to be what other people expected. What my mum expected, what Slytherin expected, what—just, expected. These gifts..." They gestured at the pile of paper and treasures. "You all see me. The real me. And you made gifts based on that person, not the person you think I should be."

"Same," Finn said quietly. "The Remembrall broke, and I was so scared that somehow I'd broken something about my family. But these gifts—" he touched his bookmark, his book, his sketch, his wand holder, "—they're about who I actually am. What I actually love. Not what I'm supposed to be."

"When I was planning what to give you all," Zara said, "the wandmaker asked me to describe each of you. And I realized I could. I know you. I know what matters to you, what scares you, what makes you happy. And you know the same about me. When did that happen?"

"December," Mira said. "Or maybe before, but December is when we really paid attention."

"Seek wonder in small things," Finn quoted. "That's what the frost said."

"And we did," Elliot added. "We sought each other."

They sat in silence for a moment, surrounded by wrapping paper and books and sketches and pressed flowers. The afternoon light was fading now, the room growing dimmer, but none of them moved to light a lamp.

"I got you books," Mira said, "because books are how I understand the world. How I make sense of things. And I wanted to share that with you—show you how I see each of you through the stories and knowledge I think you'd love."

"I made bookmarks," Finn said, "because plants are my language. Everything important can be said with the right flower, the right leaf. These were me trying to tell you who you are to me."

"The wand holders," Zara explained, "are because our wands are the most important tools we have. They're extensions of ourselves. I wanted you to have something that honored that—that said your magic matters, you matter, and the person you are is worth protecting and celebrating."

Elliot took a shaky breath. "The sketches are the scariest thing I've ever given anyone. Because they're not just drawings—they're how I see you. And giving someone that, showing them how you carry them in your head... that's terrifying. But also, after everything this month, after you all accepted me, I thought—I thought maybe it was time to be that vulnerable."

"I'm glad you were," Mira said.

"We all were," Finn agreed. "Vulnerable, I mean. These gifts are all vulnerable in different ways."

"That's what makes them perfect," Zara said. "We're giving each other the truth. The real thing, not the safe thing."

More silence. Comfortable this time, full instead of empty.

"Can I tell you all something?" Finn asked, voice careful. "I asked Oliver to the ball because you all made me brave enough. Zara, you said I should. Elliot, you showed me it was okay to be who I am. Mira, you made me feel like being uncertain was part of learning, not a weakness." He looked around at them. "I don't think I could have done that without this. Without us."

"I almost didn't go to the ball at all," Mira admitted. "Too much social anxiety, too much worry about looking stupid or doing the wrong thing. But the Grey Lady told me about regret, about missing moments because she was too focused on achievement. And you three kept showing me that showing up matters more than being perfect."

"I wore what I wore to the ball—that outfit that felt actually like me, not like boy-clothes or girl-clothes but like Elliot-clothes—because we'd already talked about identity," Elliot said. "Because you made it safe to experiment, to try, to be uncertain. I don't think I could have stood in front of the whole school dressed like that without knowing you three had my back."

"And I learned that I don't have to do everything alone," Zara said. "That asking for help isn't weakness, that letting people in doesn't make me less strong. The scavenger hunt proved that, but so did everything else this month. Every time one of you challenged me or supported me or just—saw me."

Finn reached into his tin and pulled out the biscuits—ginger and chocolate chip, still warm from the kitchens. They passed the tin around, eating in companionable silence as the room grew darker around them.

"I don't want December to end," Mira said finally.

"It won't," Elliot countered. "Not really. We'll still be us in January."

"And February," Finn added.

"And every month after," Zara finished. "This isn't just a December thing. We're not just December friends."

"December friends," Mira repeated, smiling. "That would be a terrible band name."

"The worst," Elliot agreed.

"We could be the Wonder Seekers," Finn suggested. "Because of the frost message."

"Absolutely not," Zara said. "We're not naming ourselves. That's how you end up with something embarrassing that sticks forever."

"Too late," Elliot said. "We're the Wonder Seekers now. I'm getting t-shirts made."

"You are not—"

"With our faces on them—"

"Elliot, I swear—"

They dissolved into laughter, the sound echoing off the stone walls. And somehow that felt right too. Moving from tears to laughter, from vulnerability to joy, the way real friendship does.

Eventually, they gathered their gifts carefully, wrapping them back up to protect them on the journey to their respective dormitories. But they moved slowly, reluctant to let the moment end.

"Same time tomorrow?" Finn asked. "For Christmas Eve?"

"Obviously," Zara said. "We're watching the sunset from the Astronomy Tower. Already planned it."

"You planned something without telling us?" Mira asked.

"I'm full of surprises."

"You really aren't," Elliot said fondly.

At the door, they paused. Some unspoken agreement made them all stop, turn back to look at the room that had held them so many times this month.

"Thank you," Mira said quietly. "For the gifts. For December. For everything."

"Thank you," Finn echoed.

"This was—" Zara started, then stopped, shaking her head. "I don't have words."

"We don't need them," Elliot said. "We know."

And they did.

---

Later that night, in Ravenclaw Tower, Mira added to her December journal:

*December 23rd: We exchanged gifts today. Not the expensive kind or the impressive kind, but the kind that matter—the kind that say "I see you, I know you, I celebrate who you are." Books and sketches and flowers and carved wood. Each gift a reflection of both the giver and the receiver. Each one a small act of love.*

*Finn gave me flowers that represent who I am. Zara gave me a wand holder carved with symbols of wisdom and mystery. Elliot drew me at the window reaching for frost, caught the exact moment I felt wonder overcome worry.*

*We talked about how these gifts are acts of vulnerability—showing each other not just what we've bought or made, but how we see each other. The truth of our friendship, wrapped up and handed over.*

*We laughed and cried and ate biscuits in the fading afternoon light. We named ourselves the Wonder Seekers—ironically but also not. We promised that this isn't just December, that we'll carry this forward.*

*And I believe it. I believe that whatever comes next—exams and summers and fifth year and everything beyond—we'll face it together. Because these gifts aren't just presents. They're promises.*

*Twenty-three days into December, and I understand what the frost was trying to tell me: Wonder isn't just in magical phenomena or perfect essays. It's in being known. In knowing others. In the small, profound gift of friendship.*

Outside her window, snow continued to fall over Hogwarts. Christmas Eve was tomorrow, and then Christmas Day, and then the term would end and they'd all go home for the holidays.

But Mira wasn't afraid of that anymore. She had books inscribed with her friends' handwriting, a bookmark made from flowers that meant courage and dreams, a wand holder carved with symbols that represented who she was, and a sketch that showed how Elliot saw her—full of wonder and reaching for beauty.

She had the gifts. The real gifts.

And she had the givers.

In three other dormitories, three other students looked at their own treasures—at books and sketches and flowers and carved wood—and felt the same thing: gratitude, love, and the deep certainty that they'd found something rare.

Four people who saw each other clearly and chose each other anyway. Four people who'd spent December learning that the greatest magic wasn't in spells or potions, but in the courage to be vulnerable and the grace to accept vulnerability in return.

The gift of magic, it turned out, was the gift of being known.

And they'd given it to each other freely, wrapped in paper and string and truth.
