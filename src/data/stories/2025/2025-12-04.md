Elliot Novak had always been good at Potions. Not just good—exceptional. It was the one class where they never had to try, where their hands knew the precise angle to hold a stirring rod, where they could smell the exact moment a potion reached the right temperature. Their mother was a renowned potioneer, and everyone assumed the talent ran in the family like eye color or a crooked smile.

Which made it all the more humiliating that their cauldron currently contained what looked like dishwater.

"This doesn't make any sense," Elliot muttered, staring at the murky grey liquid. Around them, the Advanced Potions dungeon hummed with the usual sounds of fourth-year students working: the gentle bubble of simmering cauldrons, the scratch of knives on cutting boards, the occasional curse when someone added an ingredient too quickly.

Professor Slughorn's jovial voice carried across the room as he moved between workstations. "Remember, students, the Veritaserum Cordis is a subtle bit of magic! It's not about forcing honesty—anyone can brew truth potions, ho ho!—it's about _accepting_ honesty. The potion mirrors your relationship with your own truth. Deny what you know deep down, and it will never clear properly!"

Elliot resisted the urge to hex something. They'd followed the instructions perfectly. Moonstone, powdered, added counterclockwise. Essence of dittany, three drops, not two or four. Valerian root steeped for exactly four minutes. They'd done everything right.

And yet.

At the workstation beside them, Mira peered into her own cauldron with her usual intense focus. Her potion wasn't perfect—it had a faint cloudiness around the edges—but it was mostly transparent, showing the copper bottom of her cauldron. She'd probably get an Exceeds Expectations at least.

"How's yours looking?" Mira asked without glancing up, in that way she had of being aware of everything even when she seemed absorbed in her work.

"Like pond scum," Elliot said flatly.

Now Mira did look over, pushing her reading glasses up her nose. "That bad?"

"See for yourself."

Mira leaned over to peer into Elliot's cauldron, and her eyebrows rose. "Oh. That's... that is quite murky."

"Thank you for that insightful observation."

"Did you follow the—"

"Yes, I followed the instructions. To the letter. Twice." Elliot grabbed their stirring rod and gave the potion an irritated stir. It swirled but didn't clear. "I don't understand what I'm doing wrong."

Professor Slughorn materialized behind them with his usual uncanny timing. "Ah! Young Novak! Having some difficulty, are we?"

Elliot forced their expression into something politely neutral. "The potion won't clarify, sir. I've checked my measurements three times."

"Hmm, yes, I see." Slughorn bent to examine the cauldron, his large frame blocking the light from the nearest candle. "Technique seems sound. Ingredients prepared correctly. Color and consistency suggest you've done everything right up to the point of refinement."

"So why won't it clear?"

Slughorn straightened, and there was something gentle in his usually bombastic manner. "My dear student, the Veritaserum Cordis is called the Potion of Reflection for a reason. It doesn't fail because of poor technique. It fails because..." He paused, choosing his words carefully. "Well. Perhaps you might take a few moments to think about what truth you might be avoiding. Hmm? No rush. We have another twenty minutes of class."

He patted Elliot's shoulder—a gesture that was probably meant to be encouraging but felt patronizing—and moved on to the next student.

Elliot stared into their murky potion and felt something hot and uncomfortable twist in their chest.

---

By the end of class, Mira's potion had cleared completely. So had most of the others', though a few students still had faint cloudiness. Elliot's remained stubbornly opaque, and they had to pour it down the disposal drain while Professor Slughorn made sympathetic noises about "these things happen" and "try again next lesson."

They left the dungeon in silence, Mira trailing slightly behind in that way she did when she wanted to give someone space but didn't want to leave them alone. The castle's corridors were decorated for December now—garlands of holly and enchanted snow that drifted but never accumulated—but Elliot barely noticed.

"You want to talk about it?" Mira asked as they climbed the stairs from the dungeons.

"Nothing to talk about," Elliot said. "I'll figure it out for next class."

"Elliot—"

"I said I'll figure it out."

Mira was quiet for a moment, and Elliot felt a stab of guilt for snapping. But before they could apologize, Mira spoke again, her voice thoughtful rather than hurt.

"You know, when I was doing the potion, I kept thinking about my Ancient Runes exam. How I need to get an Outstanding. How anything less would be... I don't know. Failure, I guess."

Elliot glanced at her. "That's why your potion was cloudy at first?"

"Mmm. And then I thought about the frost. From the first of December. About what it said. Seeking wonder in small things. And I thought... maybe I don't need to be perfect. Maybe good enough is actually enough." She shrugged. "That's when my potion cleared."

They walked in silence for a few more steps. Then: "Are you suggesting my potion won't clear until I have some kind of emotional revelation?"

"I'm not suggesting anything," Mira said mildly. "Just telling you about my experience. You'll figure out yours."

They reached the entrance hall, where students were streaming toward the Great Hall for lunch. Elliot could see Finn and Zara at one of the long tables, waving them over. The thought of food made their stomach turn.

"I'm going to the library," Elliot said abruptly.

Mira didn't argue. "Want company?"

"No. Thanks," Elliot added, softer. "I just need to think."

"Okay. Come find us if you change your mind."

Elliot nodded and headed in the opposite direction, their footsteps echoing in the stone corridor. Behind them, they could hear Mira explaining to the others, and Finn's concerned "Is Elliot alright?" They didn't wait to hear the answer.

---

The library was quieter than usual, most students prioritizing lunch over study. Elliot found an empty table in the back corner and pulled out their Potions textbook, flipping to the chapter on truth and revelation potions.

_The Veritaserum Cordis differs from traditional truth potions in that it requires no unwilling subject. Instead, it serves as a mirror, reflecting the brewer's relationship with their own inner truth. A potion that remains unclear indicates resistance, denial, or fear of acknowledgment. Only when the brewer accepts what they already know—but perhaps wish they didn't—will the potion achieve perfect transparency._

Elliot read the passage three times, each time feeling that uncomfortable twisting sensation grow stronger.

What truth was they avoiding?

They knew what people expected from them. Their mother was Lucia Novak, whose potions had won international awards, whose breakthrough work on healing draughts had saved countless lives. When people learned Elliot was her child, they always lit up with recognition. "Oh! You must be so talented!" "You'll follow in her footsteps, obviously!" "What an incredible legacy to inherit!"

Elliot had never bothered to correct them. It was easier to nod and smile and let people believe what they wanted. And anyway, they _were_ good at Potions. They enjoyed the precision of it, the way everything had rules and order. It made sense in a way the rest of the world often didn't.

But did they want to _be_ a potioneer?

The question sat in their mind like a stone.

They'd never really thought about it. The path had always seemed laid out: do well at Hogwarts, apprentice with someone prestigious (Mother had connections, of course), eventually take a position at a research institution or open their own laboratory. It was what everyone expected. What their mother expected, though she'd never said it explicitly. She didn't have to. The pride in her eyes when Elliot got top marks in Potions said it clearly enough.

But what did Elliot actually want?

They realized they didn't know. They liked drawing, but that was just a hobby, right? They were good at Transfiguration too, and they found chess interesting, and they'd been thinking lately about curse-breaking because it seemed like a puzzle with real-world stakes...

But none of that was potion-making. None of that was the path everyone assumed they'd take.

"Oh," Elliot said aloud, very quietly, to the empty library corner. "Oh, no."

Because suddenly they understood. The potion hadn't failed because of technique. It had failed because Elliot was terrified—absolutely terrified—of disappointing their mother. Of not being the child she'd imagined. Of admitting that maybe they didn't want what everyone assumed they wanted.

Of admitting they didn't know what they wanted at all.

The realization felt like missing a step in the dark. Their hands were shaking slightly, and there was a pressure behind their eyes that they absolutely refused to acknowledge. They were in the library. They were fine. Everything was fine.

Except it wasn't, was it? Because they'd spent four years letting everyone believe they were going to be a potioneer like their mother, and they'd never once stopped to ask themselves if that's what they actually wanted. And now they were fourteen and taking Advanced Potions and people were starting to talk about career paths and apprenticeships, and Elliot had no idea what to say because the truth was—

The truth was they were scared. Scared of disappointing people. Scared of not living up to expectations. Scared of not knowing who they were supposed to be if they weren't Lucia Novak's talented child following perfectly in her footsteps.

Elliot put their head down on their arms and let themselves sit with that fear for a moment. The library was warm and quiet, and nobody could see them from this angle, and somehow that made it possible to just... feel it. The weight of expectations they'd never asked for. The uncertainty of not having answers. The fear of change.

And underneath all of it, a tiny, fragile thought: _Maybe it's okay not to know. Maybe it's okay to be uncertain. Maybe I don't have to have everything figured out right now._

They weren't sure how long they sat there. Long enough that lunch must have ended, because they could hear the sounds of students moving through the corridors again, heading to afternoon classes. Elliot straightened slowly, wiping their eyes—they absolutely hadn't been crying, just tired—and looked at their Potions textbook.

_Only when the brewer accepts what they already know—but perhaps wish they didn't—will the potion achieve perfect transparency._

"Alright," Elliot said softly. "I accept it. I don't know what I want. I'm scared of disappointing my mother. I might not want to be a potioneer. I might want something else entirely, and I have no idea what. And that's..." They took a breath. "That's where I am right now. And it's terrifying, but it's true."

Saying it aloud didn't make the fear disappear. But somehow, it made it feel more manageable. Like something they could work with instead of something crushing them.

---

The next Potions class was two days later. Elliot arrived early, set up their workstation with their usual precision, and waited for Professor Slughorn to begin the lesson.

"Today," Slughorn announced cheerfully, "those of you who didn't achieve clarity with your Veritaserum Cordis will have another opportunity! And for those who succeeded—well, we'll be moving on to the Draught of Peace. Equally challenging, I assure you!"

Elliot began preparing their ingredients. Their hands moved through the familiar motions: moonstone, counterclockwise. Essence of dittany, three drops. Valerian root, four minutes precisely.

Mira, working beside them again, caught their eye. "You okay?"

"I think so," Elliot said. And then, because honesty seemed important right now: "I'm scared, but I think I'm okay."

"Scared of what?"

"Everything." Elliot gave a short laugh. "The future. My mother's disappointment. Not knowing who I'm supposed to be. Take your pick."

"For what it's worth," Mira said, adding crushed pearl to her own cauldron, "I don't think you have to know who you're supposed to be. I think you just have to know who you are right now. The rest will come."

"When did you get so wise?"

"I read it in a book," Mira said, deadpan, and Elliot laughed for real this time.

They turned back to their potion. The base was prepared correctly—they could tell by the color and the way the steam rose in gentle spirals. Now came the final stage: the moment of refinement, where the brewer had to hold the truth clearly in their mind while adding the final ingredient.

Elliot picked up the vial of unicorn hair—a single strand, silver-white and impossibly fine. They held it over the cauldron and took a breath.

_I don't know what I want to do with my life. I might not want to be a potioneer. I'm terrified of disappointing my mother. I'm scared of uncertainty. And right now, in this moment, that's okay. I don't have to have all the answers. I just have to be honest about where I am._

The unicorn hair touched the surface of the potion.

For a moment, nothing happened. Then the murky grey began to shift. Clouds of darkness swirled and dispersed. The color lightened, clarified, became translucent. Elliot stirred once, counterclockwise, and watched as the last wisps of cloudiness dissolved.

The potion was perfectly clear. They could see all the way to the bottom of their copper cauldron.

"Well done!" Professor Slughorn's voice made Elliot jump. He beamed down at the cauldron. "Excellent work, Novak! I knew you'd get there. Sometimes these things just require a bit of... personal reflection, hmm?"

"Yes, sir," Elliot said quietly.

After Slughorn moved on, Mira leaned over to look. "It's beautiful."

"It's terrifying," Elliot corrected. "Do you know what this means? I'm going to have to actually figure out what I want. And probably talk to my mother about it eventually."

"Eventually," Mira agreed. "But not today. Today you just made a perfect potion."

Elliot looked at the clear liquid in their cauldron—a mirror reflecting nothing back but their own face, distorted slightly by the curve of copper. They thought about their mother, about expectations and fear and uncertainty. They thought about the future, vast and unclear and full of possibilities they hadn't let themselves consider.

And they thought about the truth they'd finally acknowledged: that not knowing was okay. That uncertainty didn't mean failure. That they had time to figure things out.

"You're right," Elliot said. "Today I made a perfect potion."

"And?"

"And... maybe that's enough for now."

---

That evening, the four friends gathered in their usual empty classroom—the one near the Astronomy Tower that nobody else seemed to use. Finn had brought biscuits from the kitchen, and Zara was recounting a particularly dramatic Transfiguration mishap involving another student and an unfortunate incident with a guinea fowl.

Elliot sat slightly apart, sketching in their leather notebook. They were drawing Mira bent over a book, captured in that particular expression of intense focus she got when reading something fascinating. It was easier to think while their hands were busy.

"You've been quiet," Finn observed, offering the plate of biscuits. "Everything alright?"

Elliot took a biscuit, considering. Then: "I'm thinking about the future. Career stuff."

"Already?" Zara made a face. "We're fourteen."

"My family is very... goal-oriented," Elliot said carefully. "Everyone assumes I'll be a potioneer like my mother."

"But do you want to be?" Mira asked. She wasn't looking up from her own book, but she was clearly listening.

"I don't know," Elliot admitted. "And I think that's what I've been scared to admit. That I have no idea what I want."

Finn nodded thoughtfully. "That makes sense. The not knowing part, I mean. I think... maybe that's okay? We don't have to have everything figured out."

"Says the person who's known they wanted to work with magical plants since they were eight," Elliot pointed out.

"Fair," Finn laughed. "But I don't know _how_ I want to work with them. Greenhouse management? Magizoology focusing on plant-creature relationships? Teaching? Running my dad's business? There's a lot of not-knowing even in the knowing, if that makes sense."

"It does, actually."

Zara stretched, her joints cracking. "I thought I knew what I wanted—to play professional Quidditch. But after the match yesterday, I'm thinking maybe coaching? Or sports management? I don't know. The point is, we're allowed to change our minds. We're allowed to not have answers."

"When did everyone get so wise?" Elliot asked, echoing their earlier comment to Mira.

"We've always been wise," Zara said loftily. "You just weren't ready to hear it."

Elliot threw a biscuit at her, which she caught and ate with a grin.

Later, after Finn and Zara had left for their common rooms and Mira was packing up her books, Elliot caught her arm.

"Thank you," they said. "For suggesting I think about what truth I was avoiding. In Potions."

"I didn't suggest anything," Mira said, but she was smiling. "You figured it out yourself."

"I had help," Elliot insisted. "Good friends who let me be uncertain and scared and don't need me to have all the answers."

"Well," Mira adjusted her reading glasses, "that's what friends are for, isn't it? Sitting with each other in the uncertainty until things get clearer."

"Like a potion," Elliot said.

"Like a potion," Mira agreed.

They walked together through the quiet corridors, past the December decorations and the suits of armor and the sleeping portraits. Outside the windows, snow was falling again, gentle and steady. Elliot thought about their clear potion, about admitting their fears, about the long conversation they'd eventually need to have with their mother.

But not tonight. Tonight they were just a student at Hogwarts, walking with a friend, with snow falling and the whole uncertain future stretching ahead. Tonight, they didn't need to have answers. They just needed to be honest about the questions.

And somehow, that felt like enough.
