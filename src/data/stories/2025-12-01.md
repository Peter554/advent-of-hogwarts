The first thing Mira Patel noticed when she woke on December 1st wasn't the cold—though the dormitory was decidedly chilly—but the light. It was wrong somehow, filtered and fractured, casting strange blue-white patterns across the stone walls of Ravenclaw Tower.

She pushed herself up on her elbows, reaching automatically for her reading glasses on the bedside table. The other three beds in her dormitory were still curtained, their occupants breathing the deep, steady rhythm of sleep. Through the tall Gothic window, dawn was just breaking over the Hogwarts grounds, the sky that particular shade of grey that promised either snow or rain.

But the window itself was what caught her attention. Ice had formed across the glass in elaborate patterns—not the random fractals she'd seen on windows at home in London, but something else entirely. Something deliberate.

Mira slipped out of bed, her bare feet finding the cold stone floor. She padded across the room, pulling her Ravenclaw dressing gown tighter against the December chill. As she drew closer to the window, her breath caught.

The frost wasn't just beautiful. It was runic.

Ancient Runes was one of Mira's favorite subjects, and she'd spent countless hours poring over the textbook Professor Babbling had recommended for supplementary reading. These symbols on the window—delicate lines of ice that seemed to glow with their own internal light—were unmistakably runic. She could make out Dagaz, the rune of awakening, and nearby what looked like Wunjo, the rune of joy. But the sequence didn't quite make sense to her sleepy brain, and several of the symbols she didn't recognize at all.

She reached out, her fingertips hovering just above the glass. The frost was cold enough that she could feel it from an inch away, but there was something else too—a faint tingle of magic that made the fine hairs on her arm stand up.

"What are you doing?"

Mira jumped, spinning around. Her roommate, Claire, was peering out from between her bed curtains, eyes squinting against the early morning light.

"The window," Mira said, gesturing. "Look at the frost."

Claire groaned and pulled her pillow over her head. "It's not even seven yet. Look at frost later."

But Mira couldn't stop looking. The patterns seemed to shift and shimmer as the light changed, new symbols revealing themselves while others faded. It was like watching a message being written in real time, and the researcher in her couldn't let it go.

She needed her friends.

---

Twenty minutes later, Mira had managed to dress, grab her Ancient Runes textbook, and send hurried notes via the school's owl post system to Finn, Zara, and Elliot. She'd considered waiting until breakfast, but what if the frost melted? What if this was a once-in-a-lifetime phenomenon?

Finn was the first to arrive, still tugging his yellow and black scarf into place and looking distinctly rumpled. His reddish-brown hair stuck up in about six different directions, and there was a smudge of what looked like potting soil on his cheek.

"This better be good," he said, but he was smiling. "I was actually having a decent dream for once."

"Look." Mira pointed at the window.

Finn's eyes widened. He moved closer, then stopped, cocking his head like he was listening to something. "Is it just me, or does it feel... alive?"

"You feel it too?" Mira felt a rush of relief. Sometimes her friends teased her about being too caught up in books and theory, but Finn had an intuitive sense for magic that she envied. If he thought something was special, it probably was.

"What's alive?" Zara appeared in the doorway, slightly out of breath. She was already dressed in her Quidditch practice gear, hair pulled back in her usual box braids. "I was on my way to early morning drills when your owl nearly took my head off. This better not be about homework."

"It's not homework," Mira protested. "Look at the window."

Zara looked, and her expression shifted from annoyance to wonder. "Okay," she said slowly. "That's pretty cool. What is it?"

"Ancient Runes, I think. But I can't read all of them."

"Can anyone?" Elliot's dry voice came from the doorway. They leaned against the frame, arms crossed, looking far more put-together than Finn despite the early hour. Their grey eyes took in the frost pattern with interest. "Morning, by the way."

"Morning," Mira said. "And I think I can read parts of it, but not the whole thing. That's why I needed you all. Different perspectives."

Elliot pushed off from the doorframe and approached the window, their silver rings catching the strange blue light of the frost. "Alright. Let's see what we've got."

For the next hour, the four of them huddled around the window, Mira's textbook open on a nearby desk as they tried to decipher the symbols. Some were straightforward—runes of winter, of memory, of learning. Others were archaic variants that even Professor Babbling's advanced texts didn't clearly explain.

"Wait," Finn said suddenly. He'd been quiet for a while, just staring at the patterns. "What if it's not one message? What if it's... layered?"

"Layered how?" Zara asked.

"Like..." Finn gestured vaguely, the way he often did when he was trying to articulate something he understood intuitively rather than intellectually. "Like a plant. You've got roots and stems and leaves, and they're all part of the same thing, but they're also separate. What if the frost is like that?"

Mira looked back at the window with fresh eyes. What if Finn was right? What if instead of trying to read it left to right, top to bottom, like a sentence, they needed to see it as a whole?

She stepped back, letting her eyes unfocus slightly, the way Professor Babbling had taught them to do when studying particularly complex runic inscriptions. And suddenly, she saw it.

"Oh," she breathed.

"Oh?" Elliot raised an eyebrow. "Care to share with the class?"

"It's not a sentence. It's a... a concept. Or a reminder." Mira's mind was racing now, pieces clicking together. "Dagaz—awakening. Wunjo—joy. Isa—ice, stillness. And this one—" she pointed to a symbol near the bottom, "—that's a variant of Raido. Journey. But they're arranged in a circle, see? They feed into each other."

"So what does it mean?" Zara asked.

Mira pulled out her notebook and started sketching the pattern, her hand flying across the page. "I think... I think it's about paying attention. About finding wonder in small moments. The ice and stillness—that's winter, but also pause, rest. And the awakening and joy and journey—those are about staying curious, about finding magic in everyday things even when the world is cold and dark."

"That's..." Finn smiled. "That's actually really nice."

"But who made it?" Zara asked the practical question. "Did someone enchant your window?"

"I don't think so," Elliot said slowly. They'd been examining the edges of the frost pattern. "Look at how it's formed. This isn't a spell someone cast. It's too... organic. It grew this way."

"Then how—"

"The library," Mira said suddenly. "We need to check the library. If this is a natural phenomenon, someone must have documented it before."

---

Madam Oakwood, the librarian, raised her eyebrows when four students showed up before breakfast, but she directed them to the Restricted Section after Mira explained what they were researching. "Natural magical phenomena," she mused. "Try the section on castle magic. And don't touch anything that hisses."

It took them another hour of searching through dusty volumes—with Elliot nearly triggering what appeared to be a hex on one particularly old tome—before they found it. A slim leather journal tucked between two much larger books, its cover embossed with a simple date: 1973-1974.

Inside, the handwriting was neat and precise: *Observations on Magical Frost Formations in Ravenclaw Tower, by Margaret Chen, Seventh Year.*

"Listen to this," Mira said, reading aloud. "'On the first of December, 1973, I observed an unusual frost pattern on the northern window of the fifth-year girls' dormitory. The pattern appeared to contain runic symbols, though whether by design or coincidence, I could not immediately determine. Upon investigation, I discovered that this phenomenon occurs once every seven years, on December 1st, when the conditions are precisely correct: a clear dawn after a freezing night, with the castle's natural magic at a particular seasonal resonance.'"

"Once every seven years," Finn repeated. "That means—"

"This hasn't happened since 2018," Elliot finished. "And won't happen again until 2032."

Mira kept reading. "'The frost patterns are not random, nor are they cast by any individual spell. Rather, they are a form of communication from the castle itself—or perhaps from the hundreds of students who have studied within these walls, their magic seeping into the very stones. Each pattern is unique, tailored to the observer. What I saw this morning was a message I needed: seek wonder in small things. During this busy term, I had forgotten to look up from my books and see the beauty around me.'"

There was a moment of silence as the four friends absorbed this.

"The castle sent you a message," Zara said finally. "That's... okay, that's amazing."

"Not just me," Mira said, looking around at her friends. "Us. We all saw it together."

"And what does that mean?" Finn asked.

Mira thought about it. She thought about how she'd woken up early, immediately worried about the Ancient Runes exam coming up in January, about her Transfiguration essay, about maintaining her grades. She thought about how she sometimes got so caught up in achieving, in being perfect, that she forgot why she loved magic in the first place.

"I think," she said slowly, "it means we should pay attention this month. Not just to our studies, but to... everything. To moments like this. To the magic in small things."

Elliot nodded thoughtfully. "Not the worst reminder to get on December 1st."

"Definitely beats the motivational posters they used to have in my primary school," Finn agreed.

Zara checked her watch and swore. "I completely missed Quidditch practice. Captain's going to kill me."

"Tell them you were doing essential magical research," Elliot suggested.

"Right, because 'I was looking at fancy frost' is definitely going to fly."

But Zara was grinning, and as they left the library together—Finn and Zara arguing good-naturedly about the relative importance of Quidditch versus magical phenomena, Elliot contributing sarcastic commentary—Mira felt something settle in her chest. A sense of rightness. Of beginning.

December 1st. The first day of the last month of term. And somehow, despite all the stress and exams and essays looming on the horizon, it felt like something special was starting.

She just had to remember to look for it.

---

Later that evening, after classes and dinner and homework, Mira returned to her dormitory. The frost had melted from the window, leaving nothing but a few water droplets trailing down the glass. But she remembered the patterns, had copied them carefully into her notebook. And more importantly, she remembered the feeling of standing there with her friends, all of them working together to decode something beautiful and strange.

She sat at her desk and pulled out a fresh piece of parchment. At the top, she wrote: *December Magic: A Personal Journal.*

Underneath, she began to write about the frost, about the runes, about Margaret Chen's journal from 1973. But also about Finn's bedhead and the soil on his cheek, about Zara showing up in her Quidditch gear ready to complain but staying anyway, about the way Elliot's rings had caught the light. The small things. The wonderful things.

Outside, the first snow of December began to fall, and Hogwarts settled into its winter beauty. Somewhere in the castle, three other students were thinking about frost and runes and the magic of paying attention. And in Ravenclaw Tower, Mira Patel smiled as she wrote, her glasses sliding down her nose, utterly content with this small, perfect moment.

It was, she thought, exactly what the message had meant.
