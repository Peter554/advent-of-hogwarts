The thing about Peeves was that he never did anything halfway.

Elliot discovered this firsthand when they rounded the corner onto the fourth-floor corridor Monday morning and were immediately greeted by a suit of armor belting out what could only be described as a deeply personal roast set to music.

*"Here comes Elliot with their silver rings, sketching little private things! Hiding portraits, keeping secrets, hope nobody ever peeks at—"*

"Absolutely not," Elliot said, spinning on their heel.

But the armor's voice followed them, echoing off the stone walls. *"Running away won't help you flee, from truths that everyone can see—"*

Elliot broke into a jog, their face burning. Behind them, they could hear other students laughing—or groaning as the armor turned its attention to them.

By the time they reached Ancient Runes, their hands were shaking with a mixture of embarrassment and anger. They slid into their usual seat next to Mira, who took one look at their face and frowned.

"What happened?"

"Peeves," Elliot said tightly. "He's enchanted a suit of armor on the fourth floor. It's singing... personal things. About everyone who walks past."

Mira's eyes widened. "How personal?"

"Personal enough."

Before Mira could respond, the door burst open and Zara stormed in, radiating fury. Her box braids were slightly disheveled, and her jaw was set in a way that usually preceded someone getting hexed.

"I'm going to destroy that armor," she announced, dropping into a seat. "I'm going to melt it down and turn it into chamber pots."

"The singing armor?" Mira asked.

"It sang about my Quidditch stats," Zara said, seething. "Made up a whole verse about how I fumbled the Quaffle in last week's practice. How did it even know about that?"

"Peeves knows everything," Elliot muttered.

Professor Babbling entered then, calling the class to order, but the lesson was punctuated by whispered complaints about the armor. Apparently, everyone who'd passed through the fourth-floor corridor that morning had gotten their own personalized embarrassment.

At lunch, the four friends gathered at their usual spot—today the Slytherin table, which Elliot had claimed by virtue of arriving first—and found the entire Great Hall buzzing with indignation.

"It told everyone about my broken Remembrall," Finn said miserably, pushing food around his plate. "Made it rhyme with 'fumble' and 'tumble.' Very clever. Very humiliating."

"It brought up my runic frost obsession," Mira added. "Something about 'studying ice while missing life twice.'"

"That doesn't even scan properly," Elliot said.

"Doesn't matter. Everyone heard it." Mira sighed. "We have to go past that corridor to get to Charms this afternoon. I'm considering taking the long route through the dungeons."

"No," Zara said firmly. "We're not letting Peeves win. That's exactly what he wants—for us to be too embarrassed to use that corridor."

"So what do we do?" Finn asked. "Try to un-enchant the armor?"

"Professor Flitwick tried this morning, according to the fifth years," Elliot said. "Apparently the enchantment is too clever. It'll take time to unravel without damaging the armor itself."

"We could just hex it," Zara suggested.

"And get detention," Mira pointed out. "It's still school property."

"Worth it."

"No, it's not." Mira was quiet for a moment, her expression thoughtful. "What if we're thinking about this wrong?"

"What do you mean?" Elliot asked.

"Well, we can't remove the enchantment, and we can't destroy the armor. But what if we don't need to? What if we could... change what it's singing about?"

"I don't think we can reprogram Peeves's magic," Finn said doubtfully.

"Not reprogram. Redirect." Mira leaned forward, her eyes bright with that particular spark that meant she'd had an idea. "Think about it—Peeves does this stuff because he wants a reaction. He wants us upset and embarrassed. But what if we gave him a different kind of attention?"

"I'm not following," Zara said.

"What if we challenged him?" Mira said. "Not to a fight, but to something more interesting. He's clearly very clever with lyrics. What if we acknowledged that, but suggested a different subject matter?"

Elliot was starting to understand. "Like... Christmas carols?"

"Exactly!" Mira's expression was triumphant. "It's mid-December. We've got the Yule Ball coming up in a week. What if the armor sang festive songs instead of embarrassing ones?"

"Peeves would never go for it," Finn said, but he was smiling. "Would he?"

"Only one way to find out," Zara said, standing up. "Come on. We've got fifteen minutes before Charms, and we have to go past that corridor anyway."

---

The fourth-floor corridor was noticeably empty when they arrived, most students having chosen alternate routes. The suit of armor stood near the middle of the hallway, a full set of medieval plate armor complete with helmet and sword. It looked perfectly ordinary until they drew closer.

Then it burst into song.

*"Four little friends think they're so clever, walking together now and forever! But secrets and doubts and worries unsaid, will follow them always straight into their—"*

"Okay, that's enough," Zara said loudly, cutting off what was surely going to rhyme with 'bed' in an extremely inappropriate way. She planted herself directly in front of the armor, hands on her hips. "Peeves! We know you're listening. Get out here!"

For a moment, nothing happened. Then the air shimmered, and Peeves materialized above the armor, floating cross-legged and wearing his usual expression of malicious glee.

"Little students want to complain?" he cackled. "Don't like the armor's sweet refrain?"

"Actually," Mira said, stepping forward with the careful tone one might use when approaching a dangerous creature, "we were impressed. The rhymes are quite clever."

Peeves's eyes narrowed suspiciously. "Is this a trick?"

"No trick," Mira assured him. "The lyrics are genuinely creative. The wordplay is sophisticated. It's just..."

"What?" Peeves drifted lower, genuinely curious despite himself.

"It's a bit easy, isn't it?" Elliot said, picking up on Mira's strategy. "I mean, singing about people's embarrassing moments—that doesn't take much skill. Anyone could do that."

"Anyone could NOT!" Peeves said indignantly.

"Sure they could," Zara said, warming to the plan. "You just mention something awkward and add a rhyme. But you know what would be actually impressive? Making up Christmas carols. Good ones. That people would actually want to hear."

Peeves floated higher, doing a backflip. "Peeves can do ANYTHING with songs!"

"Prove it," Finn said quietly.

The poltergeist stopped mid-flip. "Prove it?"

"Yeah," Mira said. "We challenge you. Can you make this armor sing festive songs instead? Real carols, not just mean ones with Christmas words swapped in. Songs people would actually enjoy and maybe even sing along to?"

"And if Peeves does this, what does Peeves get?" The poltergeist's eyes glittered with interest.

"Attention," Elliot said simply. "Right now, everyone's avoiding this corridor. But if the armor sang Christmas carols, people would come here specifically to listen. You'd have an audience instead of just victims."

Peeves considered this, floating in slow circles around the armor. The four friends held their breath, not daring to look at each other in case it broke the spell.

"FINE!" Peeves shouted suddenly. "Peeves will show you! Peeves will make the BEST Christmas songs, and you'll all sing along, or Peeves goes back to the embarrassing verses!"

"Deal," Mira said quickly, before anyone could reconsider.

Peeves zoomed down to the armor, passing through it in a flash of cold air. The armor shuddered, its metal plates clanking together, and then—

*"Deck the halls with boughs of holly, fa-la-la-la-la, la-la-la-la!"*

But it wasn't just the familiar tune. As the armor sang, magical holly garlands materialized along the corridor walls, their berries glowing softly. The armor's voice had changed too—less mocking, more... musical. Almost beautiful, in a tinny, metallic way.

*"'Tis the season to be jolly, fa-la-la-la-la, la-la-la-la! Don we now our bright apparel, fa-la-la, la-la-la, la-la-la! Sing the ancient Yuletide carol, fa-la-la-la-la, la-la-la-la!"*

"It's actually... nice," Finn said wonderingly.

Zara cupped her hands around her mouth. "Don we now our bright apparel!" she sang along loudly, slightly off-key but enthusiastic.

A few students who'd been hovering at the far end of the corridor, waiting for the coast to clear, edged closer. One of them—a third-year Hufflepuff—tentatively joined in on the "fa-la-la-la-la."

Mira nudged Elliot. "Come on."

"I don't sing," Elliot protested.

"Today you do," Mira said firmly, and began singing along with the chorus.

What else could Elliot do? They joined in, their voice quiet but present. Finn added his voice too, and soon all four of them were singing with the enchanted armor while Peeves swooped overhead, adding percussion by banging on a nearby shield.

More students appeared, drawn by the music. What had been an empty corridor was filling with people—some cautious, some curious, but all of them staying once they realized the armor was singing actual Christmas carols instead of personal attacks.

The armor finished "Deck the Halls" and immediately launched into "The Holly and the Ivy," this time with even more magical decorations appearing. Actual holly and ivy wound their way along the walls, the plants growing in fast-forward. The armor's helmet tilted back as it sang, and tiny snowflakes began to fall from the ceiling, melting before they hit the ground.

"This is brilliant," a fifth-year Ravenclaw said, stopping beside Mira. "I was dreading walking past here, but this is actually lovely."

"Sing along," Mira encouraged. "That was part of the deal."

"What deal?"

"You'll see."

More students joined the growing crowd. By the time the armor started on "We Wish You a Merry Christmas," there had to be at least thirty people in the corridor, most of them singing along. The sound echoed off the stone walls, transforming the previously dreaded hallway into something festive and warm.

Peeves was having the time of his afterlife. He zoomed through the crowd, occasionally dropping water balloons that turned out to be filled with harmless, glittery snow. He conducted the singing with exaggerated gestures, making the armor's voice swell at dramatic moments. And unless Elliot was very much mistaken, the poltergeist looked genuinely pleased—not with his usual malicious glee, but with something closer to actual joy.

"I can't believe this worked," Finn said, grinning.

"It's not over yet," Mira cautioned. "We need to make sure it sticks."

As if on cue, Professor McGonagall appeared at the end of the corridor, no doubt drawn by the commotion. The singing faltered as students noticed her approach, but she simply surveyed the scene—the singing armor, the magical decorations, the crowd of students, and Peeves conducting overhead—with her usual stern expression.

"Mr. Peeves," she said crisply. "I assume you're responsible for this?"

"Peeves made BEAUTIFUL songs!" the poltergeist declared proudly. "Students asked for carols, and Peeves provided!"

"Actually, that's true, Professor," Mira spoke up quickly. "Peeves was causing trouble this morning, but we challenged him to create something festive instead. And he did. This is all Christmas carols now."

Professor McGonagall's lips twitched in what might have been the beginning of a smile. "I see. And the holly and ivy?"

"Decorative flourishes," Elliot supplied. "Non-harmful."

The professor was quiet for a long moment, and Elliot was certain they were all about to get detention for encouraging Peeves. But then McGonagall did something unexpected.

She began to sing.

*"We wish you a merry Christmas, we wish you a merry Christmas, we wish you a merry Christmas, and a happy New Year!"*

Her voice was clear and surprisingly strong. The students stared for a moment, then enthusiastically joined in. The armor's voice swelled, harmonizing with the human singers. Even Peeves seemed momentarily stunned before recovering and conducting with renewed vigor.

When the carol ended, Professor McGonagall nodded approvingly. "Much better than the alternative, Mr. Peeves. I expect this corridor to remain festive rather than embarrassing for the remainder of the term. Is that understood?"

"Peeves will keep singing carols!" the poltergeist agreed. "As long as students keep listening!"

"Oh, they'll listen," Zara assured him. "This is way better than taking the long route through the dungeons."

"Indeed," Professor McGonagall said. "Now, shouldn't most of you be heading to your afternoon classes?"

There was a general scramble as students checked the time and realized they were pushing the limit. The crowd dispersed, but they went with smiles on their faces, several still humming the tunes.

"Miss Patel," Professor McGonagall said quietly, stopping Mira as she turned to leave. "That was clever thinking. Redirecting rather than confronting."

"Thank you, Professor," Mira said, a bit startled.

"Five points to Ravenclaw. And five each to Hufflepuff, Gryffindor, and Slytherin," she added, including the others. "For creative problem-solving and improving the school environment. Now off to class, all of you."

They hurried toward Charms, arriving slightly late but in considerably better moods than earlier.

---

Over the next few days, the fourth-floor corridor became something of a destination. Students would take detours just to hear the singing armor, which cycled through an impressive repertoire of Christmas carols—some traditional, some more obscure, and a few that Peeves seemed to be making up on the spot but which were nonetheless cheerful and seasonally appropriate.

The magical decorations had become permanent fixtures, transforming the once-ordinary hallway into a festive thoroughfare. Someone (probably Peeves) had added enchanted candles that floated near the ceiling, and the snow continued to fall gently from nowhere, melting just before it touched the ground.

"It's become a thing," Finn reported at dinner on Thursday. "First years are bringing their friends to see it. I heard some seventh years saying it's the best Peeves has ever been."

"He's actually behaving," Zara marveled. "I never thought I'd see the day."

"He's not behaving," Elliot corrected. "He's getting what he wanted all along—attention and appreciation. We just channeled it in a less destructive direction."

"Same principle as when we redirected him with the pudding incident," Mira noted.

"We agreed never to speak of the pudding incident," Finn reminded her.

"My point," Mira continued, ignoring him, "is that sometimes you don't need to fight a problem head-on. Sometimes you just need to reframe it. Change the context, and the whole situation transforms."

Elliot thought about this—about the armor that had started as harassment and become entertainment, about embarrassing verses transformed into festive carols. They thought about their own situation too, the pendant hidden under their shirt that had once felt like a secret to guard and now felt like a quiet affirmation.

"It's not always that simple, though," they said slowly. "Some problems can't just be redirected."

"No," Mira agreed. "But more of them can than we might think. It's worth trying before we assume we have to just endure things or fight them."

"Deep thoughts for dinner conversation," Zara said, but she was smiling. "Are we going to walk past the armor after this? I could use some festive cheer before tackling my Transfiguration essay."

"Absolutely," Finn said. "I heard it's been doing harmonies."

They finished their meal and headed up to the fourth floor, joining a small crowd of students who'd had the same idea. The armor was midway through "Good King Wenceslas," the music echoing beautifully off the stone walls. Peeves was visible as a shimmer in the air, conducting enthusiastically.

When the poltergeist spotted them, he zoomed down, doing a loop-the-loop around the group.

"It's Peeves's special friends!" he announced. "The ones who made the challenge!"

"We're not friends," Elliot said, but without much conviction.

"Special friends get special songs!" Peeves ignored them completely. "Armor, do the one about the four students!"

The armor paused, then launched into a melody none of them recognized. It took Elliot a moment to realize Peeves was making it up on the spot.

*"Four friends together in winter's embrace, each one unique but sharing this space. Ravenclaw's wisdom and Hufflepuff's heart, Gryffindor's courage and Slytherin's art. Different houses but one point of view: friendship's the magic that makes them all true!"*

It was cheesy. It was sentimental. It was also kind of perfect.

"Did you just write something nice about us?" Zara asked, genuinely shocked.

"Peeves can do nice!" the poltergeist said defensively. "When students aren't being boring!"

"It's a good song," Finn said sincerely. "Thank you."

Peeves did another backflip, clearly pleased, then zoomed off to bother some third years into singing along.

"Well," Mira said, watching him go, "I think that's about as close to friendship as you get with Peeves."

"I'll take it," Elliot said.

They stood there a while longer, listening to the armor cycle through more carols while students came and went, all of them singing along or humming or simply enjoying the festive atmosphere. What had started as an embarrassing ordeal had become something genuinely lovely—proof that sometimes the best solution wasn't to fight back but to find a new way forward.

And as they finally headed to their respective common rooms, homework calling and the Yule Ball drawing ever closer, Elliot felt something settle in their chest. A sense of possibility. Of problems that could be solved with creativity rather than conflict. Of uncomfortable situations that could be transformed into something better.

The singing armor was just one small thing in the grand scheme of Hogwarts, but it represented something larger. The power of seeing situations differently. Of choosing collaboration over confrontation. Of turning embarrassment into joy.

December was teaching them all kinds of lessons, Elliot thought. And this one might be among the most valuable.

Behind them, the armor launched into "O Come All Ye Faithful," and the corridor filled with voices—human and magical, harmonizing together in something unexpectedly beautiful.

And somewhere in the castle, Peeves cackled with delight, already planning tomorrow's carol selections.
