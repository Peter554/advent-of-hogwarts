Finn had been waiting for this moment all week.

"Are you sure Professor Sprout said it was okay?" Mira asked for the third time as they crossed the snow-covered grounds toward the greenhouses. The sun had set an hour ago, and the glass structures glowed with warm golden light against the December darkness.

"Yes, I'm sure," Finn said patiently, adjusting the thick gloves he'd brought from the potting shed. "She trusts me with the advanced specimens now. I've been helping her after class for two months."

"Advanced specimens," Elliot repeated, their breath misting in the cold air. "That's a very diplomatic way of saying 'plants that can kill you.'"

"They won't kill you if you know what you're doing," Finn said, though his grin suggested he wasn't entirely opposed to a little danger. "Besides, I wanted to show you all. Greenhouse Seven is incredible. Most students never get to see inside."

"I still think this could wait until daylight," Mira muttered, but her eyes were bright with curiosity despite her protests.

"Where's your sense of adventure?" Zara asked, throwing an arm around Mira's shoulders. She'd come straight from Quidditch practice and still wore her team scarf, her box braids pulled back in a high ponytail. "Finn's been going on about these plants for weeks. I want to see what's got him so excited."

"Everything gets Finn excited," Elliot pointed out. "Last week it was compost."

"That was specialty compost," Finn protested. "Dragon dung and ashwinder eggs. Do you know how hard it is to get the proportions right?"

"I truly don't, and I'm happy to keep it that way."

But they were all smiling as they reached the greenhouses. Finn led them past the familiar structures—Greenhouse One where first years learned the basics, Greenhouse Three with its more interesting specimens—to the smallest building at the far end of the row. Greenhouse Seven was barely larger than a garden shed, but its glass panels were fogged with moisture and heat, and even from outside, Mira could see the dense tangle of vegetation within.

Finn produced a key—actually trusted with a key, which said something about Professor Sprout's confidence in him—and unlocked the door. A wave of humid, fragrant air rolled out, carrying scents of earth and growing things and something else, something almost electric.

"Welcome," Finn said, ushering them inside with obvious pride, "to Greenhouse Seven."

---

The interior was like stepping into a different world.

Where the other greenhouses were organized with neat rows and labeled planters, Greenhouse Seven was almost wild. Plants climbed the walls and hung from the ceiling in elaborate tangles. The air shimmered with heat from enchanted braziers, and magical lights floated among the leaves, casting everything in a dreamlike glow.

"It's beautiful," Mira breathed, her earlier nervousness forgotten. She moved toward a cluster of flowers that seemed to shimmer between colors—blue to purple to silver and back again.

"Don't touch those," Finn said quickly. "Chromatic lilies. They're harmless, but they'll stain your skin for a week."

Mira pulled her hand back, but she was smiling. "What else?"

Finn was in his element now, moving through the greenhouse with easy confidence. He pointed out specimens as they walked the narrow path between the planters: a miniature palm tree whose leaves chimed like bells when disturbed, a collection of flowers that sang in soft, wordless harmonies, and a potted shrub that appeared to be pruning its own dead branches with tiny animated scissors.

"Self-pruning roses," Finn explained. "They're experimental. Professor Sprout is trying to develop a variety that can maintain itself. It could revolutionize magical gardening."

"That's actually brilliant," Elliot said, crouching down to examine the plant more closely. "Imagine the applications for potion ingredients—plants that could harvest themselves at optimal potency."

"Exactly what Professor Sprout said." Finn beamed. "She's talking about writing a paper on it next year. She might even let me co-author if the research goes well."

"That's amazing, Finn," Zara said warmly. "You should be really proud."

Finn's ears turned slightly pink, but his smile widened. "Come on, there's more."

He led them deeper into the greenhouse, where the plants became progressively stranger and more dangerous-looking. A section of carnivorous specimens snapped their teeth as they passed. A vine with thorns that glowed faintly orange writhed in its pot, reaching toward them before Finn tapped it gently with his wand and murmured something that made it settle.

At the back of the greenhouse, separated from the others by a low fence, stood a young tree that looked deceptively ordinary—if you ignored the fact that its branches were twitching ominously.

"Is that what I think it is?" Mira asked, stopping a safe distance away.

"Baby Whomping Willow," Finn confirmed. "Only five years old. The one by the castle is over fifty. This one's still in training, learning to control its defensive responses."

"You train them?" Elliot asked, fascinated despite themselves.

"Sort of. You work with them, help them understand the difference between threats and just... the world existing around them. This one's doing really well. Watch."

Finn approached the fence slowly, speaking in a low, calm voice. "Hey there, it's just me. You remember me, right? We're friends."

The tree's branches stilled, then drooped slightly in what looked almost like recognition. Finn reached out carefully and touched one of the lower branches. It curled around his wrist gently, not squeezing, just... holding.

"That's incredible," Mira said softly. "I had no idea they could be so—"

"Gentle?" Finn finished. "They're not naturally violent. They're just scared and defensive. Once they trust you, they're actually quite sweet."

He unwrapped the branch carefully and stepped back. The Whomping Willow's branches waved in what might have been a farewell.

"Alright," Zara said, "I'm officially impressed. What else have you got in here?"

Finn's expression turned slightly mischievous. "Well, there's one more thing. But you have to promise to stay back and not touch anything without asking first."

"Now I'm worried," Elliot muttered.

"You should be," Finn said cheerfully, and led them to the far corner of the greenhouse.

---

The Venomous Tentacula occupied the entire back corner, its massive pot nearly three feet across. The plant itself was a twisted mass of thick, ropy vines, each one lined with wicked-looking thorns. At the center, a bulbous head swayed slightly, two eye-like spots seeming to track their movement.

"That," Zara said flatly, "is terrifying."

"It's actually one of the safer specimens in here," Finn said. "As long as you don't threaten it or get too close. They're only dangerous when they feel attacked."

"And someone thought 'let's keep this at a school' was a good idea?" Elliot asked.

"They're valuable for potion ingredients and magical research. Plus, Professor Sprout is one of maybe ten people in Britain who can successfully cultivate them." Finn approached the plant carefully, keeping his movements slow and obvious. "They're really quite fascinating once you understand them. The vines can sense vibrations in the air and ground. They're basically blind but incredibly sensitive to—"

He reached up toward a watering system suspended above the plant, checking the reservoir level as he'd done dozens of times before. But this time, his sleeve caught on a protruding branch from a neighboring plant. He jerked his arm to free it, and the sudden motion sent his elbow directly into one of the Tentacula's hanging vines.

The reaction was instantaneous.

The vine lashed out, catching Finn across the chest and sending him stumbling backward. He crashed into the low fence separating the dangerous specimens from the rest of the greenhouse, and the fence splintered with a sharp crack.

The Tentacula, interpreting the noise and motion as an attack, erupted into full defensive mode.

Vines burst from the pot like striking serpents, tearing free of the restraints that had held them loosely in place. The plant's head swiveled wildly, and the vines began thrashing in all directions, their thorns gleaming dangerously in the magical light.

"Get back!" Finn shouted, scrambling to his feet. But there was nowhere to go—the path was narrow, and the vines were already spreading across the greenhouse, knocking over pots and tearing through smaller plants.

One vine swept toward Mira, and Zara moved without thinking.

"Protego!"

A shimmering shield snapped into existence just in time, the vine bouncing off harmlessly. But it immediately redirected toward Elliot, who barely managed to duck.

"We need to contain it!" Finn yelled over the crashing of pots and the hissing of the agitated plant. "If it gets to the other specimens—"

"Tell us what to do!" Zara shouted back, maintaining her shield as two more vines struck it in rapid succession.

"I don't know!" Finn's voice cracked with panic. "I've never seen it this angry. We need Professor Sprout—"

"No time," Elliot said, their voice cutting through the chaos with sudden clarity. They'd pressed themselves against the glass wall, eyes tracking the vines' movements with calculating precision. "Finn, you said they're sensitive to vibrations, right? What else? There has to be a weakness."

"I—" Finn's mind was racing, trying to remember his lessons, his reading, anything that might help. "They're tropical. They need heat and humidity. They're weak to—"

"Cold!" Mira's voice rang out with sudden certainty. "I read about them in *Flesh-Eating Trees of the World*. They're vulnerable to frost. Their vines lose mobility in temperatures below freezing."

"The windows," Elliot said immediately, understanding. "If we break the glass, the December air will rush in."

"But all the other plants—" Finn started.

"Will survive better than we will if this thing breaks completely free," Zara said grimly, her shield flickering under the repeated assaults. "Do it!"

Elliot raised their wand, and Finn could see their hand trembling slightly despite their calm expression. But when they spoke, their voice was steady.

"Diffindo!"

The cutting curse flew true, striking one of the glass panels near the ceiling. The glass shattered, and cold December air poured into the greenhouse like water through a broken dam.

The effect on the Tentacula was immediate. Its thrashing slowed, the vines' movements becoming sluggish. But it was still dangerous, still had enough mobility to be a threat.

"Again!" Mira called out. "We need more cold!"

"Glacius!" Zara cast while maintaining her shield, frost spreading across the nearest vines.

Elliot shattered another window, then another, their cutting hexes precise even under pressure. The temperature in the greenhouse plummeted, warm humid air replaced by frigid wind that made them all gasp.

The Tentacula's vines were moving in slow motion now, still writhing but without their earlier violence. Finn could see his opportunity.

He moved forward carefully, speaking in the same calm, low voice he'd used with the Whomping Willow. "It's alright. Nobody's attacking you. We're not threats. You're safe."

He reached out with his bare hand—his gloves had been lost in the initial fall—and touched the main trunk of the plant, just below the bulbous head. His fingers were already numb from the cold, but he could feel the plant's tension, its fear pulsing through the vine tissue.

"You're safe," he repeated, channeling what little magic he could into the touch, trying to project calm and safety and peace. "We're friends. Remember?"

For a long moment, nothing happened. Then, slowly, the vines began to retract. The Tentacula pulled its limbs back toward its pot, curling them in close like a creature hugging itself for warmth and comfort.

Finn kept his hand on the trunk, murmuring reassurances, until the last vine had settled. Only then did he step back, his legs suddenly shaky.

The greenhouse was a disaster. Broken glass crunched underfoot, pots were overturned, and smaller plants were scattered across the floor. The temperature had dropped from tropical to barely above freezing, and their breath misted in the air.

But they were all alive and unhurt.

"Everyone okay?" Zara asked, finally lowering her shield charm.

"Define 'okay,'" Elliot said, but they were already moving to check on Mira, who nodded shakily.

"I'm fine. Finn?"

"I'm..." Finn looked around at the destruction, and his face crumpled. "Professor Sprout is going to kill me. She trusted me, and I ruined everything."

"You didn't ruin anything," Mira said firmly. "It was an accident. You saved us just now."

"After I caused the problem in the first place!"

"Mr. MacLeod, I believe I'll be the judge of that."

They all spun around to find Professor Sprout standing in the doorway, her dragon-hide gloves and patched hat indicating she'd been working in one of the other greenhouses. Her expression was unreadable as she surveyed the damage.

Finn felt his stomach drop. "Professor, I'm so sorry. I was just showing them the specimens, and I accidentally triggered the Tentacula's defensive response, and—"

"Are any of you injured?" Professor Sprout interrupted.

"No, Professor," they chorused.

"And the Tentacula? How did you manage to calm it?"

"We broke the windows to let in cold air," Elliot explained. "To slow down its movements. Then Finn talked it down."

Professor Sprout's eyebrows rose. She moved through the greenhouse, stepping over debris, until she reached the Tentacula. The plant was huddled in its pot, vines curled tight, looking almost... sad.

The professor examined it carefully, then turned to Finn with an expression he couldn't quite read.

"You used the cold to suppress its defensive response, then employed empathic contact to soothe it before the temperature could cause permanent damage to the plant's tissues. Is that correct?"

"I... yes?" Finn said uncertainly.

"And whose idea was the cold?"

"Mine," Mira said quickly. "I remembered reading about their weakness to frost."

"The window breaking was mine," Elliot added. "Precise application seemed better than a general freezing charm."

"I maintained shields to protect everyone while they worked," Zara contributed.

Professor Sprout looked at each of them in turn, and then—unexpectedly—she smiled.

"Well done, all of you. That was exceptional crisis management and teamwork." She turned back to Finn. "Mr. MacLeod, what you just demonstrated is exactly the kind of thinking I need in an advanced Herbology student. You assessed the situation, coordinated your resources, and adapted your approach based on the plant's needs and limitations."

"But the greenhouse—"

"Can be repaired with magic in about ten minutes," Professor Sprout said briskly. "The broken glass is nothing. What matters is that you kept your head, protected your friends, and prioritized the wellbeing of the plant even while ensuring your own safety." She paused, her expression growing more serious. "Though I will expect a full written account of what triggered the incident and how you'll avoid similar accidents in future."

"Yes, Professor," Finn said, relief flooding through him.

"And perhaps," Professor Sprout added with a knowing look, "future tours should take place during daylight hours with me present, at least until you've had more advanced training."

"Yes, Professor," Finn repeated, appropriately chastened but unable to completely suppress his smile.

"Now then," Professor Sprout pulled out her wand, "let's repair this damage before the other specimens catch their death of cold. You four can assist. Consider it a practical lesson in greenhouse restoration."

---

Twenty minutes later, the greenhouse looked almost as good as new. Professor Sprout had repaired the glass with a series of intricate wand movements, and they'd all helped replant the scattered specimens and clean up the broken pottery. The Tentacula, once the temperature had been restored to tropical levels, had actually unfurled slightly and accepted some water from Finn without any aggressive behavior.

"I think it knows you saved it," Professor Sprout commented as Finn carefully refilled the plant's reservoir. "Tentaculas have long memories. You've likely made a friend for life."

"A friend that tried to kill me," Finn said, but he was smiling as he patted one of the now-docile vines.

When they finally left the greenhouse, stepping back out into the frigid December night, they were all exhausted but exhilarated.

"That," Zara announced, "was the most exciting thing we've done all month. And that's including the enchanted armor incident."

"I'm never going to forget the look on Finn's face when that thing broke free," Elliot said, and despite the trauma of the moment, they were grinning. "Pure panic."

"I was terrified!" Finn protested. "I thought I'd killed us all!"

"But you didn't," Mira pointed out. "You figured it out. We all did, together."

They walked in companionable silence for a moment, their boots crunching in the snow, the castle glowing warm in the distance.

"Can I just say," Finn started quietly, "thank you. All of you. You could have run, gotten a professor, left me to deal with it. But you stayed and helped."

"Of course we did," Zara said, bumping his shoulder with hers. "That's what friends do."

"Besides," Elliot added, "running would have been the boring choice. And we've learned that the creative solution is usually better."

"I don't know if fighting a carnivorous plant counts as creative," Mira said, but she was smiling.

"It does when we win," Zara declared.

They were almost back to the castle when Finn stopped suddenly, a thought occurring to him.

"You know what? Professor Sprout said I demonstrated the right kind of thinking. She's never said anything like that before."

"Because you did," Mira said. "You were amazing in there, Finn. The way you calmed that plant after we'd slowed it down—I don't think anyone else could have done that."

"And you knew exactly what information we needed," Elliot added. "The weakness to cold, the empathic contact. You coordinated the whole thing."

"With your help," Finn insisted. "Mira's knowledge, Elliot's precision, Zara's protection—I couldn't have done any of it alone."

"That's the point," Zara said. "None of us could have. But together?" She grinned. "Together we're pretty damn impressive."

Finn felt something warm settle in his chest, pushing back against the lingering cold from the greenhouse. They were right. Individually, they each had their strengths, but combined—combining Mira's research skills with Elliot's strategic thinking, Zara's quick reflexes with his own understanding of magical creatures—they were formidable.

"Next time," Elliot said as they reached the castle doors, "maybe we could have an adventure that doesn't involve mortal peril?"

"Where's the fun in that?" Zara asked.

"I'm with Elliot," Mira said. "Something nice and safe. Like... I don't know, dueling club?"

"That literally involves people throwing hexes at each other," Finn pointed out.

"Yes, but in a controlled environment with supervision."

"Fair point."

They parted ways in the entrance hall—Finn and Zara heading toward their respective common rooms, Elliot toward the dungeons, Mira up to Ravenclaw Tower. But before they separated, they stood together for a moment, still energized by their shared experience.

"Same time next week?" Zara asked with a grin. "Different greenhouse?"

"Absolutely not," Finn said, but he was laughing.

"We'll find something," Mira assured them. "Something that doesn't involve breaking things or nearly dying."

"You're taking all the excitement out of December," Elliot complained.

"Good," Mira and Finn said in unison.

They said their goodnights and went their separate ways, but Finn found himself replaying the evening as he walked through the corridors. The initial terror of the Tentacula breaking free. The way his friends had immediately jumped into action. The feeling of the plant calming under his hands, trusting him even after being frightened.

And Professor Sprout's words: *Exceptional crisis management. The kind of thinking I need in an advanced student.*

He'd spent so much time feeling like he wasn't quite good enough—not academic like Mira, not strategic like Elliot, not naturally confident like Zara. He was just Finn, who liked plants and got dirt under his fingernails.

But tonight had proven that his skills mattered. That understanding magical creatures and having a calm presence and being able to work with growing things—those were valuable abilities. Maybe even exceptional ones.

By the time he reached the Hufflepuff common room, he was smiling despite his exhaustion. He'd have bruises tomorrow from his collision with the fence, and he still had to write that incident report for Professor Sprout. But it had been worth it—to see his friends' faces light up at the singing orchids, to share his passion with people who cared, to work together to solve a genuinely dangerous problem.

December kept teaching them lessons, he thought as he climbed the stairs to his dormitory. About friendship and trust and discovering what you were capable of when you combined your strengths.

Not bad lessons to learn, especially with the Yule Ball and the end of term approaching. Whatever challenges came next, he knew they could face them together.

And that knowledge was better than any plant magic he'd learned in Greenhouse Seven.

Though the singing orchids had been pretty amazing too.
