Professor Willow Thorne had a gift for making announcements sound exciting. She stood at the front of the Charms classroom on Friday afternoon, practically vibrating with enthusiasm, her wand tracing sparkles in the air as she spoke.

"I'm thrilled to announce," she said, her eyes bright, "that Hogwarts will be reviving the Dueling Club this year! Professor Flitwick and I have been working on the curriculum, and we'll be holding our first session next Thursday evening."

A ripple of interest went through the classroom. Zara, sitting near the front, sat up straighter.

"Now, I want to be very clear about what this club is and isn't," Professor Thorne continued. "This is not about aggression or showing off. Dueling, when practiced properly, is an art form. It's about control, strategy, respect for your opponent, and precise spellwork. We'll be focusing on defensive magic primarily, with offensive spells taught only in context of structured dueling."

She waved her wand, and a sign-up sheet appeared, floating gently to pin itself to the notice board.

"All years welcome, though we'll be grouping by skill level. Sessions are Thursday evenings, seven o'clock, in the Great Hall. Anyone interested should sign up by Monday." She smiled. "And please do come with an open mind. You might surprise yourself with what you're capable of."

As the class packed up their things, Zara was already out of her seat and across the room, quill in hand. Mira, gathering her Charms notes at a more leisurely pace, watched her friend scrawl her name on the list with decisive strokes.

"You're signing up?" Mira asked, though it wasn't really a question.

"Obviously." Zara grinned over her shoulder. "Come on, you should too. It'll be fun!"

"I'm not really the dueling type," Mira protested.

"Neither am I," said a Hufflepuff named Oliver, who was hovering near the list. "But it sounds interesting. Different from just learning spells in class, you know?"

Finn, who'd been chatting with Elliot near the door, caught the tail end of the conversation. "What sounds interesting?"

"Dueling Club," Zara said. "And you're all signing up with me. No arguments."

"I didn't realize we got a vote," Elliot said dryly, but they were already moving toward the notice board, examining the sign-up sheet.

"You don't," Zara said cheerfully. "Come on, it'll be good for us. Practical magic, working on reflexes. Besides, when else are we going to learn proper dueling? I'm not talking about fighting," she added quickly, seeing Mira's hesitant expression. "I'm talking about the sport of it. The skill."

Finn was already signing his name below Zara's. "I'm in. Could be useful, honestly. I'm rubbish at defensive spells."

"That's because you're too nice to think about defending yourself," Elliot observed, but they were pulling out their own quill. "Fine. But if I hex any of you by accident, remember you volunteered for this."

Mira looked at her three friends, all watching her expectantly. She thought about the frost message from the beginning of the month—about seeking wonder in small things, about trying new experiences. She thought about Professor Thorne's comment about surprising yourself.

"Alright," she said, taking the quill from Elliot. "But if I'm terrible at this, you're all forbidden from laughing."

"We would never," Finn said, entirely unconvincingly.

---

Thursday evening found them walking together toward the Great Hall, which had been transformed for the occasion. The house tables had been pushed against the walls, leaving a large open space in the center. Cushioning charms had been placed around the perimeter, and the enchanted ceiling showed a clear winter night sky, stars bright against the darkness.

Professor Thorne stood on a raised platform with Professor Flitwick and a tall, stern-looking wizard Elliot recognized as Professor Vance, who taught Advanced Defense Against the Dark Arts to seventh years.

The turnout was impressive—at least sixty students, ranging from nervous-looking second years to confident seventh years. Elliot spotted their fellow Slytherins and gave a brief nod of acknowledgment. Zara was practically bouncing on her toes, her athletic energy barely contained.

"Welcome, everyone!" Professor Thorne's voice carried easily across the space, amplified by a light Sonorus charm. "I'm delighted to see so many interested faces. Before we begin, let's establish some ground rules."

She waved her wand, and words appeared in the air, glowing softly:

*1. Respect your opponent at all times*
*2. Stop immediately when the professor calls halt*
*3. No dark magic, no dangerous hexes*
*4. Control is more important than power*
*5. Learn from every match, win or lose*

"These rules are not negotiable," Professor Vance added, his voice gravelly but not unkind. "Anyone who cannot follow them will be asked to leave and will not be invited back. Dueling is a privilege, not a right. Am I clear?"

A chorus of "Yes, Professor" echoed through the Hall.

"Excellent," Professor Thorne said, her enthusiasm returning. "Now, let's begin with the basics. Everyone spread out—give yourself some space. We're going to start with the Shield Charm."

---

The first hour was less exciting than Zara had hoped—lots of practicing the Shield Charm, *Protego*, which she'd already mastered for Quidditch purposes. But she could see the wisdom in starting with defense. Some of the younger students were struggling with the wand movement, and Professor Thorne moved among them patiently, adjusting grips and correcting incantations.

Elliot, Zara noticed, had gone very still and focused. They were practicing the shield charm with an intensity that bordered on meditation, each cast precise and controlled. The shimmering barrier they produced was textbook-perfect—neither too large nor too small, the energy distributed evenly across its surface.

"Beautiful form, Novak," Professor Vance said, pausing beside them. "Have you studied shielding before?"

"Only what we've learned in Defense Against the Dark Arts," Elliot said. "But my mother's a potioneer. She always says precision matters more than power."

"Your mother is wise. That principle applies to dueling as well." He moved on, but Elliot stood a little straighter, something like pride flickering across their features.

Beside them, Finn was having less success. His shields kept appearing at odd angles, or failing to materialize at all, his wand movement too hesitant.

"You're overthinking it," Mira said, watching him struggle. "It's instinct, not calculation."

"That's easy for you to say," Finn muttered. "You're getting it."

Mira was, in fact, getting it, much to her own surprise. Once she'd stopped worrying about doing it perfectly and just focused on the feeling—the desire to protect, to create a barrier—the spell had clicked. Her shields weren't as elegant as Elliot's, but they were solid and reliable.

"Alright!" Professor Thorne clapped her hands. "Good work, everyone. Now let's move on to something a bit more challenging. We're going to practice *Expelliarmus*—the disarming charm. Pair up, please. We'll practice taking turns—one attacks, one defends."

The Great Hall filled with the sound of shuffling as students found partners. Zara immediately grabbed Mira, leaving Elliot and Finn to work together.

"Go easy on me," Finn said, grinning nervously.

"Always," Elliot replied, but there was a glint in their eye that suggested they were enjoying this more than they'd expected.

---

By the time they'd practiced *Expelliarmus* for twenty minutes, paired with various partners, Elliot had discovered something unexpected: they were good at this. Very good.

It wasn't just the precision—though that helped. It was the tactical aspect, the split-second decision-making, the way dueling required you to read your opponent and anticipate their moves. It reminded them of wizard chess, actually. Planning three steps ahead, looking for openings, protecting your vulnerabilities.

They'd successfully disarmed seven different partners, and been disarmed only once—by a seventh year Ravenclaw who clearly had significant experience.

"You're a natural, Novak," Professor Flitwick squeaked, hovering nearby on a stack of books to observe their match. "Have you considered competitive dueling?"

"I've never really thought about it," Elliot admitted.

"Well, think about it. You have the temperament for it—calm under pressure, strategic thinking. Those are gifts."

As Professor Flitwick moved on, Elliot felt something warm unfold in their chest. They were good at this. Not just competent, but genuinely talented. It felt... significant, somehow. Like discovering a part of themselves they hadn't known existed.

"Alright, everyone!" Professor Thorne called out. "For our final exercise tonight, we're going to have some friendly matches. Pairs will face off—best of three exchanges. Remember: the goal is not to win, but to learn. Control, respect, and precision. Professors will be monitoring and will step in if necessary."

She began calling out pairings, students moving to designated areas of the Hall. Elliot found themselves still partnered with Finn, and they moved to their assigned spot near the western wall.

"Ready?" Finn asked, settling into what he probably thought was a dueling stance. It was a bit too wide, a bit too tense.

"Ready," Elliot said, and they were.

Professor Vance stepped between them, raising his hand. "On my mark. Remember—control is key. Three... two... one... begin!"

Finn moved first, which was a mistake. "*Expelliarmus!*"

Elliot had already cast *Protego*, the shield appearing instantly. Finn's spell bounced off harmlessly. In the same fluid motion, Elliot countered with their own disarming spell, the red light catching Finn squarely in the chest. His wand flew from his hand, and Elliot's free hand snapped up to catch it before it hit the ground.

"Point to Novak," Professor Vance said. "Reset."

Finn retrieved his wand, looking slightly sheepish. "Okay, that was embarrassing."

"You telegraphed your intent," Elliot said, already analyzing. "Your wand movement started before you spoke. I knew what was coming."

"Right. Okay. I'll try to be less obvious."

The second round went similarly. Finn tried to be clever, feinting with his wand before casting, but Elliot saw through it. Another disarming spell, precisely cast, and Finn's wand went flying again.

"Point to Novak. Match to Novak, two-zero."

Around them, other matches were more evenly balanced. Zara and her opponent—a Gryffindor sixth year—were trading successful hits, both laughing when their spells collided mid-air in a shower of sparks. Mira was taking on a Hufflepuff third year, both of them clearly nervous but trying hard.

But Elliot and Finn's match had drawn some attention. Elliot caught a few of their fellow Slytherins watching with approval, and Professor Flitwick was observing with interest.

"Well dueled, Novak," Professor Vance said. "MacLeod, you're being too reactive. Try to anticipate, not just respond. Shall we go another round?"

"Sure," Finn said, but his enthusiasm had dimmed slightly.

This time, Elliot decided to show off just a little. When Finn cast *Expelliarmus*, Elliot didn't just block—they deflected, sending the spell harmlessly into the floor, then followed up with a combination they'd read about in a library book last year: *Protego* to defend, then immediately *Expelliarmus* from behind the shield. It was technically difficult and required precise timing.

It worked perfectly.

Finn's wand flew across the room before he'd even processed what had happened. A few students actually applauded, and Elliot felt a surge of pride that was quickly followed by something else when they saw Finn's face.

Finn looked... deflated. Discouraged. Like he'd failed some test he'd been doomed to fail from the start.

"Excellent technique, Novak!" Professor Flitwick was almost hopping with excitement. "The shield-into-disarm combination—very advanced! I haven't seen a fourth year pull that off in years!"

Elliot nodded, accepting the praise, but they were watching Finn, who was mechanically retrieving his wand from where it had landed near the drinks table.

"Well done," Finn said when he returned, his smile not quite reaching his eyes. "You're really good at this."

"Thanks," Elliot said, and felt the first twinge of guilt.

---

The session wrapped up shortly after, with Professor Thorne praising everyone's efforts and reminding them that next week they'd work on more advanced defensive spells. Students dispersed in chattering groups, energy high from the evening's activities.

Elliot found themselves surrounded by several Slytherins, including Marcus Flint and Daphne Greengrass, both fifth years.

"Novak, that was brilliant," Daphne said. "Where did you learn that combination?"

"Just... reading, mostly," Elliot said. "Magical theory."

"You should compete," Marcus added. "There's an inter-school youth dueling tournament in February. Hogwarts usually sends a team. You'd be a strong candidate."

"Maybe," Elliot said, distracted. They were looking for their friends, and spotted them near the entrance—Zara was gesticulating wildly, clearly recounting some dramatic moment from her matches. Mira was smiling, looking relieved that it was over. And Finn...

Finn was quiet, his hands shoved in his pockets, nodding along but not really participating in the conversation.

"I've got to go," Elliot said abruptly. "But thanks. I'll think about the tournament."

They made their way through the crowd to their friends.

"—and then the spell literally exploded between us!" Zara was saying. "Like fireworks! It was amazing. Professor Thorne said that sometimes happens when two spells of exact equal strength collide. How cool is that?"

"Very cool," Mira agreed. "Though I'm glad my matches were less explosive. I think I won one? Maybe? It was hard to tell."

"You won two," Finn said. "I was watching during my break. You did great."

"Not as great as Elliot," Zara said, turning to them with a huge grin. "Seriously, that was incredible. You absolutely demolished everyone. Did you see Professor Flitwick? I thought he was going to recruit you on the spot."

"It was just practice," Elliot said, feeling increasingly uncomfortable with the praise.

"Just practice?" Zara laughed. "Come on, take the compliment. You were brilliant. Wasn't Elliot brilliant, Finn?"

"Yeah," Finn said, his voice carefully neutral. "Really impressive."

The discomfort in Elliot's chest intensified. This didn't feel right. Winning was supposed to feel good—and it had, for a moment. But now, looking at Finn's forced smile and careful words, it felt hollow. Worse than hollow. It felt mean, even though that hadn't been their intention at all.

"I'm exhausted," Mira said, stifling a yawn. "All that magical energy. I need to get back and probably just sleep. Essay can wait until tomorrow."

"Same," Zara said. "Though I'm more energized than exhausted. That was such a rush! We're all coming back next week, right?"

"Definitely," Mira said.

"Yeah," Finn said, without enthusiasm.

"Of course," Elliot added, though they weren't entirely sure anymore.

They said their goodnights, Zara and Mira heading toward their respective towers together, still chattering. Finn started toward the Hufflepuff common room, and Elliot found themselves falling into step beside him.

"Hey," Elliot said. "Wait up."

Finn slowed, looking over. "What's up?"

"I—" Elliot stopped, trying to find the right words. Apologies didn't come naturally to them, and they weren't even entirely sure what they were apologizing for. "About the matches. I think I got... carried away."

"What do you mean? You did great."

"I know. That's the problem." Elliot ran a hand through their hair, frustrated with their inability to articulate this. "That last round. The combination move. That was me showing off. And you looked—you looked like I'd crushed something."

Finn was quiet for a moment, walking slowly down the corridor. Students passed them, still excited from the evening, but Finn seemed far away from all of it.

"You didn't do anything wrong," he said finally. "You're just good at it. Really good. And I'm... not."

"You're learning," Elliot said. "Everyone's learning. That's the point."

"Yeah, but some people are learning faster than others." Finn's voice was careful, not angry, just... resigned. "I've always been rubbish at defensive magic. Offensive magic too, honestly. I can grow things and identify magical creatures and brew a decent healing potion, but combat magic? I'm useless."

"You're not useless."

"Elliot, you disarmed me three times in under two minutes. Without breaking a sweat. While I stood there like an idiot not even reading your moves correctly."

Elliot winced. "Okay, when you put it that way, it sounds bad."

"It's not bad. It's just... true." Finn sighed, leaning against the stone wall. "And it's not your fault. You found something you're talented at. That's good. I'm happy for you, really. It's just—" He paused, looking for words. "Sometimes it's hard to be the person who's struggling when everyone around you is succeeding. You know?"

Elliot did know. They thought about Potions class, watching their mother's natural talent reflected in their own work while other students struggled. They'd never thought about how that might feel from the other side.

"I do know," they said quietly. "And I'm sorry. Not for being good at it—I'm not going to apologize for that. But for the showing off. For that last combination. That was my pride taking over. My competitive Slytherin nature." They managed a small smile. "I forgot we were supposed to be helping each other learn, not just winning."

Finn looked at them, some of the tension leaving his shoulders. "Your competitive nature is part of who you are. You don't have to apologize for that either."

"Maybe not. But I can choose how I use it." Elliot straightened, making a decision. "Listen, what you said about not reading my moves—that's something that can be taught. It's not about magical power or talent. It's about observation. Pattern recognition. I could... I mean, if you wanted, I could help you. Work with you on defensive casting."

"You'd do that?" Finn looked surprised.

"Why wouldn't I?" Elliot said. "You're my friend. And honestly, teaching someone else will probably make me better too. I'll have to think about why I make certain choices, put it into words. That's useful."

"That's very Slytherin of you," Finn said, but he was smiling now—a real smile. "Finding the benefit for yourself even in helping others."

"I contain multitudes," Elliot said dryly. "So, what do you say? Want a personal dueling tutor?"

"Only if you promise not to go easy on me. I don't want pity. I want to actually get better."

"Deal. No going easy. But also no showing off. I'll push you, but I won't make you feel like an idiot."

Finn extended his hand, and Elliot shook it, the gesture oddly formal but somehow right.

"Thanks," Finn said. "For... all of this. For noticing I was upset, and for caring enough to do something about it. Not everyone would."

"Well," Elliot said, feeling embarrassed again but in a better way, "you're not everyone. You're my friend. And friends don't let friends feel terrible after what's supposed to be a fun club activity."

They started walking again, the tension between them dissolved into something more comfortable.

"Can I ask you something?" Finn said after a moment. "Why do you think you're so good at dueling? Like, what makes it click for you?"

Elliot considered the question. "I think... it's like chess. It's strategy. I can see the board—or in this case, the space between us—and I can see the possibilities. What you might do, what I can do in response, what the outcomes would be. And I can make those decisions fast. It's not really about the magic itself, it's about the thinking."

"Huh." Finn nodded slowly. "That makes sense. For me, magic is more... feeling. Like with Herbology, I don't have to think about what a plant needs, I just sort of know. But combat is all thinking, no feeling. That's probably why I'm terrible at it."

"Maybe," Elliot said. "Or maybe you just need to find a way to make it more intuitive. To feel it instead of thinking it. That's something we can work on."

"You sound like a professor."

"Please never say that again. I'm having an identity crisis as it is."

Finn laughed—a real laugh this time. "Fair enough."

They reached the corridor where their paths would diverge—Finn toward the Hufflepuff common room near the kitchens, Elliot toward the dungeons and Slytherin territory.

"Same time next week?" Finn asked. "Dueling Club?"

"Absolutely. And maybe we can practice one evening before then. Actually work on your defensive casting."

"I'd like that." Finn hesitated, then added, "And Elliot? It really was impressive tonight. You should be proud of yourself. Just... maybe remember that winning isn't everything."

"Says the person whose best friend is a Quidditch captain," Elliot pointed out.

"Zara would also benefit from that reminder," Finn said seriously. "I'm an equal-opportunity advice giver."

"Noted. And Finn?" Elliot caught his eye. "You're not useless. You're one of the kindest, most perceptive people I know. You noticed I was getting too competitive before I did. That's its own kind of strength."

Finn's ears went a bit red. "Thanks. Right. Well. Good night, then."

"Good night."

Elliot watched him head down the corridor, his distinctive reddish hair visible until he turned the corner. Then they started toward the Slytherin common room, thinking about the evening—about winning and losing, about friendship and competition, about the line between healthy confidence and crushing someone's spirit.

Professor Thorne had said dueling was about control, respect, and precision. Elliot had focused on the control and precision. But the respect—that was about more than just following the rules. It was about seeing your opponent as a whole person, not just an obstacle to defeat.

They'd remember that next time. Would still try to win, because that was who they were. But would remember that *how* you won mattered just as much as *if* you won.

---

The next morning at breakfast, Elliot found their friends at the Ravenclaw table—something they'd all taken to doing, rotating between houses rather than always sitting separately.

"Morning," they said, sliding onto the bench beside Mira.

"Morning!" Zara said cheerfully through a mouthful of toast. "Still buzzing from last night. I've been reading about dueling techniques. Did you know there are entire books just about wand grip and how it affects spell trajectory?"

"I did, actually," Mira said. "I checked three of them out this morning."

"Of course you did," Elliot said fondly.

Finn arrived a few minutes later, looking more rested than he had the night before. He grabbed breakfast and dropped into the seat across from Elliot.

"So I was thinking," he said. "If you're serious about helping me with defensive casting, maybe we could start tomorrow? Sunday afternoon? I can book one of the empty classrooms."

"Sounds good," Elliot said. "Come prepared to work. I'm a strict teacher."

"I'm terrified," Finn said, not sounding terrified at all.

"As you should be," Elliot replied.

Zara looked between them, clearly sensing something had shifted but not sure what. "Everything okay with you two?"

"Everything's good," Finn said.

"Better than good," Elliot added. "We had a good talk last night. Cleared some things up."

"That's what friends do," Mira said, looking pleased. "Talk about difficult things instead of just letting them fester."

"When did you become a relationship counselor?" Zara asked.

"I read books," Mira said primly. "They contain wisdom."

"They contain words," Elliot corrected. "Wisdom is what you do with the words."

"Which is why I'm helping Finn improve his defensive casting," Elliot said, "instead of just feeling smug about being good at dueling."

"You can do both," Finn said. "I don't mind a little smugness. You earned it."

"See?" Elliot gestured at him. "This is why Finn's the best. He gives permission for smugness."

"In small doses," Finn clarified.

"Naturally."

They ate breakfast together, talking about classes and essays and weekend plans. The morning sun streamed through the enchanted ceiling—a clear December day, cold but bright. Students came and went, the Great Hall filled with the comfortable noise of Saturday morning at Hogwarts.

And at their table, four friends sat together—learning about competition and kindness, about winning and losing, about how to be talented without being cruel, and how to struggle without giving up. Small lessons, maybe, but important ones.

The kind that would matter long after the Dueling Club ended for the term.

The kind that, like Mira's frost message and Finn's hot chocolate evening, were really about paying attention to each other. About seeing not just the magic, but the people using it.

About being human—or as human as possible—in a world full of wonder.

Elliot thought about that as they finished their toast, watching their friends debate the merits of different shielding charms. About how easy it would have been to just walk away last night, to bask in the praise and ignore Finn's quiet disappointment.

But that wasn't the kind of person they wanted to be. And their friends—these three people who'd somehow become essential to their daily life—deserved better than that.

So they'd chosen differently. Had chosen to see. Had chosen to care.

And that, Elliot thought, reaching for more jam, was probably its own kind of magic. The kind that didn't require a wand at all.

Just intention. Just attention. Just being willing to show up, even when—especially when—it was uncomfortable.

"What are you thinking about?" Mira asked, noticing their distraction.

"Just that I'm glad I signed up for Dueling Club," Elliot said truthfully. "Even if my reasons for being glad are different than I expected."

"Sometimes the best things come from unexpected places," Mira said.

"Is that in one of your books?"

"Probably. Everything's in books if you look hard enough."

"Even instructions for being a decent human being?"

"Especially those," Mira said seriously. "Though I find the best lessons usually come from actually paying attention to people."

"Wise words," Finn said.

"I have my moments," Mira replied.

And in the warmth of the Great Hall, surrounded by friends who challenged them to be better, Elliot smiled. They'd found something they were good at—really good at. But more importantly, they'd found the wisdom to know that being good at something was less important than how you treated the people around you while doing it.

That was the real victory.

The dueling just made it more fun.
