The path down to Hagrid's hut was slippery with fresh snow, and Mira had to watch her footing as the class made their way across the grounds. The December morning was grey and cold, the kind of weather that made her grateful for the warming charm she'd cast on her robes before leaving the castle.

"Where d'you think we're going?" Finn asked, his breath making clouds in the cold air. "We're passing the paddocks."

"Maybe the forest?" Zara suggested, adjusting her Gryffindor scarf. "He mentioned something last week about winter being a good time to observe certain creatures."

Elliot, walking beside them with their hands shoved deep in their pockets, said nothing. They'd been quieter than usual during breakfast, Mira had noticed, though she hadn't wanted to pry.

Hagrid was waiting for them at the edge of the Forbidden Forest, his massive frame somehow even more imposing against the dark trees. His beetle-black eyes were crinkled with excitement, and he bounced slightly on the balls of his feet—never a good sign if you valued self-preservation, but usually a guarantee that the lesson would be interesting.

"Right then, gather 'round!" he called as the last of the fourth years arrived. "Today we've got somethin' special. Now, I know some o' yeh might've heard of Thestrals before, maybe seen the carriages that bring yeh up from the station pulled by 'em."

A ripple of interest went through the class. Mira had always wondered what pulled the carriages, though she'd assumed it was some kind of invisible magic rather than actual creatures.

"The thing about Thestrals," Hagrid continued, his voice taking on a more serious tone, "is that not everyone can see 'em. Only people who've seen death can see Thestrals. An' I don't mean just knowin' someone died—I mean witnessed it, understood it, processed it."

The mood of the class shifted immediately. Some students looked uncomfortable. Others, like Dennis Pritchard, looked confused and a bit scared.

"Now, that don't make 'em evil or bad luck or any o' that nonsense," Hagrid said firmly. "They're gentle creatures, Thestrals are. Loyal, intelligent, an' useful too—they've got an excellent sense of direction. But they've got an unfortunate reputation on account of their appearance and what yeh have to go through to see 'em."

He turned and gave a low whistle, and from deeper in the forest came the sound of movement.

"How many of you can see them?" a Slytherin girl named Miranda asked nervously.

"We'll find out," Hagrid said. "Don't be ashamed either way. Ain't got nothin' to do with courage or character. Just life experience."

The creature that emerged from between the trees made several students gasp—though whether from seeing it or from the strangeness of something invisible moving through the snow, Mira couldn't tell.

She couldn't see anything. Just the disturbed snow, the tracks of hooves, and the way Hagrid's hand reached out to stroke something at about horse-height.

"Can you see it?" she whispered to Finn.

He shook his head, looking both relieved and curious. "You?"

"No."

But beside them, Zara had gone very still. And Elliot had taken a step forward, their expression transformed from distant quietness to something like wonder.

"Oh," Elliot breathed. "I'd forgotten how beautiful they are."

Mira turned to look at them in surprise. Elliot was staring at the empty space beside Hagrid—no, not empty. Not to them.

"You can see it?" Finn asked gently.

Elliot nodded, not looking away. "My grandmother. Two years ago."

Zara spoke up too, her voice steadier but with an undercurrent of old grief. "My uncle. When I was ten."

Hagrid nodded approvingly. "Right then. So we've got—" he looked around the class, counting, "—looks like five o' yeh can see 'em properly. That's about usual for a class this size. For those of yeh who can't see Thestrals, don't worry. We're all gonna work together today."

He gestured, and the creature—or creatures, Mira couldn't tell how many there were—moved into the clearing. Other students were reacting now. Miranda could clearly see them too, and a Hufflepuff boy Mira didn't know well. A Ravenclaw sixth year who'd joined their class was nodding with recognition.

"Now," Hagrid said, "I'm gonna have those of yeh who can see the Thestrals partner up with those who can't. We're gonna practice feeding 'em and caring for 'em, but it's gonna require trust. If yeh can't see where the creature is, yeh need to trust yer partner to guide yeh."

He pulled out a bucket that made Mira's nose wrinkle—raw meat, from the smell of it.

"Thestrals eat meat, they do. Now don't be afraid if yeh can't see 'em. They're gentle, and they won't hurt yeh. But I want yeh all to experience interacting with 'em, even if yeh can't see 'em with yer eyes."

The class began to divide up naturally. Zara touched Mira's arm. "Want to partner with me?"

"Yes," Mira said immediately, grateful for the familiar face.

Nearby, Elliot was already approaching Finn. "Come on," they said quietly. "I'll show you."

Hagrid distributed chunks of meat, showing them how to hold it flat on their palms so the Thestrals could take it gently. Mira watched as the students who could see the creatures began to move into what looked like empty space, but must be wherever the Thestrals were standing.

"Okay," Zara said, taking Mira's hand. "There are three of them in the clearing. We're going to approach the one on the left—she's smaller, probably younger. She has her head down right now, like she's shy."

Mira let Zara guide her forward, feeling ridiculous walking toward nothing but trusting her friend completely. The snow crunched under their feet.

"Hold out your hand," Zara instructed. "Palm flat, meat in the center. Good. Now just... hold steady."

Mira stood there, arm extended, feeling foolish and fascinated in equal measure. Then—

Something warm and slightly damp touched her palm. She gasped as the meat was lifted away by something she couldn't see, and then there was a sensation like velvet brushing against her hand, followed by what felt distinctly like a large animal's breath warm against her skin.

"She's eating," Zara said, smiling. "And now she's nuzzling your hand. I think she likes you."

Mira laughed breathlessly. "This is so strange. I can feel her, but—"

"I know. But she's real, even if you can't see her. She's standing right in front of you. Black coat, white eyes, wings folded against her sides."

"Wings?"

"They're part horse, part dragon, part something else entirely. Skeletal, but graceful. Trust me, they're less frightening than people think."

Across the clearing, Elliot was guiding Finn through the same process. Mira watched as Finn held out his hand nervously, then broke into a delighted grin when the invisible creature took the meat.

"That's amazing!" Finn exclaimed. "I can feel—is that a nose?"

"Muzzle," Elliot corrected gently. "And yes. You can pet him if you want. Just move slowly."

Finn reached out cautiously, his hand appearing to stroke empty air. But his expression was one of pure wonder. "His coat feels different than I expected. Softer."

"They're surprisingly warm too," Elliot said. "Despite being so associated with death, they're very much alive."

There was something in Elliot's voice—a tenderness that Mira rarely heard. They were still guiding Finn, but their other hand had reached out to touch the Thestral themselves, and their expression had gone distant and thoughtful.

Hagrid was moving through the class, offering encouragement and corrections. When he reached Elliot and Finn, he paused.

"Doin' well there," he rumbled approvingly. "Elliot, yeh've got a good touch with 'em. Natural, like."

"My grandmother loved them," Elliot said quietly. "When I first saw them—the Thestrals pulling the carriages at the start of third year—I was frightened. Everything felt frightening then. But she would have been fascinated by them. She always loved creatures that other people misunderstood."

"Sounds like she was a wise woman," Hagrid said gently.

"She was." Elliot's hand continued stroking the invisible creature. "She died of dragon pox. I was there at the end, at St. Mungo's. It was terrible and peaceful at the same time, if that makes sense. She told me she wasn't afraid. That death was just another journey."

Finn had turned to face Elliot fully now, listening. Nearby, Mira and Zara had stopped their own work with the Thestral, giving Elliot space to speak but staying close enough to hear.

"Seein' death changes yeh," Hagrid said, his voice unusually soft. "Ain't no way around that. But what yeh do with that change—that's up to you. Some people let it make 'em hard or afraid. Others let it teach 'em to value what they've got while they've got it."

"I think," Elliot said slowly, "seeing the Thestrals made it real in a way it hadn't been before. Like, I knew she was gone, but when I got off the train and saw the carriages being pulled by these creatures I'd never seen before—creatures that only appear when you've crossed that particular threshold—it hit me. Really hit me. That I was different now. That I'd experienced something I couldn't un-experience."

The Thestral Elliot was touching turned its invisible head—Mira could track the movement by the way Elliot's hand shifted—and seemed to nuzzle against them.

"But they're not frightening," Elliot continued, voice stronger now. "Not once you understand them. They're just... honest. They don't pretend death doesn't exist. They acknowledge it. Live alongside it. And there's something comforting about that."

Zara spoke up from beside Mira. "My uncle died in a car accident. Muggle car—he was visiting my mother's non-magical family. I was in the car too, in the back seat. I was ten."

Mira reached for her friend's hand instinctively.

"I saw the Thestrals when I started at Hogwarts," Zara continued. "And I hated them at first. They reminded me of the worst day of my life. But over the years, I've realized they're not symbols of death. They're symbols of having survived it. Of being strong enough to see it and keep going."

"That's beautiful," Finn said quietly. "I'm glad I can't see them—I wouldn't want to have gone through what you both have. But I'm glad they exist for you. If that makes sense."

"It does," Elliot said. They finally looked up from the Thestral, meeting Finn's eyes and then Mira's and Zara's. "And I'm glad I have friends who listen without trying to fix things or make it less hard than it was."

For a moment, the four of them stood in the snowy clearing, connected by invisible threads of understanding. Around them, the rest of the class continued working with the Thestrals, but it felt like the four of them existed in their own small bubble of intimacy.

Hagrid cleared his throat—not impatiently, but gently bringing them back to the present. "Right then. Let's practice leadin' 'em around a bit. Those of yeh who can see 'em, try guidin' them while yer partners walk alongside."

The rest of the lesson passed in a blur of activity. Mira walked beside a creature she couldn't see but could feel—the displacement of air, the warmth of a large body, the occasional brush of wing or flank against her shoulder. Zara guided them around the clearing, her voice steady and sure.

When Hagrid finally called the end of class, Mira felt strange—a mixture of exhilaration and melancholy she couldn't quite name.

"That was incredible," she said to Zara as they trudged back up toward the castle. "Thank you for trusting me to work with her."

"Of course," Zara said. "And thank you for trusting me to guide you. I know it must have felt weird, following someone toward something you couldn't see."

"That's what friendship is though, isn't it?" Mira said thoughtfully. "Trusting each other to guide us through things we can't see or understand on our own."

"Getting philosophical on me, Patel?"

"It's the Ravenclaw in me. I can't help it."

Finn and Elliot caught up with them, Finn still chattering excitedly about the experience. "Did you feel how soft they were? I expected something skeletal to feel rough or cold, but—"

"They're living creatures," Elliot said, but they were smiling. "Soft and warm and very real."

"I want to read more about them," Finn declared. "Hagrid said they have an incredible sense of direction. Imagine having a mount that could find its way anywhere, even places you'd never been."

"Harry Potter flew to London on one during the war," Mira said. "I read about it in a history book. He trusted it to take him where he needed to go."

"See? Philosophy again."

They were almost to the castle when Elliot stopped suddenly. The other three paused, turning back.

"Thank you," Elliot said, looking at each of them in turn. "For listening. About my grandmother. I don't talk about her much, and it felt... it felt good. To share that with you."

"You can talk about her anytime," Finn said immediately. "We'd like to hear about her."

"What was she like?" Mira asked. "Besides loving misunderstood creatures."

Elliot's expression softened. "She was brilliant. Literally—she was a researcher for the Department of Mysteries. But she was also funny, in this dry, subtle way. She taught me to play wizard chess when I was six, and she never let me win. Said I'd never learn properly if she did."

"Sounds like you get your chess skills honestly then," Zara said. "And probably your sense of humor too."

"Definitely the sense of humor," Elliot agreed. They started walking again, but slower, more thoughtful. "She was also the first person I talked to about... about not feeling like just a boy. She didn't make a big deal out of it. Just asked what I wanted to be called and used those words from then on. Like it was the simplest thing in the world."

"It should be that simple," Finn said quietly.

"Yeah," Elliot agreed. "It should be."

They reached the castle doors, where warmth and the smell of lunch cooking wafted out to meet them. The mood lifted naturally, the weight of the morning's revelations settling into something more comfortable. They'd touched on grief and loss and identity, but they'd done it together, and somehow that made it manageable.

"I'm starving," Zara announced. "Think they'll have those meat pies again?"

"I hope so," Finn said. "After handling raw meat for the Thestrals, I could definitely eat."

"That's a concerning sentence without context," Elliot observed.

As they filed into the entrance hall with the rest of their class, Mira glanced back over her shoulder at the grounds. The forest edge where they'd spent the morning with the Thestrals—visible and invisible—looked peaceful in the grey winter light.

She thought about trust. About seeing and being seen. About how some things were invisible to some people but perfectly clear to others, and how that didn't make them any less real. About grief and how it changed you, marked you, let you see things others couldn't.

About how the best response to invisible pain was visible friendship.

"Come on, Mira," Finn called. "You're doing that thinking thing again."

"I'm always doing the thinking thing," she protested, but she hurried to catch up with them.

In the Great Hall, they claimed their usual spot—a mix of house tables that had stopped feeling strange and started feeling like home. As they ate and talked and laughed, Mira caught Elliot's eye across the table. They gave her a small smile, and she smiled back.

Some creatures were invisible until you'd been through something that changed you fundamentally. Some experiences were like that too—invisible to people who hadn't lived them. But that didn't mean people couldn't support each other across that divide. Couldn't trust each other, guide each other, acknowledge each other's invisible weights and visible strengths.

Outside, snow began to fall again, and somewhere in the forest, Thestrals moved through the trees—seen by some, invisible to others, real to all.

Just like grief. Just like growth. Just like the bonds of friendship that didn't need to be visible to be unbreakable.

---

That evening, Mira added another entry to her December journal:

*December 9th: Today I learned to trust what I cannot see. I learned that grief makes some things visible that others cannot perceive, but that doesn't make those who see them broken—it makes them marked by survival. I learned that Elliot's grandmother loved misunderstood creatures, and that Zara has carried loss since she was ten years old, and that both of them are stronger and gentler for what they've experienced.*

*I learned that Thestrals are soft and warm and real, even when invisible. And I learned that sometimes the best thing you can do for a friend is to walk beside them through things you cannot see, trusting them to guide you, letting them know they're not alone.*

*Magic is not always about spells and wands. Sometimes it's about showing up. About listening. About acknowledging that we all carry invisible things, and choosing to believe in each other anyway.*

*That feels like the most powerful magic of all.*

She closed the journal and looked out her dormitory window at the December night. No magical frost tonight, just regular darkness and the distant lights of Hagrid's hut far below.

But that was okay. Some magic happened in big, visible ways—frost messages and Thestrals. And some magic happened in the quiet, invisible spaces between people who chose to understand each other.

Both kinds mattered. Both kinds were real.

And both kinds made Hogwarts, and December, and this strange beautiful fourth year, feel like exactly where she was meant to be.
