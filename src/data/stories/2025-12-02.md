---
title: "The Niffler's Hoard"
date: "2025-12-02"
---

Finn MacLeod had always found greenhouses peaceful. Even on the coldest December morning, the air inside Greenhouse Three was warm and humid, thick with the scent of dragon dung fertilizer and the sweeter notes of flowering Flutterby bushes. His hands were already dirty from repotting a batch of Bouncing Bulbs for Professor Sprout, and he had two more hours of volunteer work ahead of him before his first class.

He didn't mind. In fact, he preferred it this way—arriving early, working alone with the plants, the quiet broken only by the occasional rustle of leaves or the soft patter of the enchanted watering system. It gave him time to think, or more accurately, time to not think. Just to be.

He was reaching for another pot when he heard it: a high-pitched, distressed squeaking coming from somewhere near the greenhouse's foundation.

Finn froze, listening. There it was again—definitely not a plant sound. He set down his trowel and followed the noise to the back corner where the greenhouse met the castle's stone wall. The squeaking was louder here, more frantic, coming from a drainage grate set low in the wall.

Finn crouched down, peering through the grate. Two beady black eyes stared back at him from the darkness.

"Oh no," Finn said. "Biscuit?"

The eyes blinked. The squeaking intensified.

Biscuit was something of a legend among the students—a Niffler who had taken up residence somewhere on the grounds and had an unfortunate habit of "borrowing" anything shiny that wasn't nailed down. Finn had seen him a few times, usually fleeing the scene of some minor theft with his pouch bulging. Professor Sprout insisted Biscuit was harmless, just following his instincts, though that was cold comfort to the students who'd had jewelry or Galleons go missing.

"How did you even get in there?" Finn asked.

Biscuit squeaked miserably in response.

Finn tried the grate, but it was firmly attached, and the gaps between the bars were too narrow for the Niffler to squeeze through—though he'd clearly managed to get in somehow, probably when he was less... plump. His pouch looked particularly full.

"Alright," Finn said. "Hold on. I'm going to get help."

---

"So let me get this straight," Zara said twenty minutes later, arms crossed as she stared at the drainage grate. "You want us to help rescue the creature who stole my good quill last week?"

"He didn't steal it," Finn protested. "He borrowed it. Nifflers can't help themselves."

"My quill had a gold nib, Finn. It's gone. That's stealing."

"Please?" Finn gave her his most earnest look. "He sounds really distressed."

Zara sighed, but she was already pulling out her wand. "Fine. But if we get him out and he runs off with my House pin, I'm hexing him."

"I'll help with that," Elliot said, arriving with Mira in tow. They looked slightly annoyed at being summoned before breakfast, but curious enough to show up. "What's the situation?"

Finn explained quickly. Mira immediately knelt by the grate, examining the mechanism. "It's held with a permanent sticking charm, but there should be a release. Give me a moment."

While Mira worked, Elliot peered through the grate at Biscuit. "He's really wedged in there. How full is that pouch?"

"Very," Finn said. "Which is probably why he can't get back out the way he came in."

"Typical," Zara muttered, but she was smiling slightly now.

"Got it," Mira announced. The grate swung open with a rusty creak.

Biscuit didn't immediately emerge. Instead, he backed further into the pipe, squeaking suspiciously.

"He doesn't trust us," Finn said. "Can't blame him, really. We probably look like giants from his perspective."

"So how do we—" Elliot started, but Finn was already lying flat on his stomach, reaching carefully into the pipe.

"Hey, buddy," he said softly. "It's okay. We're not going to hurt you. Promise."

For a long moment, nothing happened. Then slowly, cautiously, Biscuit's long snout emerged from the darkness. Finn stayed very still, hand extended. The Niffler sniffed his fingers, then his silver rings—Elliot's loan from last week that Finn kept forgetting to return—and his eyes lit up with interest.

"Oh no you don't," Finn said, but gently. "Those aren't for you. Come on, let's get you out of there."

With surprising gentleness for a creature known for chaos, Biscuit climbed onto Finn's arm. He was heavier than expected, his pouch absolutely bulging. Finn carefully backed out of the pipe, cradling the Niffler against his chest.

"There we go," Finn said. "See? That wasn't so bad."

Biscuit chirped, then immediately tried to investigate Zara's House pin.

"Absolutely not," Zara said, taking a step back.

But Biscuit wasn't trying to steal it. Instead, he wriggled out of Finn's arms and started waddling purposefully across the greenhouse floor, pausing every few steps to look back at them expectantly.

"I think," Mira said slowly, "he wants us to follow him."

"You're joking," Elliot said.

Biscuit squeaked insistently and kept walking.

"Well," Finn said, brushing dirt off his robes, "we've come this far."

---

Biscuit led them on a winding path through the castle that none of them had known existed. First through a narrow passage behind a tapestry on the second floor, then down a spiral staircase that seemed too small for human students, then along what must have been old servant corridors from when the castle was first built.

"This is either really cool or the beginning of a horror story," Zara muttered as they squeezed through a particularly tight archway.

"Could be both," Elliot offered.

Finally, Biscuit stopped in front of what looked like a dead end—just blank stone wall in a forgotten corridor deep in the castle's foundations. He pawed at a particular stone, squeaking urgently.

"There must be a door," Mira said. She ran her hands over the wall, feeling for catches or seams. "Revelio," she tried, and a faint outline appeared in the stone. "There. It's hidden, but not locked. Just... forgotten, I think."

Finn pushed, and with a grinding sound, a section of wall swung inward, revealing a small room beyond.

"Merlin's beard," Zara breathed.

The room was maybe ten feet square, with a low vaulted ceiling. And every surface—the floor, the stone benches, the windowsill of a narrow window that must open somewhere in the castle's exterior—was covered in treasure.

Except "treasure" was a generous term. There were Galleons and Sickles, yes, but also buttons—hundreds of buttons—along with quills, pins, buckles, bits of jewelry, lost watches, coins from various eras, and countless other shiny objects. It was a dragon's hoard in miniature, sorted into what must have been some organizational system comprehensible only to a Niffler.

Biscuit waddled to the center of the room and began emptying his pouch, adding his latest acquisitions to their respective piles with what could only be described as pride.

"This is incredible," Mira said, stepping carefully around a collection of what appeared to be medieval brass buttons. "Some of this stuff must be centuries old."

"That's my quill," Zara said, pointing.

"And someone's prefect badge," Elliot added. "Several someones, actually."

Finn was examining a pile of lockets and chains, each one carefully arranged. One caught his eye—silver, with delicate engraving, older than the rest. He picked it up carefully.

"Look at this," he said. The locket was tarnished but beautiful, with a pattern of leaves etched into its surface. He opened it carefully. Inside was a photo, moving in the way wizarding photos did, showing a group of young witches in old-fashioned Hogwarts robes, laughing together.

"That style of uniform..." Mira leaned in. "That's from at least fifty years ago. Maybe sixty."

"Should we take it?" Finn asked. "I mean, it clearly meant something to someone."

"Biscuit brought us here for a reason," Elliot pointed out. "Maybe this is it. Maybe he wants us to return things."

Biscuit squeaked and nudged the locket with his snout.

"I think you're right," Finn said. He closed the locket carefully and slipped it into his pocket. "We'll find out who it belonged to."

---

Finding the owner turned out to be easier than expected. Madam Oakwood, the librarian, took one look at the locket when they approached her after breakfast and went completely still.

"Where did you find this?" Her voice was barely a whisper.

"In the castle," Finn said carefully. "Hidden away. We thought... we thought someone might have lost it."

Madam Oakwood took the locket with trembling hands. She opened it, looked at the photo inside, and her eyes filled with tears.

"I lost this in my sixth year," she said softly. "1968. I was studying in the library—this library—and I took it off while I was writing an essay because the chain kept getting tangled in my quill. I set it down, and when I looked for it later, it was gone. I thought..." She shook her head. "I thought it was gone forever."

"Who are they?" Mira asked gently, nodding to the photo.

"My friends," Madam Oakwood said. "My best friends from school. Margaret—that's Margaret Chen, she was a Ravenclaw, brilliant witch. She did that study on magical frost patterns. And Sarah, and Diana. We were inseparable." She smiled, though tears were still streaming down her face. "Sarah passed away ten years ago. Diana lives in Australia now. We still write, but I haven't seen her in person in ages. And Margaret... Margaret works for the Department of Mysteries. I see her at Christmas sometimes."

She looked up at the four students standing before her. "Thank you," she said. "You can't know what this means. It's like... it's like having a piece of them back. Of who we were."

"I think we understand a little," Finn said, glancing at his friends.

Madam Oakwood studied them for a moment, then smiled through her tears. "Yes," she said. "I think perhaps you do."

---

They didn't tell Madam Oakwood about Biscuit's hoard, not entirely. It seemed like the kind of secret that should remain secret, at least mostly. But they did make a plan, the four of them, to visit the hidden room periodically and check for things that clearly meant something to someone—photographs, engraved items, things that were treasures in more than just the shiny sense.

"Biscuit's actually doing something kind of beautiful," Mira said as they walked to their morning classes. "He's preserving bits of people's stories. He doesn't know they're stories, obviously. To him they're just shiny things. But to us..."

"To us they're memories," Finn finished. "Connections."

"Still want my quill back, though," Zara said, but she was smiling.

"Fair enough," Elliot said. "I saw at least three quills in there. We'll mount a retrieval mission."

That evening, Finn returned to the hidden room alone, just to check on Biscuit. The Niffler was curled up in a nest made of particularly shiny buttons, fast asleep. Finn had brought an offering—a handful of brass Knuts that he'd polished until they gleamed—and he left them in a pile near Biscuit's favorite collection.

"Thanks, buddy," he said softly. "For sharing your treasures with us."

Biscuit cracked one eye open, saw the Knuts, and made a contented chirping sound before going back to sleep.

Finn smiled and headed back through the winding passages toward the main castle. Above him, Hogwarts settled into its evening rhythms—students finishing homework, ghosts drifting through corridors, the castle itself breathing with centuries of magic and memory.

And in a forgotten room in the foundations, a Niffler slept surrounded by treasures, each one a tiny piece of someone's story, preserved and protected. Not stolen. Saved.

Finn thought about Madam Oakwood's face when she'd opened the locket. He thought about his own family heirlooms—the Remembrall his dad had given him, the pressed flowers his mum saved from their garden. Objects were just objects, until they weren't. Until they became vessels for love and memory and connection.

Maybe that was the real magic, he thought. Not the spells or the potions or even the castle itself, but the way certain things could carry pieces of people forward through time. The way a silver locket could hold friendship decades after the photo was taken. The way a Niffler's hoard could become a treasure trove of stories.

He made a mental note to write home this weekend, to tell his parents he loved them and appreciated the things they'd passed down. And maybe to be a little more careful with his family Remembrall. Not because it was fragile—though it was—but because it was a story worth protecting.

As Finn emerged into the main corridor, he nearly walked straight into Professor Sprout.

"Mr. MacLeod," she said, eyebrows raised. "You're looking remarkably pleased with yourself."

"Just thinking about treasures, Professor," Finn said.

Professor Sprout's eyes twinkled. "The best kind of thinking. Off to dinner with you, then."

Finn grinned and headed toward the Great Hall, where his friends would be waiting. Where new stories were being made every day, small and shining and worth saving.

Just like Biscuit knew all along.
