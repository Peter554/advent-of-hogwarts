# Writing Style

How the stories should read.

## Audience and tone

- Young adult reader. Warm, hopeful, gently funny; wonder is treated as part of everyday life.
- **No violence, threats, peril played for fear, or sexual content.** Mishaps and tension are fine; danger is not the point.
- Humor comes from character and is always kind.
- Diversity and LGBTQ+ themes are part of the characters' lives and treated as normal: present in some stories, never the whole of every story, never sensationalized or lectured about.

## Voice

- **Point of view:** third person limited, staying with one character per scene. Rotate point-of-view characters across the month so no one dominates.
- **Language:**
  - British English spelling and punctuation.
  - British characters still say British things in dialogue (Mum, jumper, term).
- **Show, don't tell.** Carry emotion through action, objects, setting and subtext. **State the lesson at most once.** Generally avoid a closing paragraph that summarizes the moral.
- **Dialogue:** natural and specific to each speaker, and how teenagers actually talk. Avoid speeches. Characters should sometimes disagree, misunderstand, joke at the wrong moment or say the wrong thing.
- **Distinct voices:**
  - **Mira:** precise, curious, over-qualifies her sentences.
  - **Finn:** warm, plain-spoken, uses plant and creature metaphors.
  - **Zara:** direct, energetic, teasing.
  - **Elliot:** dry, clipped, deflects with wit.
- **Concrete detail over abstraction:** sensory winter atmosphere (cold stone, candle smoke, snow on wool), real Hogwarts places and lessons. Spend description on the story's central magical idea, not on general coziness.
- **Lore:** use the Harry Potter world as texture. Don't explain it to the reader.
