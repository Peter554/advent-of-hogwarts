# Learnings from 2025

A review of the 24 stories published in December 2025, to help the 2026 calendar do better. The 2025 stories are being left as published, including their imperfections. Don't edit them; learn from them.

## What worked well

- **A small, consistent cast with distinct strengths.** Mira (theory), Finn (intuition, living things), Zara (courage, leadership) and Elliot (precision, strategy). Stories where these differences _combine_ to solve a problem were among the best: Dec 11 (Transfiguration), Dec 15 (Greenhouse Seven) and Dec 22 (Scavenger Hunt).
- **Small, concrete magical premises.** Runic frost, a Niffler's hoard, a potion that only clears when you admit a truth, a library alcove full of decades of marginalia, a singing suit of armor. These gave each story a clear hook and a reason to exist.
- **The quietest moments landed hardest.** Finn's dad's letter (Dec 8), the margin notes (Dec 10), the Thestral clearing (Dec 9) and Sinistra's tea on the tower (Dec 7). Emotion carried by a concrete object or scene beat emotion stated outright.
- **Each character's arc developed across the month:**
  - Mira, from perfectionism to presence.
  - Elliot, through career doubt, coming out, and their art.
  - Finn, through the Remembrall, to coming out to Zara and asking Oliver.
  - Zara, learning to lead by trusting her team.
- **The identity themes were handled warmly and without drama.** The friends react to Elliot's and Finn's coming-out with simple acceptance and careful questions. This matched the reader's interests without making every story about identity.
- **Variety of story types.** Mysteries (frost, Niffler), a sports match, classroom challenges, creature lessons, a ghost, a prank, a near-disaster, festive set pieces and quiet character pieces.
- **Callbacks that reward a daily reader.** The frost motto, the library alcove reused in the scavenger hunt, the Remembrall in Peeves's song, and the aurora at the ball echoing the frost runes.
- **Rooting in canon without leaning on it.** Hagrid, McGonagall, Sprout, Slughorn, the Grey Lady, Peeves, Thestrals and Dobby's legacy are all used as texture around original characters.
- **Traditions the friends created** (the solstice pact, the library note, "the Wonder Seekers"). These give 2026 natural things to return to.

## What didn't work well

### Length

The plan targeted 1,200–2,400 words (5–10 minutes). The stories averaged about **3,150 words**; only 3 of 24 were under 2,400, and the longest (Dec 6) was about 4,300. The excess was mostly repetition and moralizing, not plot. **Respect the word limit.**

### Repetitive endings and phrasing

- 14 of 24 stories mention Mira's journal; most _end_ with a journal entry that restates the story's lesson.
- Several end with "in three other dormitories, three other students…" or "four friends… exactly where they belonged".
- "Seek wonder in small things" appears in 9 stories, and "December magic" / "that was its own kind of magic" in many more.
- Overused phrases: "something settled in her chest", "felt something crack open", "the best kind of magic", "That's what friends do", "Very Ravenclaw of you", "I have my moments", "When did you get so wise?"

### Telling instead of showing

Most stories state their moral two or three times: in dialogue, in narration, _and_ in a closing journal entry. Characters often deliver therapy-style speeches ("vulnerability isn't weakness", "you're allowed to want whatever you want"). Trust the reader.

### Low friction

Every conflict resolved fully within the same story, usually within a few pages. The friends almost never disagree, misunderstand each other, or stay annoyed. Real stakes and small, lasting tensions would make the warmth more earned.

### Mira-centric

Mira narrates or frames a large share of the stories, and her journal is the default device. Zara gets the fewest point-of-view stories. Spread point of view more evenly.

### Romance

Finn and Oliver's story was sweet but told three times (see below). Mira and Priya's spark was introduced late (Dec 16) and only began at the ball. Give relationships room to develop across the month.

### Canon and anachronism

2025 is about 27 years after the Battle of Hogwarts, but the stories used people who can't be students or kitchen elves then:

- Colin Creevey, who died in 1998, as the ball photographer.
- The Patil twins at the Yule Ball as students.
- Marcus Flint and Daphne Greengrass as fifth years.
- Winky as a "young" kitchen elf.

Staff who stayed on (McGonagall, Flitwick, Sprout, Slughorn, Sinistra, Hagrid) are fine, but double-check anyone else's age and timeline.

### Over-explaining

Some stories explain Harry Potter lore or identity concepts at length. The reader knows the world.

## Inconsistencies found in 2025

These are left as published.

### Plot and timeline

1. **Finn's coming-out and asking Oliver happens three times.**
   - Dec 13: Finn has _already_ told Zara he's gay, and Oliver, a "fifth year", has _already_ said yes.
   - Dec 16: Finn comes out to Zara for the first time, Oliver is "in our year", and he hasn't asked yet.
   - Dec 20: Finn asks Oliver, and Oliver says yes.
   - Also Dec 17: Mira thinks about Finn "telling Zara about Oliver", which she can't know yet.
   - _Use Dec 16 and Dec 20 as the real events; Oliver is in Finn's year._
2. **Elliot comes out twice.**
   - Dec 9: they tell the friends their grandmother was the first person they talked to about "not feeling like just a boy", and that she asked what they wanted to be called.
   - Dec 12: they come out as non-binary and the friends react as if it's new.
   - _Use Dec 12._
3. **The solstice story contradicts the Yule Ball.** The ball is on Dec 21, the solstice. The Dec 19 story is _set_ on "December 21st, the winter solstice", says "Two more days until the Yule Ball", and dates its journal entry and pledge Dec 21. _2026: decide the date of every big event before writing._
4. **The frost's seven-year cycle doesn't fit.** Margaret Chen saw it in 1973, and Dec 1 says it repeats every seven years (2018, 2025, 2032). But 1973 + 7k never equals 2025. (Next sighting per the stories: **2032**, so not in 2026.)
5. **Madam Oakwood's locket.** She lost it in her sixth year, 1968, and the photo shows her best friend Margaret Chen. But Margaret was a seventh year in 1973, so a second year in 1968.
6. **The Remembrall.**
   - Dec 2: Finn reflects on "the Remembrall his dad had given him".
   - Dec 8: his dad posts it to him for the first time, as a loan.
   - _Since Dec 8, it's Finn's to keep._
7. **The Tentacula, mentioned too early.** On Dec 10, Finn is reading about Tentacula after "yesterday's excitement". The Greenhouse Seven incident is Dec 15; yesterday was the Thestral lesson.
8. **Repeated gifts.**
   - Elliot's sketches: on Dec 18, Mira, Finn and Zara each keep their portrait from the sketchbook. On Dec 23, Elliot _gives_ Mira the same frost sketch and Finn the same greenhouse sketch.
   - Mira's book for Elliot: on Dec 13 she buys _The Alchemy of Identity_ at Tomes and Scrolls. On Dec 23 she gives _The Courage to Be_, "found in Diagon Alley last summer".
   - Mira's book for Finn: a medicinal plants guide on Dec 13, but "magical herbology across cultures" on Dec 23.
9. **The wandmaker.** On Dec 23, Zara commissions "a wandmaker" (she/her) for the wand holders, with no mention that Finn's dad is a wandmaker.

### Dates, weekdays and time

10. **Weekdays.**
    - Dec 14 is set on "Monday", but Dec 14, 2025 was a Sunday.
    - Dec 17 is set on "Tuesday", but it was a Wednesday.
    - Dec 22 calls the ball "Saturday's festivities… two nights prior", but the ball was Sunday Dec 21, one night before.
11. **A sunrise at 2am.** The ball ends around 2am, and Mira returns as "dawn was just a hint", then watches "the sun beginning to rise". In a Scottish December, sunrise is around 8:45am.
12. **Dec 6's timeline.** The club is announced on a "Friday", the first session is "next Thursday", and breakfast is the next "Saturday". The story covers about a week but is published on a single date.
13. **The Ravenclaw common room as a meeting place.** On Dec 18 and Dec 20, non-Ravenclaws gather there; possible via the riddle, but worth deciding.

### Characters and the world

14. **Two Priyas.** Priya the Gryffindor Chaser (Dec 3) and Priya Desai of Slytherin (Dec 16 on). Also two Marcuses: the Gryffindor Chaser, and Marcus Flint.
15. **Zara's family.**
    - The 2025 plan and Dec 7 say pure-blood. Dec 9 has her uncle visiting "my mother's _non-magical_ family".
    - Dec 12 says her sister told her about the Triwizard Yule Ball (1994), which would make that sister about 30 years older than Zara.
    - Dec 13 says Amara, 10, is her _younger_ sister. Dec 20 says she learned hair from her _older_ sister.
16. **Zara's badge.** On Dec 13 a third year stares at Zara's "prefect badge", but fourth years can't be prefects.
17. **Zara's cloth.** "Kente" is Ghanaian. For an Igbo family (Okonkwo, Chidi), akwete or George cloth would fit.
18. **Mira's subjects.** The 2025 plan says Ancient Runes _and_ Arithmancy.
    - Dec 16: Priya takes Arithmancy "instead" of Runes, implying Mira doesn't take it.
    - Dec 17 and Dec 22: Mira has an Arithmancy exam and uses Arithmancy skills.
19. **Mira's parents.** Dec 13 says "her dad's surgery"; Dec 19 says Dad is a chemist and Mum is a GP.
20. **Flitwick vs Thorne.**
    - The 2025 plan says Flitwick retired and Thorne is the new Charms professor, and stories call her "Flitwick's successor".
    - Yet Flitwick is described as the Charms expert with an office grading second-year essays (Dec 8), co-runs the Dueling Club (Dec 6), and attends the ball and the feast.
21. **Sinistra's N.E.W.T.** She says she collapsed during her N.E.W.T. "in sixth year"; N.E.W.T.s are taken in seventh year.
22. **Classroom floor.** The hideaway classroom is on the third floor (Dec 11, Dec 12, Dec 19, Dec 23) but the fourth floor on Dec 17.
23. **The pendant.** Elliot's pendant is described as "a silver circle with a horizontal line through it". This isn't a widely recognized non-binary symbol.
24. **Seeing Thestrals.** Zara says she first saw Thestrals "when I started at Hogwarts", but first years travel by boat, not carriage.

### Prose slips

25. Dec 6: Elliot speaks two consecutive lines.
26. Dec 24: a "she asked" with an unclear speaker in the round of "Even when I'm…" questions.
27. Dec 15: "singing orchids" appear in the ending but not in the story body.

## Recommendations for 2026

1. **Keep a continuity sheet and update it after every story.** It should record:
   - Character facts: family, subjects, houses, years, appearance.
   - Who knows what, and since when.
   - Dates of big events.
   - Staff roles.
   - Every named minor character (no duplicate first names).
2. **Plan dates and weekdays up front.** Use a real December 2026 calendar: Dec 1, 2026 is a **Tuesday**. Decide which story happens on which day, and when the term ends and students go home.
3. **Write each story's synopsis before the story, then check it against the continuity sheet**, especially for "first time" events like coming out, confessions and asking someone out.
4. **Enforce the word limit** (see `story-structure.md`) and vary the endings (see `writing-style.md`).
5. **Rotate point of view** deliberately: roughly six stories per main character, plus any new characters.
6. **Allow some friction and some open threads**: not every story needs a bow on top.
7. **Check canon ages.** Any canon character must plausibly be at Hogwarts, alive and the right age in 2026.
8. **Review pass before publishing.** Read each story against the continuity sheet and the previous stories, and fix the contradictions _before_ release.
